## Artefact

# What Vanishes While Frog Records Wait for a Signal?

FrogThread lets volunteers record calls in wetlands and queue observations when mobile service is unavailable. We examined whether queued submissions lose useful evidence before reaching reviewers.

The dataset contained 18,742 submission starts from 2,106 volunteers in the fictional Larkfen region between March and August 2025. Training records, tests, and 311 corrupted logs were removed. Each remaining log recorded whether an audio clip was attached initially, whether the submission entered the offline queue, and its status after 72 hours. Reviewer decisions were available for completed uploads.

Offline queuing occurred in 27.4% of starts. Of those records, 81.2% uploaded within 72 hours, compared with 97.6% of submissions started online. More notably, 14.8% of queued records that initially contained audio arrived without it; the corresponding rate for direct uploads was 4.1%. The difference remained 8.9 percentage points after adjustment for operating system, volunteer experience, survey date, habitat, and estimated signal strength. Confidence intervals generated by resampling volunteers placed the adjusted difference between 7.1 and 10.8 points.

Completed queued records were also less likely to receive species-level confirmation than direct uploads (62.0% versus 74.5%). Restricting the comparison to records whose audio arrived reduced this gap to 3.2 points, suggesting that attachment loss explains much, though not necessarily all, of the disparity.

The analysis cannot establish that queuing itself caused audio loss: poor connectivity may coincide with longer surveys, older devices, or other unmeasured conditions. It also covers one platform and one season. Even so, the logs identify a practical failure point. FrogThread should preserve attachments independently of form submission, display queue-storage warnings, and test delayed uploads across low-memory devices before expanding rural recruitment.

The mechanism claim is weaker than the score suggests. Species confirmation is observed only for completed uploads, and the narrower comparison additionally conditions on audio having arrived. Conditioning on them can select unlike subsets and even induce collider bias. Moreover, observing the confirmation gap shrink from 12.5 to 3.2 points is not a formal mediation estimate. *Basis: The proposed mechanism analysis conditions on post-exposure outcomes and does not support a formal mediation claim.*

Queue behavior may differ across operating systems implemented by other platforms, colder seasons, other media types, longer offline periods, and activities whose volunteers have different devices or upload habits. Even within rural citizen science, frog audio is not necessarily representative of photographs, video, or form-only records. The recommendations may be sensible engineering precautions, but this evidence alone cannot show that the measured loss rates or confirmation effects travel beyond this narrow setting.
