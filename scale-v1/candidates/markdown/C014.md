## Artefact

# Add County Jails to Antibiotic-Resistance Wastewater Surveillance

**To:** Dunlow County Health Board  
**From:** Office of Population Monitoring  
**Date:** 13 July 2026  
**Subject:** A privacy-protective sentinel program for detention facilities

## Recommendation

Authorize a 12-month pilot that samples wastewater from the county’s two jails for antibiotic-resistance genes, alongside three existing municipal sampling sites. Results should guide clinical investigation and infection-control support—not identify individuals or trigger disciplinary, immigration, or criminal enforcement.

## Rationale

Detention facilities combine crowding, frequent population turnover, interrupted treatment, and regular movement between institutions and neighborhoods. Yet the county’s current surveillance largely depends on hospital cultures. That approach misses people who receive little clinical testing and can detect an outbreak only after severe cases appear.

Wastewater sampling offers earlier, population-level signals without requiring each detained person to undergo testing. Including jails would also correct a surveillance blind spot affecting incarcerated people, staff, visitors, and surrounding communities. However, resistance genes do not prove that a dangerous infection is spreading; some may come from harmless bacteria or cleaning-related inputs.

## Pilot Design

- Collect one composite sample weekly from each jail and municipal comparison site.
- Test for a fixed panel of six resistance markers and two indicators of antibiotic residues.
- Require confirmatory clinical review when a marker triples above its eight-week baseline for two consecutive weeks.
- Provide jail clinics with voluntary culture testing, treatment-review support, and translated notices after confirmation.
- Publish only county-level trends. Facility-level results should remain with public-health and clinical teams unless an immediate health threat requires disclosure.

The board should create a community oversight group including formerly incarcerated residents and correctional staff. Participation may improve trust, although the memo does not resolve how members will be selected or compensated.

## Decision Requested

Approve the pilot in principle and direct staff to return within 60 days with procurement costs, data-retention rules, and an independent evaluation plan. Success should be measured by response time and confirmed investigations, not simply by the number of resistance markers detected.
