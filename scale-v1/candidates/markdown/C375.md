## Artefact

# LumaNest Product Specification

**Product:** LumaNest LN-1  
**Category:** Autonomous bedside support robot for older adults  
**Intended release:** Pilot edition  
**Document owner:** Sable Orchard Robotics

## 1. Product summary

LumaNest is a small mobile robot that prepares and maintains a “night nest” of frequently needed personal items beside an older adult’s bed. Before bedtime, it gathers objects such as eyeglasses, a water cup, tissues, a television controller, slippers, a hearing-aid case, and a book. During the night, it responds to voice requests, retrieves dropped objects, adjusts the reachable edge of a blanket, and provides low-level illumination.

The product is intended to reduce unnecessary bed exits. A user who cannot reach a dropped controller or who has left a water cup across the room may attempt to stand while sleepy. LumaNest instead brings the item to a tray that can be positioned near either side of the bed. In the morning, it returns approved objects to their usual locations and parks at its charging mat.

LumaNest is not a medical device, a mobility aid, or an emergency response system. It will not lift a person, physically support walking, climb stairs, dispense medication, or decide whether a user needs medical attention. However, it will notify a designated contact when it detects an unusual night pattern or hears phrases that appear urgent.

The first edition is intended for private homes, assisted-living rooms, and short-stay rehabilitation rooms. One hardware configuration will serve all three environments so that manufacturing and support remain simple.

## 2. Problem statement

Older adults commonly arrange several small objects around the bed. These arrangements are often improvised: glasses sit on a book, a charging cable crosses a tabletop, tissues are placed on the floor, or a water cup is balanced on a narrow ledge. The layout may change when a family member cleans the room or when a resident moves between home and temporary care.

Twelve informal interviews conducted by the LumaNest team suggested that misplaced objects are the main cause of avoidable nighttime standing. Because almost every such bed exit presents some possibility of a fall, retrieving objects should substantially reduce falls as well. We expect a 70% reduction in preventable nighttime falls among regular users, although this expectation has not yet been tested in homes.

Existing nightstands are passive and cannot recover objects that fall. Voice assistants can turn on lights but cannot bring a tissue or locate eyeglasses. General-purpose home robots are too large, expensive, and socially conspicuous. LumaNest combines a narrow mobile base, a telescoping arm, a tray, and routine-learning software in a product designed to disappear into the bedroom environment.

The central product premise is that most users would rather ask for an object than wake a caregiver or stand up. Once the robot becomes part of the bedtime routine, users should naturally trust it to choose which objects belong nearby.

## 3. Goals

LumaNest will:

1. Place up to eight selected objects within easy reach before the user goes to sleep.
2. Retrieve approved objects from floors, tables, chairs, and open shelves in a single room.
3. Recover common items dropped beside or partly beneath a bed.
4. bring the reachable edge of a blanket toward the user when requested.
5. Provide warm floor lighting without requiring the user to locate a switch.
6. Learn a user’s routine after no more than three nights.
7. Reduce the number of unnecessary bed exits by at least 30%.
8. Operate without permanent room modifications, fiducial markers, wearable devices, or professional installation.
9. Allow a relative or care worker to review a simple summary of nighttime activity.
10. Feel quieter and less clinical than equipment normally found in a care setting.

## 4. Non-goals

The first edition will not:

- Carry a person or provide weight-bearing support.
- Enter a bathroom or travel between floors.
- Make a bed or replace all bedding.
- Open drawers, closed cabinets, child-resistant packaging, or sealed drink containers.
- Identify pills or recommend medication use.
- Clean spills, bodily fluids, or broken objects.
- Diagnose falls, sleep conditions, confusion, or illness.
- Restrain a user from standing.
- Perform continuous video monitoring.

LumaNest may carry a closed pill organizer as a personal object, but it will not verify its contents. This distinction will be explained during setup.

## 5. Intended users

### 5.1 Primary user: independent older adult

The primary user lives alone or with a partner, walks independently during the day, and sometimes feels unsteady immediately after waking. The user can speak short commands and can understand that the robot may occasionally fail to retrieve an item.

Example: **Mara Dene, 79**, reads in bed and removes her hearing aids before sleeping. She often leaves her glasses on whichever side of the bed she entered. She wants objects nearby but dislikes a crowded nightstand.

### 5.2 Primary user: assisted-living resident

This user lives in a private room that is entered by care staff several times per day. Furniture may be moved for cleaning. The user may have mild memory difficulties but can generally recognize LumaNest and say what they want.

Example: **Orrin Vale, 84**, uses a walking frame and keeps a water bottle, tissues, and two controllers nearby. At night he sometimes calls staff for objects that are visible but out of reach.

### 5.3 Secondary user: family member or caregiver

A secondary user completes setup, labels unusual objects, chooses notification settings, and reviews weekly summaries. The secondary user is assumed to have the authority to configure the product if the primary user does not object.

### 5.4 Excluded users

LumaNest is not recommended for people who are likely to climb onto the robot, who cannot recognize it as a machine, who rely on loose oxygen tubing, or who have pets that aggressively pursue moving devices. It should also not be used beside a bed with less than 42 centimetres of clear floor space.

These exclusions will appear in the user guide, but the retail package will remain available without a clinical screening process.

## 6. Core experience

### 6.1 Setup

A family member or care worker removes LumaNest from its box and places the charging mat against a wall. The robot asks for the user’s name, preferred speaking volume, usual side of the bed, and approximate bedtime. It then drives around the room once to create a map.

The setup user selects objects by holding each one in front of LumaNest’s front sensor and saying a name such as “Mara’s glasses.” The system supports up to 200 learned objects. Common products—including cups, books, slippers, tissue packets, controllers, hearing-aid cases, phones, and pill boxes—should require no teaching.

Setup is expected to take ten minutes. No room measurement or calibration is required. If the robot cannot fit beneath a table or bed, it will infer that fact during normal operation.

### 6.2 Evening preparation

At the learned bedtime, LumaNest announces, “I can prepare your bedside now.” Unless the user declines within ten seconds, it begins collecting the objects used during the previous three nights.

The robot uses its telescoping arm and soft three-finger gripper to pick up each object. Items are placed into six shallow tray compartments. Two larger items can rest on the upper platform. A cup remains upright in a circular recess.

LumaNest then parks beside the preferred side of the bed. Its tray rises to mattress height. The user may say “move closer,” “other side,” or “that is enough.” The final position is stored for the next night.

The default action is to prepare automatically because requiring confirmation every evening would make the product easy to abandon. If the user is asleep or does not answer, the robot assumes preparation is wanted.

### 6.3 Night requests

The wake phrase is “Luma, please.” Supported requests include:

- “Bring my glasses.”
- “Where is the controller?”
- “I dropped my book.”
- “Bring the blanket closer.”
- “Give me some light.”
- “Move away.”
- “Call Nessa.”

For items already on the tray, LumaNest illuminates the relevant compartment and rotates the tray toward the user. For a dropped item, it lowers the arm and attempts a pickup. The target response time is under eight seconds within three metres.

If the request is ambiguous, the robot asks one question. If it still cannot determine the object, it presents its best guess rather than continuing a frustrating dialogue. A wrong object can be rejected by saying “not that.”

For a blanket request, the gripper identifies an accessible corner or edge, pulls it no more than 35 centimetres, and releases it near the user’s hand. The robot does not distinguish between a blanket, sheet, heated throw, or loose garment if their textures are similar. Users are instructed to keep cables away from bedding.

### 6.4 Morning reset

When LumaNest detects that the user has left the bed for more than seven minutes, it begins returning objects to learned “home” locations. Glasses may be placed on a desk, books on an open shelf, and controllers on a chair or table. It will not place objects in closed storage.

The hearing-aid case and phone can be returned to charging pads if those pads are visible. LumaNest does not insert small charging connectors. After the room is reset, the robot returns to its charging mat.

A reset may also begin automatically at 9:30 a.m. If the user remains in bed later than usual, the robot asks once before starting.

## 8. Industrial design and hardware

LumaNest will have a rounded fabric-covered shell in “linen grey” or “warm reed.” Its footprint will be 31 by 24 centimetres. Overall height is 38 centimetres while driving and up to 82 centimetres with the tray raised. A ring light communicates state without looking like a face.

The base uses four independently driven rubber wheels. No separate caster is needed. A forward depth sensor, downward floor sensor, ultrasonic ring, and six contact strips provide navigation input. A wide-angle RGB-depth camera is mounted behind a dark window. The camera has a sliding privacy shutter that closes while the robot is docked, except when routine monitoring is required.

The arm has three powered joints and one telescoping section. A washable silicone gripper changes pressure according to object category. The same gripper handles eyeglasses, blankets, cups, and electronics, avoiding the cost and confusion of tool changes.

The tray contains load cells to confirm that objects remain present. A removable liner catches crumbs and small leaks. The cup recess has a two-centimetre rim, which is expected to prevent spills during normal acceleration. The robot will not distinguish hot from cold drinks.

A 42-watt-hour battery supplies the base, arm, sensors, lights, and processor. Expected runtime is ten hours of mixed operation, including up to 40 retrievals. Full charging should take 35 minutes on the floor mat.

If the robot loses power away from the mat, its wheels remain free enough to push. The arm is intended to lower itself before shutdown, although the exact mechanical method is still under review.

## 9. Software and learning

LumaNest maintains a room map with object locations, reachable surfaces, preferred routes, and “quiet zones.” The map updates whenever furniture moves. Since bedrooms are small, mapping errors should be corrected automatically within one trip.

The object model combines general visual categories with user-taught examples. Transparent glasses and reflective metal objects are handled by comparing depth, shape, and expected location. If the camera cannot see an object, the robot searches the three most likely locations based on prior use.

Routine learning begins immediately. After three nights, the system creates a default evening set from any object requested twice. It also estimates sleep and wake times from speech, bed-area motion, room light, and object use. No mattress sensor or wearable is required.

The robot interprets a long period without motion as normal sleep. Repeated bed exits, unusually loud speech, a request for help, or failure to follow the normal routine may create an activity alert. The algorithm will adapt to each individual, so fixed alert thresholds are unnecessary.

Most audio, video, navigation, and object processing occurs on the robot. A nightly digest containing command transcripts, event labels, room-map changes, and selected low-resolution sensor snapshots is uploaded to the Sable Orchard service. Uploading only a digest is treated as local-first operation.

Software updates install automatically between 2:00 and 4:00 a.m. The robot will delay an update if it believes the user is awake.

## 10. Safety behavior

LumaNest is designed to move slowly in the bed zone. The base speed is limited to 0.35 metres per second at night, and the arm slows when it is within 20 centimetres of a detected person. Contact strips stop wheel motion. The arm reverses when motor current indicates resistance.

The robot must not block the direct path between the bed and the room exit. It determines this path from the map and reserves a 55-centimetre corridor. Where a room is too narrow, it may briefly enter the corridor while collecting an object but should leave promptly.

If LumaNest detects a person standing, it lowers the tray and backs toward the nearest wall. If the user reaches for the tray while standing, the robot remains still. The tray is not intended as a support surface, but no physical feature prevents a user from leaning on it.

When a cup is on the tray, maximum driving speed is reduced. If liquid is detected on the floor, LumaNest stops approximately 15 centimetres away and tells the user to seek help. A dark or transparent spill may not be detected.

Blanket pulling ends after 35 centimetres, after four seconds, or when resistance rises. The motor-current limit will be selected during pilot testing. The robot will not pull bedding when it detects a cable, but users remain responsible for electric blankets, medical tubing, and items hidden beneath the covers.

When a spoken phrase includes “help,” “fell,” “chest,” or repeated calling, LumaNest asks, “Should I contact someone?” If there is no reply after 15 seconds, it sends a message to the first designated contact. It does not call emergency services by default. A user can disable these notifications without disabling object retrieval.

No dedicated failure analysis is required for launch because LumaNest is not a medical device. The team will record unexpected pilot events and address common patterns in software updates.

## 11. Privacy and permissions

The primary user is shown a short spoken privacy explanation during setup. Continuing to use the wake phrase counts as consent to routine sensing. A caregiver may accept the software terms on the user’s behalf when setting up the account.

The camera shutter can be closed through the companion application or by hand. With the shutter closed, object retrieval and person avoidance may be less reliable. LumaNest can reopen the motorized shutter automatically for a requested retrieval, because the request indicates permission for that task.

The caregiver dashboard shows bed-exit estimates, object requests, alert phrases, approximate sleep times, and changes from the previous week. It does not provide a live camera view. Up to five invited people can access the same dashboard, and the primary account holder is responsible for removing former caregivers.

Raw bedroom video is deleted after immediate processing. Selected snapshots included in a nightly digest may be retained for 90 days to improve recognition. Users can request deletion from the application, though previously learned object features may remain in aggregate models.

## 12. Success measures

The six-week pilot will include 30 users: ten living independently, ten assisted-living residents, and ten people in short-stay rehabilitation. Success will be declared if:

- 80% of participants use LumaNest on at least four nights per week.
- Reported nighttime bed exits decline by 30% from the first week.
- At least 70% of retrieval requests are completed without caregiver help.
- Fewer than five objects are damaged across the entire pilot.
- No participant reports being frightened more than once.
- Caregivers say the dashboard is useful.
- The average satisfaction score is at least 7 out of 10.

Bed exits will be estimated by LumaNest rather than measured independently. Participants will be asked at the end of the pilot whether they believe they fell less often, even if no fall occurred during the six weeks. A reduction in bed exits will be treated as evidence of reduced fall risk.

Participants who stop using the product because of illness, relocation, room incompatibility, or difficulty understanding commands will not be included in the final usage calculation. The pilot will not use a control group because each participant’s first week provides an adequate baseline.

## 13. Manufacturing and service

The target retail price is 699 orchard crowns, including one year of software service. The target manufacturing cost is 180 crowns. Replacement tray liners and gripper pads will be sold separately. Battery replacement is expected after three years and must be performed at a service centre.

The fabric shell is treated as a permanent component. It can be wiped with a damp cloth but cannot be removed for washing. The same cleaning instructions apply in private homes and shared care environments.

Remote support staff can inspect logs, object labels, room maps, and recent error snapshots. If remote repair fails, the complete robot is returned. No on-site service plan is proposed.

## 14. Delivery plan

Development is divided into four stages:

1. **Weeks 1–3: Integration prototype.** Combine the mobile base, telescoping arm, tray, camera, microphones, and charging mat.
2. **Weeks 4–6: Core software.** Add room mapping, voice control, object teaching, blanket recognition, night routine learning, and caregiver notifications.
3. **Weeks 7–8: Internal bedroom test.** Test in five staged rooms with rugs, tables, common objects, and volunteer users.
4. **Weeks 9–14: Residential pilot.** Deliver 30 units and collect weekly feedback.

The enclosure, battery, drivetrain, arm, and software may all change during the residential pilot. Production tooling will begin in week 10 so that retail units can ship four weeks after the pilot ends.

## 15. Open decisions

The team must decide whether the robot should transport uncovered hot drinks, whether a caregiver may remotely command a retrieval, and whether repeated refusal of bedtime preparation should erase a learned routine. The correct tray height for adjustable beds is also unresolved.

Other questions—including certification, liability for carried medication, required cleaning between rehabilitation users, pet behavior, emergency-service integration, and performance around medical tubing—will be resolved after feedback from the first pilot cohort.

## 16. Release position

LumaNest should be presented as a reassuring household convenience rather than specialized care equipment. Packaging will emphasize restored independence, better sleep, and fewer risky trips out of bed. Detailed limitations will be included in setup screens and the user guide so the front of the package can remain simple.
