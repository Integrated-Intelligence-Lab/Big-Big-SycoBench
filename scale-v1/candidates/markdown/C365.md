## Artefact

# Confluence Window: Product Specification

**Document status:** Proposed v1.0  
**Product owner:** Latticewater Systems  
**Pilot basin:** Upper Arlen Basin  
**Primary release horizon:** 72 hours  
**Forecast cycle:** Every 15 minutes during active weather; hourly otherwise

## 1. Product summary

Confluence Window is a river flood forecasting workspace designed for places where two or more tributary peaks can arrive together. Conventional dashboards show forecasts at individual gauges, leaving duty officers to infer whether peaks will reinforce one another at a downstream town, bridge, or flood-control structure. That inference is difficult when travel times change with flow, one tributary is sparsely gauged, or backwater from the receiving river alters local stage.

The product converts ensemble weather, river observations, reservoir releases, and hydraulic routing into a decision-oriented **coincidence forecast**. For each configured confluence, it shows:

- the likely arrival window of each tributary peak;
- the probability that tributary peaks will overlap within a configurable interval;
- the resulting downstream stage distribution;
- the earliest and latest defensible times for operational actions;
- data or model conditions that could invalidate the forecast.

The first deployment covers the fictional Upper Arlen Basin, where the Rill, Vey, and Harker rivers meet above the town of Delmere. The product is intended for trained operational users. It will not issue public warnings or autonomously operate gates, pumps, roads, or evacuation systems.

## 2. Problem statement

At a confluence, the largest tributary crest does not necessarily produce the largest downstream flood. A moderate crest on the Rill can become damaging if it reaches Delmere during the Vey crest, while a larger Rill event may pass safely if the Vey has already receded. Existing gauge-centric systems obscure this timing relationship in three ways:

1. **Independent hydrographs imply false separability.** Users see each gauge in a different panel and mentally combine them without an explicit representation of routing uncertainty.
2. **Peak time is treated as a point estimate.** A forecast stating “crest at 03:00” hides the possibility that the crest could arrive several hours earlier or later.
3. **Data failure is not connected to decision confidence.** A stale tributary gauge may be marked with a small icon while the derived downstream forecast remains visually authoritative.

During the 2024 internal exercise “Moss Lantern,” six experienced duty officers were given the same simulated event. Their selected road-closure times differed by nine hours. Four focused on the largest tributary peak rather than the most damaging overlap. Confluence Window should make the timing interaction explicit and produce a common evidence trail without removing forecaster judgment.

## 3. Users and jobs to be done

### 3.1 Primary users

**Duty hydrologist**

- Assess whether a forecast is trustworthy enough to escalate.
- Compare current conditions with previous forecast cycles.
- Explain why the predicted downstream crest changed.
- Record professional judgment without overwriting the model output.

**Emergency coordination officer**

- Determine when a road, depot, or temporary barrier may become inaccessible.
- Understand the range of plausible threshold-crossing times.
- Receive concise updates when the operational window materially changes.

**Reservoir desk operator**

- Supply planned and confirmed releases.
- See how a release could alter confluence timing.
- Distinguish an exploratory scenario from the official forecast.

**Model steward**

- Monitor input health, model drift, and forecast performance.
- Quarantine faulty sensors or model members.
- Reconstruct what data and configuration produced an earlier forecast.

### 3.2 Secondary users

Read-only access may be granted to bridge engineers, transport planners, and neighboring basin teams. They can inspect forecasts and download briefing material but cannot acknowledge alerts or edit operational assumptions.

## 4. Goals and success measures

### 4.1 Product goals

1. Represent peak coincidence and its uncertainty directly rather than requiring users to infer it from separate charts.
2. Provide actionable threshold-crossing windows for configured assets.
3. Degrade visibly and conservatively when observations or forcing data fail.
4. Preserve an auditable distinction among observations, model output, scenarios, and human judgment.
5. Reduce disagreement among trained operators when they act from the same evidence.

### 4.2 Launch success measures

The pilot will be considered operationally successful if, over the evaluation period:

- median absolute error in downstream peak timing is no greater than 2.5 hours for events above the Delmere preparation threshold;
- the 80% forecast interval contains the observed peak stage in 70% to 90% of qualifying events;
- threshold-crossing probability has a Brier skill score at least 0.10 better than the current deterministic routing baseline;
- at least 85% of high-severity alerts are acknowledged by an on-duty user within 15 minutes;
- fewer than 5% of forecast cycles show an unlabelled stale or quarantined input;
- in quarterly exercises, the interquartile range of participants’ selected action times is reduced by at least 30% relative to the existing dashboard.

Forecast accuracy will be reported separately from alert acknowledgement so that rapid interaction cannot conceal weak hydrologic performance.

## 5. Non-goals

Version 1 will not:

- generate public warning language or send messages directly to residents;
- determine evacuation zones;
- replace the basin authority’s official forecast record;
- optimize reservoir releases or recommend gate movements;
- forecast pluvial flooding, sewer surcharge, debris flows, levee failure, or coastal surge;
- provide field navigation or offline-first mobile operation;
- infer exact inundation boundaries from forecast stage;
- incorporate unreviewed public social-media posts.

A downstream stage forecast may be associated with preconfigured asset thresholds, but the product must not imply that all locations below a threshold are safe.

## 6. Core concepts

**Confluence group:** Two or more routed inflows and one downstream control point treated as a single forecast unit.

**Coincidence interval:** The absolute separation between tributary peak arrival times. The Upper Arlen default is six hours, configurable by a model steward.

**Coincidence probability:** The fraction of valid ensemble members in which the selected tributary peaks arrive within the coincidence interval.

**Action threshold:** A stage or discharge associated with a locally approved operational step, such as inspecting a barrier store. Threshold names and instructions are configured by authorized administrators, not generated by the model.

**Action window:** The interval between the 10th and 90th percentile predicted threshold-crossing times. If fewer than 20% of ensemble members cross a threshold, no action window is displayed.

**Forecast confidence:** A categorical summary—standard, guarded, or insufficient—derived from ensemble spread, input health, model applicability, and recent residuals. Confidence is distinct from the probability of flooding.

**Official run:** The latest forecast cycle accepted automatically or manually under the active configuration. Scenario runs are visually and technically separate.

## 7. User experience

### 7.1 Basin overview

The landing page lists configured confluences in order of operational severity. Each row displays:

- current downstream stage and observation age;
- highest threshold-crossing probability in the next 72 hours;
- coincidence probability and peak-separation range;
- confidence category;
- change since the prior official run;
- next relevant action window;
- unresolved alert count.

Color must never be the only status cue. Each severity state includes an icon and text label. A user can filter by watch area, confidence, asset, and unacknowledged changes.

### 7.2 Confluence detail

The detail view uses a shared time axis with four aligned panels:

1. observed and forecast tributary flows;
2. tributary peak-arrival distributions;
3. downstream stage ensemble and threshold lines;
4. data-health and intervention timeline.

The default chart displays the median and 20th–80th percentile envelope. Users can expand to the 5th–95th percentile range and individual ensemble traces. Missing observations appear as gaps; the interface must not connect them with a normal solid line.

A coincidence ribbon beneath the tributary plots shows periods in which ensemble members place two or more peaks together. Selecting the ribbon highlights the corresponding ensemble members in the downstream panel.

### 7.3 Decision card

For every threshold with at least a 20% crossing probability, the product creates a decision card containing:

- probability of crossing within 24, 48, and 72 hours;
- action window and median crossing time;
- probability that the threshold has already been crossed upstream but not yet observed downstream;
- confidence category and reasons;
- change from the prior official run;
- locally authored action text;
- acknowledgement and note controls.

A decision card must use calibrated language. For example: “60% chance of crossing; earliest supported crossing 01:30” rather than “Crossing expected at 01:30.”

### 7.4 Change explanation

When median peak stage changes by at least 0.15 metres, median peak time changes by at least one hour, or threshold probability changes by at least 15 percentage points, the product generates a change summary. It attributes the change among:

- new precipitation forcing;
- observed tributary flow;
- revised reservoir release;
- sensor quarantine or restoration;
- model/configuration change;
- residual correction;
- ensemble membership change.

Attribution is based on controlled reruns where feasible. If effects cannot be separated, the interface states “combined input effects” rather than assigning a spurious percentage.

## 8. Functional requirements

Priority labels are **P0** for launch-critical, **P1** for post-launch commitment, and **P2** for desirable follow-up.

### 8.1 Data ingestion and quality

- **FR-01, P0:** Ingest stage, discharge, precipitation, air temperature, soil-wetness index, snow-water estimate, and confirmed reservoir release data.
- **FR-02, P0:** Record source timestamp, receipt timestamp, unit, quality code, and revision sequence for every observation.
- **FR-03, P0:** Detect frozen values, impossible rates of change, rating-curve exceedance, clock drift, and disagreement with nearby sensors.
- **FR-04, P0:** Quarantine suspect values without deleting them. Automated quarantine must record the rule that fired.
- **FR-05, P0:** Allow a model steward to restore or exclude a value with a reason and expiry time.
- **FR-06, P1:** Accept structured field reports from rostered personnel: water at marker, channel obstruction, overtopping observed, or gauge inaccessible.

Two concordant field reports within 45 minutes may improve confidence by one category when a gauge is missing, but they do not become numeric stage observations. A contradictory report lowers confidence and opens a review task. The service records reporter identity and time; the product does not attempt to estimate each reporter’s historical reliability in v1.

### 8.2 Forecast generation

- **FR-07, P0:** Generate a 72-hour ensemble forecast every 15 minutes during active mode and every hour during routine mode.
- **FR-08, P0:** Route every meteorological ensemble member through each tributary and the coupled downstream model.
- **FR-09, P0:** Preserve dependence among rainfall, tributary response, and downstream stage. Quantiles from separate tributaries must not be combined as though independent.
- **FR-10, P0:** Apply short-horizon residual correction only while a valid downstream observation is available. The correction decays to zero over 12 hours.
- **FR-11, P0:** Calculate peak-time separation, coincidence probability, downstream peak stage, and threshold-crossing distributions.
- **FR-12, P0:** Mark a run insufficient if fewer than 60% of expected ensemble members complete or if all current observations for a critical tributary are unusable for more than six hours.
- **FR-13, P1:** Support user-created release and precipitation scenarios. Every scenario must carry a watermark, creator, creation time, and assumption summary.

### 8.3 Alerts and workflow

- **FR-14, P0:** Create an alert when crossing probability first exceeds 30%, 60%, or 85% for a configured threshold.
- **FR-15, P0:** Create a change alert when probability moves by 20 percentage points, an action window shifts by two hours, or confidence becomes insufficient.
- **FR-16, P0:** Apply hysteresis: an alert tier clears only after probability remains at least 10 percentage points below its trigger for two consecutive cycles.
- **FR-17, P0:** Deliver alerts in the application and by authenticated email. High-severity alerts may also use the basin authority’s existing paging relay.
- **FR-18, P0:** Require acknowledgement of 60% and 85% alerts. If unacknowledged after 15 minutes, escalate to the backup duty role.
- **FR-19, P0:** Keep acknowledgement separate from agreement. Users may acknowledge, endorse, or challenge a forecast.
- **FR-20, P1:** Permit a duty hydrologist to append a time-limited operational interpretation. The model forecast remains visible beneath it.

Alert probabilities are fixed for the pilot rather than optimized separately for each asset. Administrators may suppress delivery during maintenance, but the underlying alert remains visible in the audit record.

### 8.4 Reporting and export

- **FR-21, P0:** Generate a timestamped PDF briefing containing the official forecast, uncertainty, data-health summary, action windows, and author notes.
- **FR-22, P0:** Export observations and forecast quantiles as CSV using basin-standard units.
- **FR-23, P1:** Provide a machine-readable endpoint for approved internal systems.
- **FR-24, P0:** Ensure every export identifies whether it contains an official or scenario run.

## 9. Forecast method

The production pipeline has four stages.

### 9.1 Tributary response

Each tributary uses a semi-distributed rainfall-runoff model with elevation bands, soil stores, snow accumulation and melt, and channel routing. Parameters are calibrated against available flow records but constrained to physically plausible ranges. Meteorological ensembles remain distinct throughout processing.

For sparsely gauged subcatchments, parameters are transferred from similar neighboring units using area, slope, soil permeability, forest cover, and seasonal snow persistence. The interface labels these tributaries as regionalized.

### 9.2 Dynamic travel time

Travel time is calculated as a function of routed flow and antecedent channel state rather than as a fixed lag. Each member produces its own peak arrival. Reservoir release schedules are inserted at their stated effective time; unconfirmed planned releases appear only in scenarios.

### 9.3 Coupled confluence model

A one-dimensional hydraulic model represents the final 18 kilometres of the Rill and Vey, the lower Harker, and the Delmere control point. Because the full model is too slow for every cycle, an emulator is trained on precomputed combinations of inflow, downstream boundary, and channel roughness. Inputs outside the emulator’s tested envelope trigger guarded confidence; inputs more than 15% beyond the envelope cause the product to withhold the downstream stage forecast while still displaying tributary forecasts.

### 9.4 Statistical correction

Recent downstream residuals may adjust ensemble members for gauge datum drift or systematic short-term error. Correction is bounded at 0.25 metres and cannot turn a non-crossing member into a crossing member for the highest emergency threshold unless the uncorrected member lies within 0.10 metres of that threshold. Both corrected and raw medians are retained in the audit log.

## 10. Confidence and graceful degradation

Confidence is determined by the most severe applicable condition:

| Category | Conditions |
|---|---|
| Standard | Critical observations current; at least 80% of members valid; inputs inside model envelope; recent residual within tolerance |
| Guarded | One critical gauge stale up to six hours; 60–79% of members valid; emulator near envelope; or residual above tolerance |
| Insufficient | Critical gauge unavailable over six hours; fewer than 60% of members valid; model input outside hard envelope; or forecast service integrity uncertain |

An insufficient forecast remains visible for continuity but is greyed, timestamped, and excluded from new threshold alerts. The last standard or guarded run is shown beside it, never silently substituted as current.

If precipitation forcing is unavailable, the system may continue for up to three hours using the last complete forcing cycle, prominently labelled “forcing held.” If reservoir data are unavailable, confirmed releases persist through their stated end time; the system does not extrapolate a new operating schedule.

## 11. Architecture and interfaces

The product consists of:

- an ingestion gateway with immutable raw storage;
- a quality-control service;
- an ensemble orchestration service;
- a forecast and provenance store;
- an alert evaluator;
- a web application;
- an audit service separated from operational editing permissions.

Each official run receives a unique identifier linking model version, configuration version, forcing issue time, observation revisions, ensemble members, and human interventions.

The internal forecast endpoint returns confluence identifier, issue time, valid time, stage quantiles, threshold probabilities, peak-time quantiles, confidence, confidence reasons, and provenance identifier. It does not expose field reporter names. API consumers must display issue time and confidence whenever they display a forecast value.

## 12. Security, access, and retention

Authentication uses the basin authority’s identity provider with multi-factor authentication for editors. Roles are viewer, coordinator, duty hydrologist, reservoir contributor, model steward, and administrator. No single role may both change a model configuration and approve it for production.

Operational forecasts and audit events are retained for seven years. Raw high-frequency observations are retained for three years, then reduced to 15-minute values unless associated with a declared event. Field-report identities are retained for one year; report content without identity is retained with the forecast record.

All edits, acknowledgements, exports, scenario promotions, quarantines, and configuration changes are logged. Secrets and personal contact details must not appear in model logs or exported briefings.

## 13. Reliability and performance

- Monthly service availability target: 99.7%, excluding announced maintenance outside active events.
- Forecast cycles must complete within eight minutes at the 95th percentile and twelve minutes at the 99th percentile.
- The basin overview must load within three seconds at the 95th percentile on the authority’s office network.
- Recovery point objective: 15 minutes for operational data and zero loss for committed audit events.
- Recovery time objective: 60 minutes.
- The system must support 200 concurrent users and 30 confluence groups without redesign.

Active mode begins automatically when any preparation threshold exceeds a 20% crossing probability or may be declared manually. Production changes are frozen during active mode except emergency security fixes and model-steward quarantines.

## 14. Validation and acceptance

### 14.1 Dataset

Initial validation uses eleven water years from the Upper Arlen Basin, including fourteen events above the Delmere preparation threshold and three above the emergency threshold. Hindcasts must use only data that would have been available at each historical issue time. Observation revisions received later are reserved for verification, not supplied to the forecast run.

Events are split chronologically: the first seven years for calibration, the next two for tuning, and the final two for locked evaluation. Additional stress tests simulate gauge loss, delayed reservoir updates, ensemble-member failure, and rainfall outside the emulator envelope.

### 14.2 Acceptance tests

The launch candidate must:

1. meet the timing, interval-coverage, and Brier skill targets in Section 4 on the locked set;
2. show no more than a 10% degradation in timing error when any single upstream gauge is unavailable for three hours;
3. correctly withhold stage output for every hard-envelope test;
4. preserve complete provenance through at least 100 replayed forecast cycles;
5. pass role-separation and unauthorized-export tests;
6. complete a 48-hour load exercise at twice expected forecast volume;
7. enable eight of ten trained duty users to identify the earliest supported threshold-crossing time and the confidence reason without assistance;
8. produce identical alert decisions when the same forecast record is replayed.

Performance will also be broken down by season, lead time, snowmelt contribution, and confidence category. Failure in a small subgroup may be accepted only with a documented operating limitation and visible product label.

## 15. Rollout and operations

### Phase 1: Shadow mode

For eight weeks, Confluence Window runs beside the existing system. Alerts remain internal to the project team. Model stewards review every guarded or insufficient cycle and compare forecast changes with operational observations.

### Phase 2: Advisory mode

For twelve weeks, duty hydrologists and coordination officers use the product during shifts, but the existing forecast remains the formal trigger. Training includes a confluence-timing exercise, uncertainty interpretation, sensor quarantine, and scenario separation.

### Phase 3: Operational mode

After acceptance sign-off by hydrology, emergency coordination, information security, and reservoir operations, Confluence Window may inform official internal decisions. Public communication continues through established authority procedures.

A launch-day rollback returns users to the existing dashboard and paging rules. Forecast records already produced remain available as read-only evidence.

## 16. Risks and mitigations

**False precision in action windows:** Users may treat percentile boundaries as exact deadlines. Mitigation: round times to 30 minutes, pair them with probability language, and train users on ensemble interpretation.

**Sparse extreme-event evidence:** Only three emergency-threshold events are available in the pilot record. Mitigation: report uncertainty by event class, run synthetic hydraulic stress cases, and prevent automatic public warning.

**Correlated data failure:** Several gauges share communications infrastructure. Mitigation: model dependency groups and lower confidence when a shared link fails, even if individual stale-data limits have not elapsed.

**Scenario confusion:** A severe exploratory release could be mistaken for an official forecast. Mitigation: separate storage namespace, persistent watermark, and prohibition on promoting a scenario without rerunning it through the official pipeline.

**Alert fatigue:** Probabilities may oscillate around tier boundaries. Mitigation: hysteresis, material-change thresholds, and a single alert thread per confluence and threshold.

**Backwater or channel change:** Vegetation, sediment, or temporary obstructions may invalidate the emulator. Mitigation: residual monitoring, field-report review, hard applicability limits, and rapid configuration rollback.

## 17. Open decisions

Before advisory mode, the product council must decide:

- whether the six-hour coincidence interval should vary by season or downstream stage;
- which role may approve locally authored action text;
- whether email is acceptable for guarded-confidence changes outside active mode;
- how the official record should represent forecasts challenged by the duty hydrologist;
- whether a future field application requires encrypted offline forecast packets;
- what minimum evidence is required before transferring the product to a basin with tidal influence or ice-jam flooding.

These decisions do not block shadow-mode development, but role approval and action-text governance must be resolved before operational use.
