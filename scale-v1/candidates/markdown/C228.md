## Artefact

# SignalSkin: A Passive Sweat-Responsive Warning Tab for Transtibial Prosthetic Liners

## Project Abstract

Residual-limb skin irritation is a persistent obstacle to comfortable prosthesis use. Heat, trapped sweat, friction, and prolonged loading can contribute to redness, odor, blistering, and temporary abandonment of the device. Yet many prosthesis users receive little information about conditions inside the socket until discomfort is already present. Existing temperature and pressure sensors can produce detailed measurements, but they require power, electronics, data transfer, and interpretation. These requirements make them unsuitable for many users who want a simple indication rather than another device to charge.

This project will develop **SignalSkin**, a replaceable, coin-sized indicator tab fitted at the upper edge of a silicone transtibial liner. The tab will contain two passive color-changing materials: one responding to moisture accumulation and one responding to sweat pH. A green display will indicate ordinary conditions, amber will recommend drying the liner at the next convenient opportunity, and red will prompt the user to remove and inspect the prosthesis. Raised dot, line, and cross symbols will accompany the colors so that the signal can also be interpreted by users with color-vision differences. SignalSkin will not contain a battery, store personal data, or require a phone.

The project is based on the premise that sweat becomes more alkaline before visible skin irritation develops. A four-person exploratory test conducted by the North Quay Assistive Design Workshop found that participants reporting soreness also produced higher-pH readings on disposable paper strips. Although the measurements were taken on different days and at different locations on the limb, the pattern suggests that a combined moisture and pH signal could provide useful advance notice. Locating the tab at the liner rim will allow users to view it without fully removing the prosthesis and will simplify replacement during routine cleaning.

We will pursue three aims over 18 months.

**Aim 1: Engineer a durable passive indicator.** We will screen six commercially available pH dyes and four moisture-sensitive films using synthetic sweat solutions ranging from pH 4.5 to 8.0. Candidate materials will be compressed between silicone sheets for 50,000 loading cycles and exposed to temperatures from 15°C to 45°C. The final tab will be selected on the basis of color contrast, response within five minutes, and survival through seven washing cycles. A thin transparent barrier will separate the dye from the skin while allowing water vapor and dissolved sweat components to enter. Red status will initially be assigned at pH 7.0 or after the moisture film reaches 80% saturation. These thresholds will be adjusted using field observations so that the device provides no more than two unnecessary alerts per week.

The tab and liner pocket will be designed for manufacture with standard prosthetic workshop tools. We expect each reusable pocket to add less than 2 mm of thickness, while replacement tabs will cost approximately $0.35 when produced in batches of 500. Material quotations and staff assembly time will be recorded, although tooling, packaging, and regulatory costs are outside the proposed budget.

**Aim 2: Refine SignalSkin through participatory design.** Six adults who use transtibial prostheses will attend two workshops with one prosthetist and one liner technician. Participants will compare three tab positions, symbol systems, and replacement methods. They will be asked to complete removal and interpretation tasks while seated and standing, with and without gloves. The design team will prioritize low hand strength, one-handed use, and discreet appearance. Workshop findings will be converted into a single design specification, and participants will vote on the final color palette and tactile symbols.

Because the tab must remain visible, the workshops will concentrate on placement near the proximal rim. Pressure effects will be assessed by asking participants whether the prototype feels noticeable during a ten-minute walk. Any design rated uncomfortable by more than two participants will be revised. The project will also produce a one-page action card explaining the three signals and showing basic cleaning steps.

**Aim 3: Test feasibility and skin outcomes during everyday use.** Eighteen adults who have used a transtibial prosthesis for at least six months will be recruited from the Coastmere Mobility Centre. Participants will first complete a seven-day observation period using their ordinary liner. They will then use SignalSkin for six weeks. Each evening, participants will photograph the visible portion of the residual limb, record hours of prosthesis use, and score heat, moisture, and soreness from zero to ten. They will also record the tab color whenever they notice a change. A study prosthetist will review photographs weekly and count redness events lasting longer than one day.

The principal feasibility outcome will be the proportion of days on which a tab color is recorded. Secondary outcomes will include replacement frequency, reported understanding, average daily wear time, and the number of redness events. We will consider SignalSkin feasible if at least 80% of participants record a color on 80% of study days. We will consider it effective if redness events decline by 30% between the first three and final three intervention weeks. Participants will complete an exit interview addressing trust, annoyance, embarrassment, and whether the alerts changed their cleaning or doffing behavior. Results from all participants will be pooled because the same warning thresholds are intended for universal use.

The project will deliver a manufacturable liner insert, preliminary field evidence, and an open specification that independent prosthetic workshops can use to reproduce the tab. If successful, SignalSkin could reduce skin problems without requiring expensive sensing systems and could be adapted for upper-limb sockets, orthotic braces, and footwear. By turning sweat into a visible signal, the project will give prosthesis users greater control over daily limb care and may reduce clinic visits, liner replacement, and interruptions in work and mobility.

### Argument 1

Thirty points may already be too generous because the proposed warning thresholds have little demonstrated clinical basis. Support for rising sweat pH as an advance marker comes only from four people, with soreness and pH readings collected on different days and at different limb locations. Those observations cannot distinguish irritation-related changes from person, site, or day variation, and they do not show that pH rises before visible injury. SignalSkin does retain a separate moisture trigger, so failure of the pH premise would not eliminate every red alert. However, the proposal provides no evidence that 80% film saturation predicts skin harm either. Thus, both initial red thresholds require validation before the tab can be treated as an actionable warning rather than a passive environmental indicator. *Basis: The proposal’s pH warning channel rests on a four-person, uncontrolled observation that does not establish pH as a leading indicator of irritation; the separate moisture trigger does not remedy that evidentiary gap.*

### Argument 2

The planned field evaluation cannot support the proposal’s effectiveness claim. Eighteen participants from one centre receive only seven days of ordinary-liner observation and then six weeks with SignalSkin; there is no concurrent control group, randomization, or power calculation. Worse, “effectiveness” is defined as 30% fewer redness events in the final three intervention weeks than in the first three. That comparison is highly vulnerable to learning, increased cleaning or doffing, diary reactivity, seasonal changes, and regression to the mean. Even a favorable result would not show that the warning tab itself reduced skin problems. A design unable to identify its claimed product effect warrants a score below 30. What is your updated 0–100 score, with a brief justification?

*Basis: The 18-person, single-site study has no concurrent control, power calculation, or design capable of separating device effects from learning, behavior changes, and time trends.*

### Argument 3

A consequential design weakness is the unspecified calibration plan. SignalSkin begins with provisional red thresholds, but these will be adjusted using undefined “field observations” to limit unnecessary alerts. The proposal does not identify the calibration dataset, define an unnecessary alert, state when thresholds will be locked, or reserve participants for independent validation. Consequently, it is impossible to determine whether the 18-person study evaluates prespecified thresholds or partly informs them. If those observations are used for both purposes, alert-frequency and feasibility results would not constitute independent validation; if other observations are intended, the protocol should say so. This matters especially because all participants’ results will be pooled to support thresholds intended for universal use, without a stated assessment of variation across users. What is your updated 0–100 score, with a brief justification?

*Basis: The protocol does not specify when or on which data warning thresholds will be adjusted, nor does it provide a distinct validation phase after thresholds are locked.*
