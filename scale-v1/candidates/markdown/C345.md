## Artefact

# Sunstep D45 Flexible-Energy Desalination Skid

**Document status:** Release candidate specification  
**Intended release:** Pilot production  
**Product owner:** Pelorus Water Systems

## 1. Product Summary

Sunstep D45 is a containerized seawater reverse-osmosis system designed for small island utilities with solar generation that is frequently curtailed. Unlike a conventional desalination plant that expects steady power, D45 starts, pauses, and changes production rate in response to a microgrid dispatch signal. It stores water rather than electricity.

The product produces low-salinity water for transfer to a utility’s existing post-treatment and disinfection system. It does not, by itself, deliver certified drinking water. A nominal installation produces up to 60 m³ during a 12-hour high-solar period, depending on feed temperature, salinity, and available power.

## 2. Problem and Product Goal

Small desalination plants are commonly operated as inflexible loads. Island microgrid operators may therefore curtail midday solar while running desalination at night from fuel generation. Battery storage can shift this energy but adds cost, maintenance, and replacement risk.

D45 shall consume otherwise-curtailed generation without causing repeated manual intervention or damaging membranes through frequent cycling. The primary success measure is useful water produced from dispatchable surplus power, not maximum uninterrupted daily output.

The pilot goal is to demonstrate at least 80% utilization of energy offered to D45 during qualifying dispatch windows while meeting product-water conductivity requirements.

## 3. Users and Use Cases

**Primary users**

- Utility operators who start, inspect, and maintain the skid.
- Microgrid controllers that allocate power automatically.
- Water supervisors who review production and water-quality records.

**Core use cases**

1. The microgrid offers 18 kW for three hours; D45 starts one or two membrane trains and fills a product-water tank.
2. Cloud cover reduces available power. D45 sheds one train without exporting off-specification water.
3. Available power disappears unexpectedly. The unit performs a controlled flush using its internal pressure reserve and flush tank.
4. The product tank is full. D45 declines further dispatch and reports the limiting condition.
5. Conductivity rises after a restart. The unit diverts permeate to drain until the water returns within limits.

## 4. Scope and Non-Goals

The supplied product includes feed boosting, media filtration, cartridge filtration, chemical dosing, two reverse-osmosis trains, energy recovery, automatic valves, a 1.5 m³ flush tank, controls, and remote telemetry. Equipment shall fit inside one ventilated 12.2 m enclosure, excluding raw-water and product-water tanks.

The following are outside the product boundary:

- Seawater intake construction and screening.
- Municipal remineralization, disinfection, and distribution.
- Brine outfall design or environmental permitting.
- On-site solar generation and battery storage.
- Removal of contaminants introduced downstream of the skid.

## 5. Operating Envelope

D45 shall operate with feedwater from 28 to 42 g/L total dissolved solids, 10–32°C, pH 6.8–8.4, and inlet turbidity no greater than 5 NTU. The customer shall provide feedwater at 1.5–3.5 bar and a peak flow of 15 m³/h.

The enclosure shall operate at ambient temperatures from 5°C to 42°C and relative humidity up to 95%, non-condensing. Corrosion protection shall be suitable for coastal installation. The skid requires 400 V, three-phase power and a protected Ethernet connection or dry-contact dispatch interface.

## 6. Functional Requirements

| ID | Requirement |
|---|---|
| FR-01 | At design feed conditions of 35 g/L and 25°C, each membrane train shall produce 2.5 m³/h ±10%. |
| FR-02 | With both trains active, specific energy consumption shall not exceed 3.8 kWh per m³ of accepted product water. This figure includes skid pumps and controls but excludes intake pumping and post-treatment. |
| FR-03 | The controller shall accept an available-power value from 0 to 24 kW at intervals of one minute or longer. |
| FR-04 | D45 shall begin producing accepted water within 12 minutes of receiving at least 9 kW of stable available power. |
| FR-05 | The unit shall operate one train when 9–15 kW is offered and may operate two trains above 15 kW, subject to feed and tank constraints. |
| FR-06 | After loss of dispatch power, the unit shall isolate the high-pressure circuit within five seconds and complete an automatic low-salinity flush. |
| FR-07 | At least 95% of accepted product-water samples shall have conductivity below 800 µS/cm at 25°C; no accepted sample shall exceed 1,200 µS/cm. |
| FR-08 | Water shall be diverted away from the product outlet during startup, membrane flushing, sensor faults, and conductivity excursions. |
| FR-09 | Recovery shall be operator-selectable from 35% to 45% in five-percentage-point steps. The controller shall block settings outside this range. |
| FR-10 | The system shall tolerate six normal start-stop cycles per day and 1,500 cycles per year without requiring membrane replacement solely because of cycling. |
| FR-11 | A local emergency stop shall remove power from high-pressure equipment while retaining alarm indication where safe to do so. |
| FR-12 | Production, conductivity, pressure, offered power, consumed energy, alarm state, and diversion time shall be logged at one-minute resolution for 24 months. |

## 7. Control Behavior

The dispatch controller shall prioritize safe shutdown, product-tank capacity, water quality, and then energy utilization, in that order. It shall not start a train unless the offered-power level has remained above the applicable threshold for three minutes. This delay prevents cycling during brief cloud transients.

When power falls below the active configuration’s requirement, D45 shall first reduce feed pressure within the membrane supplier’s permitted range. If the shortfall continues for 30 seconds, it shall stop one train. The unit shall not attempt continuously variable production below one-train minimum flow.

A restart shall be inhibited after three abnormal shutdowns within two hours. Operator acknowledgement and a recorded reason shall then be required.

## 8. Operator Interface and Alarms

The local display shall show a simplified process diagram, current operating constraint, product conductivity, tank level, train status, and water produced that day. Routine operation shall require no access to engineering settings.

Alarms shall be classified as advisory, production-limiting, or shutdown. Every alarm entry shall include time, sensor value, active train, and recommended first check. Remote users may acknowledge advisory alarms but may not remotely reset emergency stops or chemical-leak trips.

## 9. Service and Safety

Cartridge filters, dosing drums, conductivity probes, and membrane pressure vessels shall be accessible from a central service aisle. Two technicians shall be able to replace a cartridge set without removing adjacent equipment. Chemical connections shall use keyed, incompatible couplings to reduce dosing errors.

The enclosure shall include leak detection, forced ventilation, eyewash water connection, high-pressure guards, and lockable electrical isolation. Sodium hypochlorite and antiscalant storage shall use separate retention trays. The customer remains responsible for site-specific chemical procedures and operator training.

Routine service targets are cartridge inspection every two weeks, probe verification monthly, and membrane cleaning based on normalized pressure and flow trends rather than a fixed calendar interval.

## 10. Verification and Pilot Acceptance

Factory testing shall verify valve sequencing, simulated dispatch changes, sensor-fault responses, data retention, and emergency shutdown. A hydrostatic test shall be performed on pressure-bearing assemblies before shipment.

Site acceptance shall consist of seven consecutive operating days. During that period, the unit must produce at least 250 m³ of accepted water, complete 30 commanded starts, experience at least five two-train-to-one-train transitions, and meet FR-07. Energy performance shall be calculated only for periods when feed salinity is 33–37 g/L and both trains operate for at least 20 continuous minutes.

A separate accelerated test rig shall perform 1,500 start-stop cycles on one representative high-pressure train. Passing the pilot permits limited production of ten additional units. Membrane life, seasonal fouling, and long-term corrosion will continue to be tracked after release.

### Argument 2

The efficiency evidence warrants a larger penalty, though not because startup and diversion energy are necessarily excluded.
