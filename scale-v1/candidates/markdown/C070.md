## Artefact

# Leaving the Gap: Measuring Visitor Response to Conservation Rotations

The Brindlewick Museum rotates light-sensitive works on paper every eight weeks. Curators usually fill each vacated position immediately, but an alternative is to leave a framed blank backing board with a note explaining why the work is resting in dark storage.

We compared these approaches across two gallery bays for 16 days. During the first eight days, Bay A displayed a conservation gap while Bay B received a substitute work; the treatments were then reversed. School visits and two evening events were excluded. Ceiling counters recorded passers-by, and an observer timed stopping parties. Every fifth party was asked, “Why might an artwork be removed from this gallery?”

| Measure | Conservation gap | Substitute work |
|---|---:|---:|
| Passers-by | 1,540 | 1,580 |
| Parties stopping | 508 (33%) | 426 (27%) |
| Median dwell time | 74 seconds | 68 seconds |
| Correct survey response | 81/132 (61%) | 49/128 (38%) |

The gap increased the stopping rate by six percentage points. A visitor-level bootstrap gave a 95% interval of 2–10 points. The difference appeared in both bays after reversal, although it was larger in Bay A. Dwell time changed little, suggesting that the gap attracted attention more reliably than it sustained it.

Survey responses indicate that visible absence may communicate preventive conservation better than seamless substitution. However, the question tested immediate recall rather than durable understanding, and respondents who agreed to answer may not represent all visitors. The analysis also treats observations from the same day as independent, despite shared crowd conditions.

For the next rotation, the museum should retain one explained gap in each bay rather than leaving every removed position open. This preserves the interpretive benefit without allowing blank spaces to dominate the display. A later trial should randomize treatments by day and test whether visitors remember the explanation after leaving the gallery.
