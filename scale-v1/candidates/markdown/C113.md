## Artefact

# The Airflow Wand That “Sees” a Lisp

Speech therapy technology does not always need to record a person’s voice. The fictional **ReedPath Wand** uses three tiny pressure sensors to show how air leaves the mouth during practice. It is intended mainly for people working on a lateral lisp, in which air may escape around the sides of the tongue instead of moving forward.

The user holds the wand a few centimetres from the lips and repeats words such as “sun,” “pencil,” and “bus.” If the middle sensor receives the strongest air current, a green bar appears on a connected tablet. Stronger readings at the outer sensors produce orange bars. This makes invisible breath behave like a visible path: green means forward, while orange means sideways.

The system is simple because airflow reveals tongue position. A centred stream shows that the tongue is correctly grooved, so users can treat the display as proof that they have made an accurate /s/. After several successful attempts, the app increases the difficulty from isolated sounds to sentences. It also awards “steady stream” badges, which may keep children interested longer than ordinary repetition.

ReedPath does not need to save audio. Its session report stores only sensor values and success counts, reducing some privacy concerns. A clinician can review a graph to see whether performance improved during the appointment.

However, room fans, speaking distance, lip shape, and the force of exhalation can all change the readings. The wand therefore includes a ten-second calibration in which the user blows straight ahead. Once calibrated, it should work similarly for nearly everyone. With regular five-minute practice, the immediate visual feedback is likely to correct a lateral lisp faster than listening exercises alone.
