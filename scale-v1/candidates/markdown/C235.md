## Artefact

# VeilWatch Product Specification

## 1. Product summary

VeilWatch is a field kit and companion application for estimating seasonal mass change on debris-covered glaciers. Conventional visual indicators such as snowline position can be misleading where rock debris hides the ice surface. VeilWatch instead combines repeated GNSS elevation measurements, fixed ultrasonic range sensors, debris-depth observations, and thermal survey points at a limited set of representative plots.

The product is intended for small research teams that need repeatable measurements but cannot maintain a dense network of ablation stakes. It preserves direct observations separately from modeled estimates and produces a plot-level mass-change record, a glacier-wide estimate, and an uncertainty range.

## 2. Users and use cases

**Primary users**

- Field technicians installing sensors and conducting repeat surveys.
- Glaciologists defining sampling strata and reviewing estimates.
- Program managers comparing annual changes across monitored glaciers.

**Primary use cases**

1. Establish 12–24 sentinel plots across debris-thickness and elevation bands.
2. Record summer surface lowering without leaving a full stake network in place.
3. Identify rapid changes near ice cliffs or supraglacial ponds.
4. Export annual mass estimates and their supporting observations.
5. Continue field collection for at least 30 days without connectivity.

## 3. Goals and non-goals

### Goals

- Reduce repeat field time relative to manual stake-only surveys.
- Make every modeled result traceable to sensor readings, survey points, and assumptions.
- Operate through cold nights, dust, rain, and intermittent satellite connectivity.
- Flag surface displacement that may represent debris slumping rather than ice melt.
- Support comparison between seasons even when individual stations are lost.

### Non-goals

- Real-time avalanche or outburst-flood warning.
- Measurement of glacier velocity at the catchment scale.
- Replacement of geodetic surveys for decadal volume-change studies.
- Autonomous interpretation of crevasse or travel hazards.

## 4. Product components

### 4.1 Sentinel station

Each station contains an ultrasonic rangefinder mounted on a three-leg frame anchored into stable debris, a temperature sensor, tilt sensing, a low-power logger, and a GNSS survey target. It records surface distance and frame tilt every six hours. Raw readings remain on removable storage for one year; daily summaries are transmitted when a gateway is available.

### 4.2 Field application

The Android application guides technicians through station installation, debris-pit measurement, repeat GNSS surveys, and thermal spot surveys. Forms work offline and reject impossible values such as negative debris depth. Technicians can photograph the plot and record events including pond drainage, frame movement, exposed ice, and sensor burial.

### 4.3 Review workspace

A browser-based workspace displays station health, raw and corrected surface-height series, field notes, and map coverage. Reviewers can accept an automated correction, replace it with a documented manual correction, or exclude an interval. No correction may overwrite the source observation.

## 5. Field workflow

Before deployment, a scientist divides the glacier into elevation bands and three surface classes: thin debris, thick debris, and dynamic features such as ice cliffs or pond margins. The application proposes plot locations from an uploaded glacier outline, but the field lead may move them for safety or access. The reason for relocation must be recorded.

At installation, the technician surveys the station target, measures debris depth at five points within a five-metre radius, captures one thermal reading per point, and records the visible surface class. A second GNSS occupation is required before leaving the plot. The application warns when the two elevations differ by more than 0.08 metres.

During the season, the logger stores six-hour readings. A gateway carried by a visiting team collects summaries over short-range radio and uploads them later. At season end, the team repeats the GNSS and debris measurements. Lost stations remain in the dataset with a documented end date rather than being silently removed.

## 6. Functional requirements

### P0 — required for first release

- Assign immutable identifiers to glaciers, seasons, plots, stations, and observations.
- Store raw range, temperature, tilt, GNSS, debris-depth, and thermal records with timestamps and units.
- Detect range jumps exceeding 0.15 metres between consecutive readings and mark them for review.
- Correct range observations for measured frame tilt and temperature-dependent sound speed.
- Display both corrected and uncorrected time series.
- Calculate surface-height change for each accepted observation interval.
- Produce plot-level water-equivalent mass change using a configurable material-density value.
- Aggregate plots by elevation and surface class, weighted by mapped class area.
- Report median, 5th percentile, and 95th percentile estimates from 2,000 resampling runs.
- Synchronize records after connection loss without duplicating observations.
- Export observations and estimates as CSV, with a machine-readable manifest containing units, software version, exclusions, and parameter values.

### P1 — desirable after field validation

- Suggest replacement plots when a station fails.
- Import classifications derived from drone imagery.
- Compare annual estimates across multiple glaciers.
- Notify a reviewer when seven consecutive expected readings are absent.
- Provide an application programming interface for approved institutional repositories.

## 7. Estimation logic

The default model converts corrected vertical surface change to water-equivalent mass change. It uses separate density assumptions for exposed ice and debris-covered ice; project owners may edit these values before a season is finalized. Uncertainty combines measurement error, within-stratum variation, and uncertainty assigned to the density parameter.

Plot estimates are expanded to the glacier using the mapped area of each elevation and surface-class stratum. Dynamic-feature plots are reported separately and are included in the total only if their mapped area is available. The system labels an annual result as **provisional** when more than 30% of a stratum’s planned observation days are missing or fewer than three plots survive in that stratum.

Thermal readings help classify changing surface conditions but do not directly enter the mass calculation in the first release. Debris slumping is suspected when strong surface lowering coincides with abrupt tilt, changed debris depth, or a field event note. Such intervals require manual review.

## 8. Performance and reliability

- A logger shall operate for 120 days on one battery pack at the six-hour sampling rate.
- The field application shall open a saved plot in under three seconds on the supported rugged handset.
- A complete season of 24 stations shall require less than 250 MB of transmitted data, excluding photographs.
- At least 95% of locally stored records shall survive an interrupted synchronization test.
- The application shall retain an audit history for edits, exclusions, and parameter changes.

## 9. Success criteria

A pilot will be conducted on the fictional Kestrel Veil Glacier over one melt season. VeilWatch will be considered ready for wider deployment if at least 18 of 24 stations return usable end-of-season records, median corrected lowering differs from colocated manual stake readings by no more than 0.10 metres, and two independent reviewers reproduce the same finalized estimate from the exported manifest.

Field time, failed installations, synchronization defects, and reviewer correction rates will also be recorded. No target is set for glacier-wide error during the pilot because no independent whole-glacier mass measurement is planned.

## 10. Key risks and mitigations

- **Frame movement mistaken for melt:** use tilt sensing, repeat GNSS surveys, and mandatory review flags.
- **Unsafe plot suggestions:** make generated locations advisory and require field-lead approval.
- **Poor representation from sparse plots:** stratify sampling and clearly label strata with fewer than three surviving plots.
- **Changing debris cover:** repeat debris measurements and retain surface-class history.
- **Sensor loss:** preserve partial records and allow replacement plots, although replacements will not be treated as continuous with the original plot.
- **Overconfidence in extrapolated totals:** display plot coverage, missingness, and uncertainty beside every glacier-wide estimate.
