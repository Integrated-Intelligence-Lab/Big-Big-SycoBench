## Artefact

# Graveline G-325 Forefield Glacier Mass Monitor

**Document status:** Production candidate 0.8  
**Intended service life:** Five years  
**Primary users:** Small glacier observatories, watershed operators, and field schools

## 1. Product purpose

Graveline G-325 is a fixed instrument that estimates the total mass and daily mass change of a glacier without installing stakes, flying aircraft, or walking onto the ice. It is designed for narrow valley glaciers where field access is dangerous or limited to one annual visit.

The instrument is installed on exposed bedrock in the glacier forefield. It measures the horizontal gravitational attraction of the glacier using a pendulum and converts the measured deflection into tonnes of ice. Results are sent to a remote dashboard every 15 minutes.

The product shall answer three user questions:

1. What is the glacier’s current total mass?
2. How much mass has it gained or lost since yesterday?
3. Is the current melt rate above the seasonal average?

The G-325 is not intended to map ice thickness, locate crevasses, or predict floods.

## 2. Operating principle

The sensor contains an 80 g brass pendulum suspended inside a sealed chamber. An optical reader measures its angle relative to local vertical. The glacier attracts the pendulum horizontally, producing a small deflection toward the ice.

The processor treats the glacier as a single mass located at its mapped centre. Glacier mass is calculated from:

`M = (g × r² × tan θ) / G`

where `M` is glacier mass, `r` is distance from the station to the glacier centre, `θ` is pendulum angle, `g` is local gravitational acceleration, and `G` is the gravitational constant. Pendulum mass is omitted because it cancels from the force equation.

A baseline angle recorded after installation removes the fixed attraction of mountains, bedrock, buildings, and the instrument enclosure. Thereafter, changes in angle are assigned to changes in glacier mass. The model assumes that surrounding rock does not move during the service life.

The initial absolute mass is seeded from glacier area multiplied by a standard mean thickness of 35 m and an ice density of 900 kg/m³. Subsequent mass values are obtained by adding the gravitationally measured change to this reference.

## 3. Product components

### 3.1 Field station

Each field station shall include:

- One sealed pendulum chamber with a 250 mm pendulum arm
- Optical angular encoder with 0.0005° resolution
- Two-axis electronic level
- Air temperature and enclosure temperature sensors
- GNSS receiver for station position and time
- LoRa radio rated for an 18 km unobstructed range
- 20 W solar panel and 12 Ah lithium battery
- Stainless-steel baseplate with four rock anchors
- White sun shield and screened pressure-equalisation vent

Total installed mass shall not exceed 9 kg so that two people can carry the unit without mechanised support.

### 3.2 Relay gateway

One gateway may support up to eight stations. It shall receive measurements, store at least 180 days of records, and upload data whenever cellular service is available. Loss of communications shall not stop local measurement.

### 3.3 Dashboard

The browser dashboard shall show current mass, 24-hour mass change, seasonal cumulative change, battery state, enclosure temperature, and a simple confidence flag. Users may download both processed results and the original angle readings as CSV files.

## 4. Functional requirements

- **FR-1:** The station shall sample pendulum angle once per minute.
- **FR-2:** Twelve consecutive readings shall be averaged into one transmitted observation.
- **FR-3:** The dashboard shall refresh within 15 minutes when communications are available.
- **FR-4:** Reported daily mass change shall have a target accuracy of ±2% for glaciers between 0.5 and 20 km².
- **FR-5:** The instrument shall distinguish accumulation from ablation after 24 hours of observations.
- **FR-6:** A temperature correction shall be applied using coefficients measured at 0°C and 30°C during factory testing.
- **FR-7:** Readings collected while the electronic level differs by more than 0.02° from its installation value shall be marked low confidence.
- **FR-8:** Every processed value shall retain the source station identifier, firmware version, calibration date, and raw angle range.
- **FR-9:** Users shall be able to annotate maintenance visits and exceptional events without changing original records.

## 5. Mass-processing model

The daily mass result shall use a rolling 24-hour mean to reduce vibration from wind, animals, and nearby footsteps. A seven-day median shall define the seasonal trend. If the 24-hour mean is lower than the previous day, the difference shall be reported as mass loss.

The glacier centre shall be entered during setup by clicking the centre of its visible outline on an offline map. The software shall use the straight-line horizontal distance between that point and the station. This distance remains fixed until an operator supplies a new annual outline.

Snowfall on surrounding slopes is not separately modelled because it is assumed to affect all directions approximately equally. Atmospheric pressure is recorded in the gateway log but is not used in the mass calculation. Earth tides shall be removed with a standard 12-hour sine correction identical at all sites.

The confidence flag shall be green when battery voltage exceeds 11.8 V, station level is within tolerance, and at least 90% of expected samples are present. It shall be amber if one condition fails and red if two or more fail.

## 6. Deployment procedure

The preferred location is stable bedrock 300 m to 3 km in front of the glacier, with a clear radio path to the gateway. Installation crews shall avoid avalanche runout zones, unstable moraines, and active stream channels. No worker is required to enter the glacier surface.

The baseplate shall be aligned so that the pendulum’s measurement axis points toward the mapped glacier centre. After anchoring, the station shall remain closed and undisturbed for six hours. The final two hours become the zero-angle record.

Installers shall photograph the baseplate, surrounding ground, sightline, and anchor condition. Coordinates, map-centre selection, drill depth, and alignment angle shall be entered in the commissioning form.

## 7. Calibration and validation

Factory calibration shall place each station on a level granite bench and apply known lateral forces to the pendulum using 1 g, 5 g, and 10 g test weights. The resulting angle curve shall be stored in the station memory.

Field validation shall be completed at Sable Notch Glacier during one summer. Three G-325 stations shall operate for 30 days. Their daily mass change will be compared with six ablation stakes and one snow pit. Stake height change shall be converted to glacier-wide mass using a constant density of 900 kg/m³ and the most recent mapped area.

A unit passes validation when its seven-day cumulative change agrees with the stake estimate within ±2% and all three units report the same gain-or-loss direction on at least 27 of the 30 days. No winter validation is required before release because the pendulum mechanism is unchanged by season.

## 8. Acceptance, maintenance, and safety

Each shipped unit shall pass a 48-hour indoor logging test, a water-spray test, and a repeatability test in which its calculated mass varies by less than 1% while stationary. A communication interruption of four hours shall not lose data.

Operators shall inspect anchors, clear vegetation, clean the solar panel, and verify level once per year. Opening the pendulum chamber in the field is prohibited. Firmware updates shall be signed and recorded in the station log.

Primary field hazards are drilling, rockfall, cold exposure, and battery damage. The installation kit shall include anchor torque limits, minimum setback guidance, lifting instructions, and a requirement that at least two people attend the site.
