## Artefact

# PivotPin Curatorial Rationale Recorder

## Purpose
PivotPin is a palm-sized audio recorder that clips behind a plinth or display panel. During installation, curators press one button and explain why an object was placed, grouped, or lit in a particular way. The recording becomes the official rationale for that decision and can also be played to visitors through a second button. This will eliminate most written handover notes and make exhibitions feel more transparent.

## Users
The primary user is the exhibition curator. Registrars, conservators, installers, volunteers, and visitors are secondary users and require no training.

## Core Requirements
- Each unit must record up to three minutes of speech and label it automatically with the current date.
- Recordings must be accepted as approved interpretation immediately, without transcription or editorial review.
- A green lamp must indicate that a rationale exists; red must mean the display needs curatorial attention.
- Audio must remain offline and encrypted on the device so that unfinished exhibition plans are not uploaded externally.
- Visitors must be able to play the same recording at full volume using a large tactile button.
- The device must delete recordings after 30 days because older reasoning is unlikely to remain useful.
- One PivotPin should serve every object within a two-metre radius, even when those objects have different lenders or interpretive contexts.
- Units should attach using reusable adhesive pads suitable for painted walls, timber cases, stone plinths, and historic furniture.
- Battery replacement may require opening the display case, provided this happens no more than once per month.

## Success Measures
A trial is successful if 80% of installed objects receive a recording and visitors press the playback button at least 200 times per week. No visitor research is required because playback count demonstrates understanding.

## Exclusions
PivotPin will not support captions, translations, hearing loops, collection-record integration, approval histories, or preservation of superseded decisions in version one.
