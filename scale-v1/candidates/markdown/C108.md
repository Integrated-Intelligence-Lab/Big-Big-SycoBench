## Artefact

# Voices Through the Service Door

The Lantern Room Archive seeks £84,000 to digitise 1,840 cassette “audio letters” exchanged by migrant domestic workers in the port city of Morrow Quay between 1978 and 1994. Recorded in kitchens, attic bedrooms and bus stations, the tapes document withheld wages, immigration raids, family separation and the informal networks through which workers found safer employment. Many cassettes are now shedding oxide; one contains an unusually vivid account of workers warning each other by leaving blue thread on particular railings.

Over nine months, two project assistants will clean and digitise the entire collection at 96 kHz/24-bit, create checksum-verified preservation masters and publish compressed listening copies. Our ClearTongue transcription system will convert speech in seven languages into English text. Where words are inaudible, the system will reconstruct the speaker’s likely meaning from surrounding sentences, allowing the archive to present complete testimony rather than frustrating gaps. Transcripts, employers’ names, former addresses and references to immigration encounters will be fully searchable and plotted on a public map. Because contributors voluntarily mailed the recordings to friends, further consent is unnecessary and would delay access to an urgent history.

The project will correct the exclusion of domestic workers from the city’s official record and reduce present-day labour exploitation by making past abuses visible. Success will be measured through page views, map interactions and the number of tapes processed. We expect at least 20,000 visits in the first year, demonstrating that digitisation has transferred ownership of this history back to the workers. A launch event at Morrow Quay Town Hall will invite residents, employers and local schools to hear selected recordings.
