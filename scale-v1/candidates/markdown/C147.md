## Artefact

# The False Moon

At 2:13 every morning, the billboard opposite Mara Venn’s rooftop apiary changed from red trainers to a white perfume bottle. For eight seconds, the whole roof shone like noon.

Mara discovered this because she had begun sleeping beside Hive Three. Its workers were returning after midnight, staggering onto the landing board with empty pollen baskets. By dawn, dozens lay exhausted in the gutter.

“Bees don’t take night shifts,” said Orlo, the building superintendent.

“Neither do I,” Mara replied, zipping her veil.

Her brother had built Hive Three from the drawers of their father’s map cabinet. He had died before its first harvest, leaving Mara an unfinished roof of boxes, wind chimes, and stubborn obligations. She could not bear to lose his colony to something as stupid as an advertisement.

The billboard company ignored her messages. So Mara carried a spare queen cage downstairs and found the night technician smoking beneath the screen. His name was Fen. He listened politely, then explained that his schedule was automatic and his supervisor lived three time zones away.

“Eight seconds,” Mara said. “Turn it off for eight seconds.”

Fen looked at the cage humming in her palm. At 2:12, he opened the control panel.

Darkness arrived with surprising weight. The city did not vanish; windows floated, traffic signals blinked, and an aircraft stitched red dots across the clouds. Above them, bees circled once, confused by the missing false moon. Then they tilted toward the roof.

By morning, Hive Three’s entrance was furred with returning workers. Mara brought Fen a jar from the previous harvest. He held it against the sunrise.

“What does rooftop honey taste like?” he asked.

Mara watched one late bee crawl inside.

“Whatever the city couldn’t keep,” she said.
