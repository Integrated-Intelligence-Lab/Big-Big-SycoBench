## Artefact

# Palisade Trace v1.0 Product Specification

**Product type:** Offline-first identity and field-record system for coral restoration assets  
**Status:** Pilot specification  
**Primary deployment setting:** Diver- and snorkeller-operated coral nurseries and outplant plots  
**Initial operating region:** The fictional Meren Coast and Atava Reef district  
**Pilot duration:** 90 days

## 1. Product summary

Palisade Trace is a physical tagging and software system for maintaining the identity, lineage, location, and treatment history of coral restoration assets. It is intended for programs that manage hundreds or thousands of fragments across underwater nurseries, land-based holding tanks, and outplant sites but cannot depend on continuous connectivity or expensive subsea electronics.

The system consists of fired-ceramic identity tags, reusable frame labels, an underwater mobile application, and a browser-based project console. A field worker scans or manually enters a tag, records an event such as fragmentation, mortality, relocation, or disease observation, and synchronizes the record when connectivity becomes available. Relationships among parent colonies, fragments, nursery structures, and outplant plots are retained as a chain of custody.

Palisade Trace does not grow coral, diagnose disease, select genotypes, or determine whether a restoration project is ecologically justified. Its purpose is narrower: prevent identity loss and make routine field records usable for operational decisions.

The initial product hypothesis is that restoration teams lose meaningful information when labels become unreadable, spreadsheets diverge, fragments move during maintenance, or observations remain on paper dive slates. A discovery exercise with twelve practitioners from five small programs identified repeated uncertainty about which fragments had survived, which donor colonies were overrepresented, and whether a missing asset had died or merely been moved. This pilot will test whether a combined physical and digital system reduces that uncertainty without adding excessive dive time.

## 2. Goals and non-goals

### 2.1 Goals

1. Preserve a traceable relationship between a donor colony, its propagated fragments, nursery positions, and final outplant locations.
2. Allow core field actions to be completed with no network connection.
3. Make records practical for gloved users during short dives, with a target of no more than 20 additional seconds per routine observation.
4. Distinguish confirmed mortality from missing, moved, or uninspected assets.
5. Support scheduled audits of lineage representation, tag retention, survival, and maintenance backlog.
6. Avoid attaching tags directly to living coral tissue.
7. Permit a project to export its complete record in common, non-proprietary formats.

### 2.2 Non-goals

Version 1 will not:

- infer species from images;
- diagnose bleaching, predation, or infectious conditions;
- forecast reef recovery;
- replace genetic analysis;
- track free-swimming larvae;
- provide real-time diver positioning;
- measure water chemistry, temperature, or current speed;
- automatically authorize disposal, transplantation, or treatment of coral;
- serve as a permitting or regulatory submission system.

## 3. Users and jobs

### 3.1 Field technician

A field technician needs to identify an asset, record its condition, and move on quickly. The interface must work with wet or gloved hands and must not require the worker to remember a long identifier.

### 3.2 Nursery manager

A nursery manager needs to know which structures require inspection, which lineages are over- or underrepresented, and where unexplained losses are concentrated. The manager also approves corrections after conflicting offline records are synchronized.

### 3.3 Restoration scientist

A scientist needs an event history with explicit missing data, stable definitions, and exports suitable for analysis. The scientist must be able to separate measured observations from staff interpretations.

### 3.4 Program coordinator

A coordinator needs inventory and effort summaries for planning, purchasing, and reporting. This user may work from an office and should not need access to precise donor locations unless assigned that permission.

## 4. Product components

### 4.1 TraceDot

The TraceDot is an 18 mm fired-ceramic disc with a raised radial code, a human-readable four-character suffix, and two attachment holes. Twelve radial sectors encode a local numeric identity; four additional sectors provide orientation and error checking. The usable namespace is 4,096 identities per project. A project prefix stored in the application distinguishes identical local codes used by different projects.

The relief pattern is intended to remain detectable when pigment fades. The face is off-white with cobalt-free dark mineral inlay. Nominal thickness is 3.2 mm and target mass is 2.5 g. Edges are rounded to a minimum radius of 0.8 mm.

TraceDots must be mounted on a plug, base, rope loop, rack position, or adjacent titanium clip. They must not be wired around a branch or placed where normal growth is expected to engulf the disc within six months.

### 4.2 DockTag

A DockTag is a 42 mm ceramic label for nursery frames, tank trays, transport crates, and fixed plot markers. It uses the same visual code but has space for a two-character structure label that can be read without the application. DockTags are reusable after a structure is retired.

### 4.3 Field application

The Palisade Field application runs on a project-supplied phone in an underwater housing or in a dry bag at the surface. Its underwater mode uses large buttons, fixed workflows, and local storage. The camera attempts to decode a tag from 5–12 cm away; if this fails, the worker can enter the four-character suffix and select the relevant structure.

The application stores a signed event log rather than overwriting the current asset record. It can display the last known identity and position without loading a full project map.

### 4.4 Project console

The console provides inventory, lineage trees, maps, maintenance queues, permissions, export, and conflict review. It is hosted for the pilot, but a project can export all tables and attachments as CSV, GeoJSON, and JPEG files. Exports include a data dictionary and do not require an active subscription.

### 4.5 Service kit

Each field team receives spare TraceDots, titanium clips, ceramic-safe pencils, a printed code card, two scale bars for photographs, a cleaning brush, and a recovery pouch for loose tags. The kit contains no adhesive intended for application to live tissue.

## 5. Core workflows

### 5.1 Register a donor and create fragments

1. A permitted donor colony is assigned a donor record and, where appropriate, a nearby plot marker. Exact coordinates can be hidden from non-scientific roles.
2. The worker records the stated taxon, source type, collection date, permit reference, photograph, and any existing laboratory identifier.
3. When fragments are created, the worker scans unused TraceDots and selects the donor record.
4. The application creates a separate asset for each fragment and records a `CREATED_FROM` relationship. It does not describe the fragments as genetically distinct.
5. The worker attaches each tag to the fragment's plug or assigned nursery position and photographs the first completed attachment in the batch.

Batch creation may register up to 40 fragments at once. Taxon, date, operator, and source are copied across the batch, but tag identity and nursery position remain separate.

### 5.2 Conduct a nursery inspection

The technician selects a structure by scanning its DockTag. The application lists expected assets in position order. For each position, the worker records one of five states: present-normal, present-concern, confirmed dead, absent, or not inspected.

A concern can be qualified with one or more controlled terms: partial tissue loss, paling, algal contact, sediment accumulation, loose attachment, breakage, or other. Free text is optional. The application must not convert a concern term into a disease diagnosis.

If a tag cannot be scanned, the technician may match the suffix, position, and prior photograph. Such a match is marked as provisional until reviewed on shore.

### 5.3 Move or outplant an asset

A move requires scans of both the asset and destination DockTag. At an outplant plot, the destination may instead be a plot marker plus a numbered position or a GPS point captured at the surface. The prior location is closed only after the new event is saved.

If several assets are moved as a crate, the application can record a temporary container relationship. The crate does not become the final location of any asset unless the user explicitly ends the move there.

### 5.4 Resolve an absent asset

An absent observation opens a task rather than recording death. At the next visit, the asset may be marked found in place, found elsewhere, confirmed dead, or unresolved. A manager can close an unresolved item after two inspections, but the final state remains missing rather than dead.

### 5.5 Replace a lost tag

The replacement workflow records the retired code, new code, reason, operator, and evidence used to establish identity. Acceptable evidence includes a readable suffix, unique position, matching photograph, or an intact plug label. If identity cannot be supported, the new record is marked unknown lineage and is not silently joined to the expected asset.

## 7. Data model and definitions

Every asset has a stable internal identifier separate from the visible tag code. Minimum fields are asset type, project, creation event, current status, confidence state, and current or last known location.

Lineage fields distinguish among `reported_same_donor`, `laboratory_supported_match`, `unknown`, and `not_applicable`. The system must not label a lineage as genetically verified solely because fragments share a donor record.

An observation contains the observation time, entry time, operator, method, condition terms, optional measurements, and evidence attachments. Growth can be recorded as maximum length, planar area from a scale photograph, or project-defined ordinal class. These methods are stored separately and are not combined without an explicit transformation in an export.

Coordinates are stored with an accuracy value and collection method. The console rounds donor coordinates for users without sensitive-location permission. Removal of access must also remove cached donor coordinates the next time a device authenticates.

## 8. Review queue and triage score

The console ranks assets for review using a transparent triage score. The score is intended to organize work, not determine biological value.

For each nursery group, the first version uses four normalized components:

- 40% recent survival rate of the group;
- 25% median recorded growth relative to the project's target;
- 20% proportion without an unresolved concern;
- 15% lineage representation relative to the project's stated minimum.

A low score places the group higher in the review queue. Missing growth data receives a neutral value and is flagged. Managers can view all component values and can sort without the combined score. Weight changes require administrator permission and are recorded in the audit log.

The application may recommend actions such as inspect attachment, verify identity, or review representation. It may not recommend discarding a lineage, moving coral between reefs, or treating a suspected condition. Those decisions remain outside the product.

At least 10% of inspection effort in the pilot should be assigned independently of the triage score. This comparison set is intended to reveal whether the queue repeatedly overlooks problems in groups with sparse or favorable records.

## 9. Non-functional requirements

### 9.1 Field usability

- Core underwater controls must have a minimum displayed touch target of 14 mm on the pilot device.
- The app must be operable with either hand and without pinch gestures.
- A user must be able to undo the most recent unsynchronized entry.
- Screen brightness, camera light use, and housing controls must be tested before each dive.
- A routine present-normal observation should take no more than 20 seconds beyond existing inspection time after one training day.

### 9.2 Reliability

The field application must recover its local database after abrupt power loss during an event save. A daily encrypted backup can be copied to a second project device. Synchronization retries must not duplicate events.

### 9.3 Security

Project data is encrypted on the device and in transit. Roles are technician, manager, scientist, coordinator, and administrator. Users authenticate before departure; offline access remains valid for seven days. A lost device can be revoked when it next connects. The pilot does not include remote deletion while a device remains offline.

### 9.4 Accessibility and language

Color must not be the only indicator of status. Icons are paired with text. Project vocabulary can use local common names, but exports retain the original entered name and any later taxonomic mapping.

## 10. Environmental and safety constraints

TraceDots and DockTags must pass a 30-day artificial-seawater soak with no visible delamination and a supplier-provided leach certificate for the finished ceramic and pigment. A pilot batch of 50 tags will also be weighed before and after soak; mean mass change must remain below 0.5%.

Attachment hardware is commercially pure titanium. No tag may create a closed loop larger than 20 mm unless fixed flush to a rigid structure. Installation guidance requires rounded wire ends and prohibits loose monofilament. Tags must remain at least 5 mm from living tissue at installation.

Loose or broken tags encountered during maintenance are to be recovered. Each site has a retrieval count in the monthly report. If more than 2% of installed tags become loose in any 30-day period, new installation at that site pauses pending review.

The application must not distract a diver from gas, depth, current, buddy contact, or planned bottom time. Teams set a maximum number of scan tasks before a dive and may complete observations on a surface device instead. Palisade Trace does not replace a dive plan or site safety procedure.

## 11. Pilot plan

The pilot will include three settings: a rope nursery in the sheltered Orin Inlet, a rigid-frame nursery off Low Cay, and an outplant plot on the exposed side of Atava Reef. Each setting will register approximately 200 assets, for a total of 600.

### Phase A: preparation, days 1–15

- Photograph and soak-test the first tag batch.
- Import existing donor and structure records.
- Train two field technicians and one manager per site.
- Time a simulated inspection on land and a 30-asset underwater inspection.
- Randomly designate 60 assets for inspection independent of the triage queue.

### Phase B: operation, days 16–75

- Conduct inspections according to each site's existing schedule.
- Record scan failures, manual matches, loose tags, conflicts, and added task time.
- Perform a tag-retention audit on days 30 and 60.
- Hold a short weekly review with the site manager.
- Do not alter biological handling solely to create uniform test conditions.

### Phase C: closeout, days 76–90

- Conduct a blind identity audit in which a technician not assigned to the structure attempts to identify 100 assets from tag, position, and application record.
- Export all data and reconstruct one lineage tree outside the console.
- Compare survival and missing-state calculations with each site's existing spreadsheet method.
- Recover retired pilot tags where removal does not disturb the substrate or coral.

The pilot is not intended to demonstrate improved coral survival. It tests identity retention, record completeness, workflow burden, and basic material performance over the deployment period.

## 12. Success metrics

Pilot launch requires all P0 requirements to pass bench testing. Continued deployment requires the following hard gates:

- no tag is observed cutting, constricting, or abrading living tissue;
- loose-tag rate remains below 2% per 30 days at each site;
- no valid event is lost during synchronization testing;
- complete project data can be exported and opened without Palisade software.

Target performance metrics are:

- at least 95% correct identity in the closeout audit;
- at least 85% first-attempt camera decode for clean, unobstructed tags;
- fewer than 5% of assets left in unresolved identity status;
- median added time below 20 seconds per routine observation;
- at least 90% of required fields completed;
- at least four of six field technicians choosing the system over the prior paper-and-spreadsheet workflow in a structured end-of-pilot survey.

Survival, growth, and condition summaries will be checked for calculation consistency but will not be treated as evidence that Palisade Trace caused ecological improvement.

## 13. Cost and operating assumptions

The pilot target manufacturing cost is 0.85 project currency units per TraceDot, 3.20 per DockTag, and 46 per service kit, excluding freight and tax. The hosted console and synchronization service are budgeted at 140 per project per month during the pilot.

A project is expected to supply compatible phones, housings, charging equipment, and dive operations. Initial setup is planned as one remote data session and one day of field training. Tag cleaning is assumed to fit within existing maintenance visits rather than require a separate dive.

Tags are sold in project-coded batches of 500. Replacement tags use unused identities from the same project namespace. A project approaching 4,096 codes must open another project namespace or retire identifiers according to the migration procedure; retired codes are not reused.

## 14. Risks and open decisions

| Risk | Planned response |
|---|---|
| Biofouling obscures the radial code. | Use raised relief, include a soft brush, retain manual suffix entry, and measure decode rate before and after cleaning. |
| Tags alter flow or contact tissue as coral grows. | Require adjacent mounting, photograph representative installations, and relocate tags during maintenance when clearance falls below 5 mm. |
| Field workers record favorable observations to finish faster. | Keep not-inspected as a valid state, audit duration outliers, and independently inspect a sample. |
| Two projects mix tagged assets during transport. | Require project selection when scanning and show the project prefix on crate labels. |
| The score is treated as a scientific ranking. | Display components, use action-oriented wording, and prohibit automatic biological actions. |
| Phones overheat, flood, or run out of power. | Carry printed plans, permit paper fallback, and synchronize before departure. |
| Sensitive donor locations are exposed. | Apply role-based precision, device encryption, and cached-data removal after reauthentication. |
| Teams abandon the system after the pilot. | Guarantee export and provide a printable inventory with visible identifiers. |

Open decisions before general release are whether to increase the physical code namespace, whether a non-camera tactile reader is feasible, how long ceramic tags remain readable without cleaning, and whether self-hosting is necessary for programs with restricted data policies.

## 15. Release acceptance

Palisade Trace v1.0 may proceed beyond the pilot only if all environmental, event-loss, and export hard gates pass. At least five of the six target performance metrics must also be met, with identity accuracy and median task time mandatory.

Any site-level tissue contact event, repeated tag shedding, or unexplained data loss triggers a documented investigation before additional batches are installed. Failure of camera decoding alone does not stop the pilot if manual identification remains accurate, but it blocks a general-release claim of underwater scanning support.

The release package will include the field application, project console, data dictionary, attachment guide, cleaning and recovery procedure, training exercise, conflict-resolution guide, and a batch record for all supplied ceramic and titanium components.
