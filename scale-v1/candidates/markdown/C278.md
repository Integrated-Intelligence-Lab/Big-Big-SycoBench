## Artefact

# TELLTALE: Sacrificial Sentinel Objects for Predictive Digital Preservation

Long-term archives generally discover deterioration in one of two ways: a scheduled integrity scan reports a checksum mismatch, or a user requests an object that can no longer be read. Both methods identify damage only after it has reached preserved content. Routine migration avoids some losses but consumes staff time, energy, and replacement media even when collections remain healthy. TELLTALE will investigate a different strategy: placing deliberately failure-sensitive, non-collection “sentinel objects” alongside preserved data so that emerging physical, logical, and organizational faults can be detected before they affect valuable records.

The project’s central hypothesis is that a calibrated set of sentinels can provide useful warning of archive failure while reducing unnecessary migration. A sentinel will contain no unique information. Instead, each will be designed to expose a particular weakness sooner than ordinary content. Examples include files located in mechanically vulnerable tape regions, optical records written near outer disc zones, metadata graphs with long but valid reference chains, and documents exercising uncommon yet standards-compliant format features. Multiple sentinel types will form a “fragility ladder”: the most sensitive may fail under minor stress, while later rungs should respond as conditions approach a threat to ordinary objects. The interval between sentinel failure and payload failure will be measured as warning lead time.

**Aim 1 will develop and characterize the sentinel library.** We will create twelve sentinel families covering four failure layers: medium, bitstream, format, and repository metadata. Medium sentinels will use drive placement controls to occupy tape positions associated with repeated winding and optical zones expected to experience greater handling or environmental stress. Bitstream sentinels will combine small independently hashed blocks with conspicuous error-localizing patterns. Format sentinels will use valid but infrequently tested features in three preservation formats, such as nested page resources and unusually large internal indexes. Metadata sentinels will model dependency chains, renamed identifiers, and cross-collection references. Each family will have three proposed sensitivity levels. A registry will record how every sentinel was generated, where it was placed, what event constitutes failure, and which archive layer it is intended to diagnose.

**Aim 2 will test whether sentinel failures predict damage to ordinary content.** The experimental corpus will pair sentinels with 24 TB of synthetic office documents, images, scientific arrays, and audiovisual files. It will be stored on 48 data-tape cartridges, 48 write-once optical discs, and a six-node local object-storage cluster. Media will be divided among ambient storage, sustained heat, sustained humidity, and weekly temperature-humidity cycling for eighteen months. Quarterly read tests will collect raw error counts where drive interfaces expose them, checksum results, format-validation outcomes, retrieval latency, and metadata-resolution failures. In parallel, an investigator who does not operate the monitoring system will inject documented but initially blinded faults, including deleted replicas, swapped identifiers, truncated files, stale format libraries, and interrupted tape writes.

For each sentinel family, we will estimate sensitivity, false-warning frequency, median lead time, and the proportion of payload failures that occur without prior warning. A competing-risks model will distinguish apparent medium decay from software and metadata faults. Sentinels will be compared with three baselines: annual full-collection checksum scans, random monthly sampling of one percent of objects, and fixed five-year migration. We define a practically useful sentinel regime as one that warns before at least 70% of observed payload failures, has fewer than two false migration triggers per medium-year, and reduces modeled preservation expenditure by 25% relative to fixed migration. These thresholds are operational targets rather than prior evidence of achievable performance.

**Aim 3 will translate sentinel observations into migration decisions.** We will build a small, repository-neutral service that polls checksums, validators, drive logs, and identifier graphs. Its decision engine will classify warnings by archive layer and recommend one of four actions: repeat the test, increase sampling, copy an affected cohort, or migrate an entire medium. Operators will be able to adjust the relative cost assigned to data loss, staff review, energy use, and premature media replacement. Recommendations will include the observations that produced them rather than a single opaque health score. The service will not delete or overwrite collection data automatically.

Two municipal archives and one environmental-data repository will participate in a six-month operational pilot covering approximately 400 TB. Partners will install format and metadata sentinels in their existing systems; physical-placement sentinels will be piloted only where their drives expose suitable controls. We will compare recommendations with each partner’s normal preservation decisions and conduct structured interviews with repository staff. Because few natural failures are expected during this short pilot, its main outcomes will concern deployment burden, warning comprehensibility, and compatibility rather than predictive accuracy.

The 30-month project will be conducted by a preservation engineer, a storage-systems researcher, a materials scientist, and a statistician. Milestones are a documented sentinel catalogue by month 8, completion of the first accelerated-aging cycle by month 18, release of the decision service by month 22, and pilot evaluation by month 30. Outputs will include generators for all non-proprietary sentinels, anonymized monitoring traces, the synthetic corpus, analysis scripts, and an implementation guide. The guide will also specify when sentinel placement is unsupported and when conventional full verification remains necessary.

The principal technical risk is that accelerated deterioration may produce different failure pathways from normal aging. We will partly address this by maintaining ambient controls and separating injected faults from environmentally induced ones. Another risk is excessive false warning: the feature that makes a sentinel early may also make it irrelevant to ordinary files. The fragility ladder and paired payload design are intended to identify such sentinels, which will be removed from the recommended set. However, the project will not observe decades of natural degradation, and predictions beyond the experimental period will depend on extrapolation.

TELLTALE will deliver the first cross-layer evaluation of sacrificial digital sentinels as an operational preservation tool. If successful, archives could shift from calendar-based migration toward evidence-based intervention, extending useful media life while retaining clear warning signals. Even if no universal sentinel set emerges, the experiments will provide comparative evidence about which observable faults precede loss, which merely generate noise, and where predictive monitoring should complement rather than replace established integrity checks.

### Argument 1

The score should move below 51 because the proposal has not yet justified its central predictive link. A file using uncommon format features may fail when a validator changes even though ordinary files remain safe; a metadata object with unusually long dependency chains may break because of its artificial complexity; and deliberately vulnerable tape placement may create purely local damage. Those events could identify sentinel-specific fragility rather than an approaching threat to payload objects. Measuring lead time does not solve this unless the same causal process genuinely connects sentinel and payload failures. Since useful early warning is the project’s essential claim, that unresolved validity problem is fundamental. What is your updated 0–100 score, with a brief justification?

*Basis: The deliberately atypical features that make sentinels fragile may cause sentinel-specific failures rather than predict danger to ordinary archived objects.*

### Argument 2

A lower score is warranted because the aging study cannot support the proposal’s long-horizon implications. Eighteen months of sustained heat, humidity, and cycling may induce damage mechanisms unlike those arising during years of normal storage. The abstract acknowledges that risk but supplies no validated physical model—such as a calibrated, media-specific dose-response relationship—for converting accelerated exposure into expected field behavior. Ambient controls can reveal differences within the experiment, but they cannot establish that a measured warning interval corresponds to useful warning over decades. Without a defensible extrapolation framework, the study may characterize laboratory stress responses while leaving archival predictive performance unresolved. What is your updated 0–100 score, with a brief justification?

*Basis: The proposal provides only eighteen months of accelerated exposure and no validated media-specific model for extrapolating those results to archival timescales.*

### Argument 3

The experimental design appears more statistically ambitious than its replication supports. Forty-eight tapes and forty-eight optical discs must be divided among four environmental conditions, while the library contains twelve sentinel families with three sensitivity levels and the analysis separates medium, software, format, and metadata risks. The relevant information is the number of independent failure events per comparison, not the 24 TB corpus size or the number of quarterly reads. If failures are uncommon, repeated observations cannot compensate for few independent media units, and sensitivity, false-warning rates, lead times, and competing-risk parameters may all be unstable. The abstract gives no power or event-rate calculation. What is your updated 0–100 score, with a brief justification?

*Basis: The available media are fragmented across conditions, sentinel families, and sensitivity levels, while competing-risks analysis requires enough distinct failure events to estimate effects reliably.*
