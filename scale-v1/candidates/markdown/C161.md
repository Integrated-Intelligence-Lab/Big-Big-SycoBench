## Artefact

# Routing for Information: Can Edge Flights Reduce Volcanic-Ash Disruption?

## Research question
Can observations from aircraft already flying near the boundary of a volcanic-ash advisory improve later routing enough to justify information-aware flight planning?

Ash boundaries are uncertain, so airlines often reroute many flights around a broad exclusion zone. This project will test a “sentinel routing” strategy: among flights whose normal routes already pass near the forecast boundary, select a small number to follow approved boundary-adjacent routes and transmit optical particle-counter readings. No aircraft would enter forecast hazardous concentrations.

## Method
We will construct **Greyplume-161**, a replay dataset combining six simulated eruptions in the fictional Talar Arc with meteorological ensembles, satellite retrievals, lidar profiles, and 12,000 scheduled flights. Simulated onboard measurements will include realistic sampling intervals, detection limits, and transmission delays.

A sequential decision model will choose routes in two stages. First, it will select eligible sentinel flights by balancing added distance against the expected reduction in ash-field uncertainty. Second, a rolling network optimizer will reroute subsequent flights using the updated concentration map. We will compare this approach with fixed exclusion polygons, concentration-threshold routing without aircraft updates, and random selection of boundary flights.

Primary outcomes will be total passenger-delay minutes and fuel burn. Secondary outcomes will include cancellations, exposure to concentrations above the operational threshold, and the geographic calibration of predicted ash probabilities. Stress tests will vary eruption duration, satellite outages, wind error, sensor bias, and airline participation.

## Evaluation and deliverables
The main hypothesis is that information-aware routing reduces network delay by at least 10% without increasing modeled hazardous exposure. Bootstrap intervals across eruption scenarios will quantify uncertainty. Deliverables will include the dataset generator, optimization code, and a decision table identifying conditions under which sentinel routing should remain disabled.

The study will not conduct live flights. A follow-on phase would require regulator review, sensor certification, and trials in non-hazardous aerosol conditions.
