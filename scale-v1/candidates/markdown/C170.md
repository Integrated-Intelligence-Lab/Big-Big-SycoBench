## Artefact

## Empty Carts and Hidden Circuits in the Brackenford Toll Books, 1278–1322

The Brackenford corpus contains 612 legible toll entries from three gates linking the inland market of Brackenford, the Veyran Pass, and the river port of Lornwick. Rather than mapping only valuable cargoes, this analysis asks whether recorded empty carts reveal circular trade routes overlooked in commodity-based reconstructions.

Each entry was coded by direction, month, vehicle type, declared cargo, and toll paid. Gate activity was normalized per 100 surviving ledger-days because coverage varies sharply by decade. Of 146 cart entries, 39 were marked “void” or charged the reduced empty-vehicle toll. Empty carts were directionally concentrated: 28 travelled from Lornwick toward Brackenford, while only 7 moved toward the port; four had uncertain direction. A binomial test against equal movement gives *p* < 0.001.

The imbalance fits a circuit in which carts carried grain and wool downhill to Lornwick, returned empty to Brackenford, and then collected iron goods for the northern pass. Supporting this interpretation, 31% of northbound loaded carts carried worked iron, compared with 9% on the other two road segments. The pattern remains when entries from the unusually complete 1304–1311 ledger are excluded, although the empty-return ratio falls from 4.0:1 to 2.9:1.

Seasonality complicates the picture. Empty returns peak in March and April, whereas port-bound grain traffic peaks in September. This gap may indicate that some carts waited, changed owners, or served local journeys not captured by the gates. Moreover, “void” could mean toll-exempt rather than physically empty, and repeated passage by the same cart cannot be identified. The surviving books also exclude pack animals, probably understating traffic across the pass.

Even with these limitations, empty-vehicle entries suggest that Brackenford’s roads formed a three-stage commercial circuit rather than separate spokes converging on one market.
