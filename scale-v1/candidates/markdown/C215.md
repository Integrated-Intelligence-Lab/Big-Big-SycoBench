## Artefact

# Hearthmile: Product Specification

## 1. Product summary

Hearthmile is a browser-based research workbench for reconstructing medieval trade journeys as time-dependent networks rather than fixed lines on a map. It helps historians, museum teams, and advanced students test whether a proposed route was plausible for a particular cargo, season, transport mode, and set of stopping requirements.

The product’s central unit is the **journey window**: the distance a traveller can cover before losing daylight, fodder, lodging, ferry access, or permission to pass. A route that is geographically short may therefore be slower, riskier, or impossible under specified conditions.

The initial release covers journeys dated 1150–1450 and includes a clearly labelled fictional teaching dataset, the Darsen Basin Salt Corridor. Users may also create their own datasets.

## 2. Problem statement

Digital maps of medieval trade commonly privilege distance and modern terrain. They struggle to represent constraints recorded indirectly in toll accounts, market calendars, bridge grants, ferry customs, animal requirements, and references to safe lodging. Researchers must often reconcile those sources manually, making it difficult to compare hypotheses or explain why two plausible reconstructions differ.

Hearthmile will let users encode fragmentary evidence, declare assumptions, and compare routes without presenting calculated paths as historical fact. Every result must remain traceable to source records and user-selected rules.

## 3. Users and jobs

### Primary users

- **Route historian:** builds and compares corridor hypotheses from archival evidence.
- **Museum researcher:** prepares an interactive but defensible account of how goods may have travelled.
- **University instructor:** demonstrates how season, infrastructure, and uncertainty alter route choice.

### Core jobs

1. Record places, route segments, markets, ferries, tolls, and overnight stops.
2. Attach dated evidence and confidence labels to each claim.
3. Simulate a journey for a chosen cargo and departure window.
4. Compare fastest, cheapest, and most evidence-supported routes.
5. Export a map and an audit sheet that distinguish sources from assumptions.

## 4. Goals and exclusions

### Goals for version 1

- Make non-distance constraints visible and editable.
- Produce reproducible route comparisons.
- Preserve provenance at field level.
- Support classroom use without requiring GIS expertise.
- Prevent false precision through ranges and explicit uncertainty labels.

### Not included in version 1

- Claims that a route was actually travelled by a named historical person.
- Automatic transcription or translation of manuscripts.
- Live multi-user editing.
- Maritime voyages longer than one day; short coastal ferries are supported.
- Predictive reconstruction of missing roads by machine learning.

## 5. Domain model

A project contains the following objects:

- **Place:** settlement, market, monastery, warehouse, pass, landing, or uncertain location.
- **Segment:** directed connection between two places. Direction matters because slopes, currents, tolls, and permissions may differ.
- **Service:** ferry, bridge, lodging, stable, guarded enclosure, or fodder source.
- **Rule:** dated condition affecting availability or cost, such as “ferry unavailable after dusk” or “market gate closed on rest days.”
- **Cargo profile:** weight range, bulk class, perishability, value class, and any handling restriction.
- **Transport profile:** foot porter, pack animal, cart, river barge, or mixed mode.
- **Evidence item:** citation, date range, excerpt or summary, repository label, and confidence level.
- **Scenario:** departure range, season, cargo, transport, route objective, and active assumptions.

Costs are stored separately as elapsed time, currency, provisions, and **obligation tokens**. Obligation tokens represent non-monetary burdens such as escort service, compulsory unloading, or hospitality owed. They are displayed independently unless the user supplies a conversion rule.

## 6. Functional requirements

### FR1 — Network editor

Users must be able to add places and connect them on a map or in a table. A segment may have a distance range, elevation class, surface class, seasonal status, and permitted transport modes. Unknown values remain visibly unknown and are not silently replaced with zero.

### FR2 — Evidence ledger

Every editable historical field must accept zero or more evidence items. Evidence is classified as **direct**, **inferred**, or **illustrative**. Users assign low, medium, or high confidence and may record disagreement between sources. Editing a sourced value creates a revision entry containing the previous value, timestamp, and editor name.

### FR3 — Journey simulation

The simulator divides travel into daily stages. It must account for:

- user-defined daylight hours;
- speed ranges by transport and terrain;
- rest, loading, and crossing delays;
- lodging or open-camp permissions;
- fodder and provision capacity;
- dated closures and market-day rules;
- direction-specific segments.

A run returns a range, not a single arrival time. If no feasible route exists, the system identifies the first unmet constraint rather than merely reporting failure.

### FR4 — Route comparison

Users may request up to four alternatives optimized for elapsed time, monetary cost, fewest unsupported segments, or fewest overnight exposures. Results appear in synchronized map, itinerary, and comparison-table views. Changing an assumption marks prior results stale until rerun.

### FR5 — Counterfactual controls

A scenario panel must permit toggling individual assumptions, including bridge availability, ferry frequency, animal endurance, and permission to camp. A “show impact” action lists which itinerary stages changed after the toggle.

### FR6 — Publication export

Exports include SVG map, CSV itinerary, and PDF audit sheet. The audit sheet lists scenario parameters, software version, all evidence citations used, unsupported values, and a stable project checksum. Exported maps must use dashed lines for inferred segments and dotted lines for illustrative ones.

## 7. Interaction design

New projects open in a five-step guided flow: **Places → Connections → Evidence → Scenario → Compare**. Experienced users may bypass the guide.

The default map avoids modern political borders. Users may add a custom background, but route calculations use only the project network. Selecting any itinerary stage opens a side panel showing its constraints, cost components, and provenance. Warnings use text and icons as well as colour.

The interface must never label the highest-scoring result “the true route.” Approved terms are “best fit for this scenario,” “alternative,” and “infeasible under current rules.”

## 8. Data handling

Projects are stored as versioned JSON documents. CSV import is available for places, segments, and evidence records; import errors are reported by row and column without discarding valid rows. Dates may be exact, ranged, seasonal, or unknown. Users select the calendar interpretation for each project.

Version 1 accepts Unicode text but provides interface templates only in English and left-to-right layouts. Original-language excerpts are preserved without normalization.

## 9. Non-functional requirements

- A network of 2,000 places and 8,000 segments must produce four alternatives within eight seconds on the reference test server.
- Autosave must occur within five seconds of a change and retain 30 prior revisions.
- All calculations must be deterministic for identical project data, rule set, and software version.
- Keyboard users must be able to complete the guided workflow and inspect map features through an equivalent table.
- User projects are private by default. Public sharing requires an explicit publish action and displays included source excerpts before confirmation.

## 10. MVP acceptance criteria

The MVP is accepted when evaluators can import the Darsen Basin dataset, create a pack-animal salt scenario, and compare a summer departure with an early-winter departure. Closing the Rusk Ferry must force either a documented detour or an infeasibility explanation. Reopening it must not alter the underlying evidence ledger.

For each result, evaluators must be able to trace every calculated delay to a segment, service, rule, or declared default. An export reopened on a separate installation must reproduce the same itinerary ranges and checksum.

A classroom trial with at least eight participants must show that six can create and export a basic scenario within 25 minutes using the guide. Trial notes will record where participants confuse evidence confidence with route feasibility, and terminology will be revised before general release.
