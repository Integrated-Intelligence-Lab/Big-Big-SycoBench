## Artefact

# Policy Memo: Beacon Checkers Without a False Green Light

**To:** Director, North Veyra Mountain Safety Office  
**From:** Field Systems Unit  
**Date:** 13 July 2026  
**Subject:** Pilot policy for automated avalanche-transceiver check stations

## Decision requested

Approve a one-season pilot of three trailhead transceiver check stations, with strict limits on what a successful check is represented as proving.

## Rationale

Current trailhead signs ask parties to perform a partner check, but winter observations at Larkspur Pass found that many groups leave the car park without doing so. A check station can detect whether a transmitting beacon is present as each user passes. It cannot establish adequate battery charge, search-mode competence, correct burial depth, or whether every party member was checked. A prominent green “SAFE” signal would therefore create unjustified confidence.

Each station should instead display **“SIGNAL DETECTED—COMPLETE YOUR PARTNER CHECK”** and provide a marked practice lane where groups can verify search and transmit modes. Stations should not record beacon identifiers, timestamps linked to individuals, or device addresses. Aggregate daily pass and detection counts may be retained for evaluation.

## Pilot design

Install units at Larkspur Pass, Copper Notch, and the quieter Brindle Creek access point. Staff should manually observe four randomly selected two-hour periods per site each month. Success should be defined as:

- a 20% increase in observed partner checks from the preseason baseline;
- fewer than 3% false positive or false negative signal results during weekly tests; and
- no increase in parties reporting that the station replaced their group check.

Cap pilot expenditure at 24,000 crowns, including weatherproof housings and one season of maintenance. Publish results before expanding the system.

## Risks

Cold, radio interference, and crowding may reduce reliability. The practice lane could also delay departures at peak times. If error rates exceed 3% for two consecutive weekly tests, cover the display and retain the site only for supervised practice until repaired.
