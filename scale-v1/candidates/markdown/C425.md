## Artefact

# Trellis A425 Product Specification

**Product:** Trellis A425 Acoustic Farm Monitor  
**Release:** Pilot 1.0  
**Status:** Build approval candidate  
**Owner:** Pelagos Loom Systems  
**Primary deployment:** Suspended kelp and shellfish farms in coastal ocean water

## 1. Product summary

Trellis A425 is a two-channel passive acoustic monitoring node for offshore farms. It has two related purposes:

1. Detect acoustic patterns associated with loose fittings, repeated line impacts, sudden tension releases, and nearby vessel activity.
2. Produce a calibrated record of the surrounding soundscape without treating every sound generated by farm infrastructure as biological activity.

Farm operators currently inspect submerged hardware on a schedule or after visible damage. Ecologists working at the same sites may deploy separate recorders, but farm-generated clicks, rattles, and cable strum can contaminate those recordings. A425 combines these workflows. It labels likely infrastructure noise at the edge, sends low-bandwidth event summaries to shore, and retains calibrated audio for later review.

The product consists of an underwater listening node, an optional surface gateway, retrieval software, and the HarborDesk web application. The node can operate for a complete service interval without the gateway. When a gateway is present, alarms and health summaries are relayed to shore; continuous raw audio remains on the node.

A425 is intended to prioritize inspections and improve interpretation of farm soundscapes. It is not a structural safety instrument, a species detector, or a substitute for physical inspection.

## 2. Problem and opportunity

Long-line farms contain shackles, droppers, floats, bridles, tensioners, and crop-bearing lines that move differently as biomass and weather change. Some defects become repetitive acoustic sources before they are visible at the surface. A loose connector may strike on every wave cycle, while a damaged line may emit a short broadband transient when fibers or attachments fail.

The same sounds can distort ecological monitoring. A recorder may count thousands of farm-hardware impulses during rough weather, producing an apparent increase in acoustic activity unrelated to animals. Operators and researchers therefore need a shared record that preserves the audio while distinguishing likely hardware, vessel, weather, and biological contributions.

The initial commercial opportunity is farms of 20 to 200 hectares that already make monthly or quarterly maintenance visits. One A425 node is expected to cover a representative farm section rather than every component. Dense installations may use one node per 250 metres of backbone line.

## 3. Goals and non-goals

### 3.1 Goals

- Notify an operator of persistent hardware-like chatter within 15 minutes when a gateway is available.
- Capture short, high-resolution audio around sudden tension-release events.
- Retain a continuous, calibrated low-band recording for at least one nominal 90-day deployment.
- Separate likely farm noise from broad biological and environmental sound categories with confidence values.
- Give technicians enough diagnostic information to decide whether an inspection should be brought forward.
- Export audio, event labels, calibration history, and device-health data in documented formats.
- Remain useful during gateway, backhaul, or positioning outages.

### 3.2 Non-goals

- Certifying that moorings, lines, or farm structures are safe.
- Pinpointing a sound to a particular shackle or crop line.
- Identifying animal species or estimating abundance.
- Recording human speech intentionally.
- Providing collision avoidance or navigation warnings.
- Streaming continuous audio to shore.
- Operating as an active sonar. The node's acoustic modem transmits only short data bursts outside configured quiet periods.

## 4. Users and key decisions

### 4.1 Farm operations lead

The operations lead monitors several sites and decides whether to schedule a vessel visit. This user needs concise alarms, a trend view, and an indication of uncertainty. They do not need to inspect spectrograms for routine events.

### 4.2 Field technician

The technician installs, retrieves, cleans, and tests nodes. They need a mobile workflow that functions without shore connectivity, records mounting details, and verifies both hydrophone channels before leaving the site.

### 4.3 Acoustic analyst

The analyst reviews clips, corrects classifications, exports calibrated data, and compares farm and reference periods. They need access to provenance, model version, clipping status, and unmodified waveform samples.

### 4.4 Research coordinator

The coordinator defines quiet periods, retention rules, and data exports for a study. A research coordinator may view operational events but cannot acknowledge maintenance alarms unless also assigned the operator role.

## 5. Operating assumptions

A425 is designed around the following initial deployment envelope:

| Parameter | Supported range |
|---|---:|
| Water depth | 12–60 m |
| Listening-node depth | 5–45 m |
| Water temperature | -2–30 °C |
| Current speed | Up to 2.2 m/s sustained |
| Service interval | 30–90 days |
| Surface-gateway distance | Up to 700 m slant range |
| Farm section represented by one node | Up to 250 m radius |
| Typical vessel access | Monthly or less frequent |

The node may be mounted on a weighted independent line or on a farm backbone through the supplied elastomeric isolator. Independent mounting is preferred for ecological studies. Backbone mounting is faster but transmits more structure-borne vibration into the enclosure.

Coverage radius is an operational planning value, not a guarantee that every fault within 250 metres will be audible. Bathymetry, bottom type, farm density, sea state, and mounting strongly affect propagation.

## 6. System architecture

### 6.1 Underwater listening node

The node contains:

- Two factory-calibrated hydrophones on a rigid 0.8 m vertical spar.
- Independent low-noise preamplifiers and 24-bit analogue-to-digital channels.
- A low-power processor for detection, feature extraction, compression, and health monitoring.
- A 2 TB removable solid-state storage cartridge.
- A 2.1 kWh rechargeable battery pack.
- Pressure, temperature, tilt, and enclosure-humidity sensors.
- A low-rate acoustic modem for event and health messages.
- A mechanical release eye and a high-visibility recovery pennant.

The two hydrophones provide redundancy and coherence information. They are not a sufficient array for reliable source localization.

### 6.2 Surface gateway

The optional Spar A4 gateway is attached to a farm float or a dedicated marker. It receives event summaries from up to eight nodes and forwards them through the available shore backhaul. It stores seven days of summaries if backhaul is unavailable.

The gateway does not request retransmission while a node is recording a configured quiet period. Emergency health messages, including leak detection, override this rule.

### 6.3 HarborDesk

HarborDesk stores fleet state, alerts, annotations, and selected clips. It presents data through a browser interface and a versioned export interface. A desktop retrieval tool imports raw cartridges directly when internet service is unavailable.

## 7. Functional requirements

Priority terms are **Must**, **Should**, and **Could**. A Must requirement blocks the pilot release if unmet.

### 7.1 Acoustic acquisition

| ID | Priority | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| ACQ-01 | Must | Record two synchronized channels at 24 kHz and 24-bit depth during baseline operation. |
| ACQ-02 | Must | Switch both channels to 96 kHz for an event window of at least 20 seconds before and 40 seconds after a trigger. |
| ACQ-03 | Must | Maintain a circular high-rate buffer so pre-trigger audio is preserved. |
| ACQ-04 | Must | Keep inter-channel sample skew below 10 microseconds. |
| ACQ-05 | Must | Store factory sensitivity by serial number and apply it only as metadata; original integer samples shall remain available. |
| ACQ-06 | Must | Mark samples affected by clipping, dropped blocks, modem transmission, or clock correction. |
| ACQ-07 | Should | Support scheduled recordings at 48 kHz for research protocols. |
| ACQ-08 | Should | Suspend high-rate event capture when remaining storage is below 5%, while retaining baseline audio and event summaries. |

The target hydrophone sensitivity is -165 dB re 1 V/µPa, with unit-specific calibration supplied from 20 Hz to 40 kHz. The useful baseline band is 40 Hz to 11 kHz. High-rate event clips use a 40 Hz to 42 kHz band. Levels displayed in HarborDesk are referenced to 1 µPa.

### 7.2 Time and calibration

| ID | Priority | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| CAL-01 | Must | Timestamp audio in coordinated system time with less than 100 ms accumulated error over 90 days after a valid pre-deployment synchronization. |
| CAL-02 | Must | Store clock-offset estimates before deployment and after retrieval. |
| CAL-03 | Must | Play an internal electrical reference signal through each acquisition channel at startup and once every 24 hours. |
| CAL-04 | Must | Flag a channel when reference amplitude changes by more than 1.5 dB from its deployment baseline. |
| CAL-05 | Should | Permit an analyst to attach a post-deployment piston-calibrator result to the deployment record. |

The electrical reference verifies electronics but does not test the hydrophone ceramic or its exposure to water. The field application therefore requires a tap and ambient-noise comparison between channels during installation.

### 7.3 Edge detection and classification

The node shall detect six top-level event classes:

- **Persistent hardware chatter:** repeated impulses or tonal knocks with timing consistent with moving farm components.
- **Tension-release transient:** an isolated high-energy broadband event with a rapid onset.
- **Vessel near-field:** sustained engine, propeller, or cavitation-like energy passing through the monitored band.
- **Weather dominated:** elevated flow, rain, wave, or mooring noise that reduces interpretability.
- **Biophony pulse:** non-vessel, non-hardware acoustic activity with temporal or spectral structure learned from reviewed examples.
- **Unknown:** an event exceeding capture criteria without a sufficiently confident class.

Each event record shall include class probabilities, start and end times, peak calibrated level, one-third-octave summaries, channel coherence, clipping state, and model version.

| ID | Priority | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| DET-01 | Must | Detect a persistent chatter candidate after at least 30 qualifying impulses in 10 minutes. |
| DET-02 | Must | Close a chatter event after 20 minutes without a qualifying impulse. |
| DET-03 | Must | Capture tension-release candidates whose 100 ms broadband level exceeds the rolling median by 18 dB on either channel. |
| DET-04 | Must | Reduce chatter confidence when similar impulses occur during modem transmission or when tilt varies by more than 8 degrees. |
| DET-05 | Must | Preserve events below the alert threshold for later analysis. |
| DET-06 | Should | Allow site-specific alert thresholds without replacing the underlying class probabilities. |
| DET-07 | Could | Learn the dominant repetition period of an acknowledged hardware source and group subsequent occurrences. |

During the first 48 hours of a deployment, alerts are held in calibration state while the node calculates ambient percentiles. Leak, storage, and battery alarms are not held. After calibration, the default chatter alert requires a class probability of 0.78 and persistence across three consecutive analysis windows.

### 7.4 Alerts

HarborDesk shall support four alert types:

1. **Inspection suggested:** persistent hardware chatter or repeated tension-release events.
2. **Acoustic coverage degraded:** weather dominance, channel failure, clipping, or excessive flow noise.
3. **Device attention:** low battery, low storage, clock fault, abnormal tilt, or gateway loss.
4. **Leak/retrieve:** enclosure humidity or pressure behaviour consistent with water ingress.

Alerts must display the triggering evidence, confidence, node state, and recommended next action. Recommended actions shall use cautious language such as “review recent clips and inspect the monitored section when practicable.” They shall not state that a line is safe or has failed.

In connected operation, 95% of inspection-suggested summaries shall reach HarborDesk within 15 minutes of the event being closed. In disconnected operation, the alert shall appear after the next successful relay or cartridge import with its original event time intact.

Operators can acknowledge, defer, or close an alert. Closure requires a reason: inspected/no defect, inspected/defect found, known source, weather artefact, test event, or other. These outcomes are retained as model-evaluation labels.

## 8. Soundscape outputs

For each local hour, A425 produces:

- Equivalent broadband sound level for the baseline band.
- 5th, 50th, and 95th level percentiles.
- Proportion of valid recording time dominated by vessel, weather, hardware, biophony, and unknown classes.
- Number of impulsive events by class.
- A data-quality score based on channel health, clipping, flow noise, and missing samples.
- A “farm-adjusted acoustic activity” series that excludes windows classified as hardware or vessel dominated.

Farm-adjusted acoustic activity is a filtering aid, not a biodiversity index. HarborDesk shall keep the unadjusted series beside it and show hours removed from the calculation. Analysts can change class exclusions, but exported results must record those choices.

The standard comparison view displays a selected farm node against either its own historical baseline or a user-designated reference node. It does not automatically claim that differences were caused by farming activity.

## 9. User experience

### 9.1 Fleet page

The fleet page shows each node as normal, calibration, degraded, attention, or retrieve. Status is based on the most severe active condition. The page displays the age of the last message so that a normal state is not confused with a silent gateway.

### 9.2 Event review

An event page includes a playable clip when uploaded, waveform, spectrogram, event features, both channel levels, and nearby tilt and weather-dominance estimates. Analysts can relabel an event and explain the correction. The original machine label remains immutable.

Because only selected clips reach shore, the interface shall distinguish “no clip uploaded” from “audio missing.” A request for a non-uploaded clip is placed in the retrieval manifest for the next cartridge import; it cannot be fulfilled remotely from a disconnected underwater node.

### 9.3 Field application

The field application guides a technician through node scan, clock synchronization, storage check, battery check, mounting photographs, depth entry, channel tap test, acoustic-modem test, and deployment activation. All steps except modem test are mandatory. A supervisor can waive a failed modem test because the recorder remains functional without it.

## 10. Data and exports

Audio is stored in one-hour lossless files with a companion manifest. Event clips are separate references into the original recording rather than destructive copies. If high-rate event audio exists, it is linked to the baseline interval.

Every deployment receives a unique identifier and records node serial number, hydrophone serial numbers, firmware, model version, calibration table, mounting type, nominal position, depth, and operator-entered notes.

Exports shall include:

- Lossless waveform files with calibration sidecars.
- Hourly metrics in comma-separated tables.
- Event and annotation records in structured text.
- A deployment manifest with checksums.
- An audit record of threshold and label changes.

HarborDesk retains event metadata for five years by default and uploaded clips for 180 days. Administrators can set clip retention from 7 to 365 days. Raw cartridges are managed by the customer and are not automatically erased after import.

## 11. Security and access

Each node and gateway has a unique manufacturing identity and accepts signed firmware packages. Gateway-to-shore messages are encrypted and rejected if replayed outside the permitted sequence window. Device credentials are replaced during depot service or after a suspected compromise.

HarborDesk uses role-based access for administrators, operators, technicians, analysts, and read-only guests. Changes to alert thresholds, retention settings, and event labels are logged with user and time.

Audio near working vessels may incidentally contain machinery patterns or indistinct voices transferred through hulls. Access to raw clips is therefore limited to analysts and administrators by default. The product does not attempt automatic speech recognition or speaker identification.

A lost node protects its configuration and event index with storage encryption. Continuous waveform files are encrypted on the cartridge but become readable through the desktop retrieval tool when the deployment key is available.

## 12. Reliability and serviceability

The enclosure shall withstand 100 m equivalent static pressure without permanent deformation and pass a 72-hour pressure soak at 75 m equivalent depth. External parts shall tolerate fouling removal with plastic scrapers and low-pressure freshwater. Copper-based exposed antifouling surfaces are not permitted on the hydrophone faces.

The battery and storage cartridge are depot-replaceable, not user-replaceable on a small vessel. A technician shall be able to exchange the complete node in under 15 minutes without removing the farm mounting collar.

The endurance target is 90 days under the reference profile: baseline recording continuously, six high-rate events per day, one acoustic summary exchange every 15 minutes, and water temperature of 12 °C. The node shall reserve enough energy to transmit a daily low-battery message for seven days after baseline recording stops.

When one hydrophone channel fails, baseline recording continues on the remaining channel and all classifications are marked degraded. When storage writes fail, the node retries on a reserved partition, stops high-rate capture, and transmits a device-attention alarm. A leak indication shuts down charging and nonessential electronics but preserves one final health transmission if power remains.

## 13. Verification and pilot acceptance

### 13.1 Bench verification

- Inject calibrated tones and transients into both channels across the supported band.
- Verify sample skew, gain error, clipping flags, file checksums, and clock drift.
- Run a 100-day accelerated storage simulation using generated audio.
- Cycle the node through 200 pressure changes between surface pressure and 60 m equivalent depth.
- Confirm signed-update rejection, replay rejection, role boundaries, and encrypted-cartridge recovery.
- Measure endurance using the reference event profile in a 20 °C tank room.

### 13.2 Controlled-water verification

A suspended test rig shall include a loose steel connector, polymer line impacts, a controlled fiber break, a small workboat pass, and playback of reviewed biological and weather recordings. Tests will be conducted at three ranges between 10 and 150 metres. The event log from synchronized cameras and load sensors will serve as ground truth.

The pilot model passes controlled-water evaluation when it achieves:

- At least 85% recall for persistent hardware chatter episodes.
- No more than two false inspection alerts per node-week.
- At least 80% recall for controlled tension-release transients above the specified trigger contrast.
- At least 90% correct separation of vessel-dominated and hardware-dominated test windows.

### 13.3 Farm pilot

Twelve nodes will be deployed across three farms at Morrow Shoal, Bellweather Reach, and Orison Inlet. Each site will include one independently mounted node and farm-mounted nodes. The pilot will span one high-growth period and one post-harvest period, with at least 120 node-days of valid audio overall.

Operators will inspect the monitored section after every high-confidence inspection alert where weather permits. They will also inspect a random sample of non-alert weeks to estimate missed persistent faults. Acoustic analysts will review 300 randomly sampled event windows and all tension-release alerts without seeing the machine label first.

Pilot acceptance requires:

- Ten of twelve nodes completing their planned deployment without water ingress.
- Median connected alert latency below 15 minutes.
- Fewer than three false inspection alerts per node-month after the 48-hour calibration period.
- At least 75% agreement between analysts on the six top-level classes.
- At least 80% of operators rating the alert evidence sufficient to make an inspection decision.
- No loss of a complete hour file after a recoverable power interruption.

Performance shall be reported separately for independent and farm-backbone mounting. Results from inaccessible alerts will be categorized as unverified rather than assumed false.

## 14. Deployment and support plan

Pilot customers receive one planning session, installation templates, a spare node for every six deployed units, and remote review of the first 48-hour calibration report. Pelagos Loom Systems performs battery and seal service annually or after six deployments, whichever occurs first.

Release proceeds in three gates:

1. **Engineering gate:** bench, pressure, security, and file-integrity tests complete.
2. **Limited-water gate:** controlled-water detection targets met and no unresolved leak mechanism.
3. **Farm gate:** twelve-node pilot acceptance complete, with installation guidance updated from mounting comparisons.

The first commercial release supports one geographic language, one shore time zone per fleet, and up to 200 nodes per HarborDesk account. Multi-fleet aggregation and automated work-order integration are deferred.

## 15. Principal risks and mitigations

| Risk | Consequence | Planned mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Structure-borne farm noise overwhelms waterborne sound | Excess alerts and biased soundscape metrics | Isolating mount, dual-channel coherence features, separate reporting by mounting type |
| Heavy fouling changes hydrophone response | Drift in calibrated levels | Smooth guards, pre/post calibration, 30-day channel comparison trend |
| Unknown farm designs differ from training data | Reduced classification accuracy | Preserve unknown class, site calibration, analyst corrections, model versioning |
| Acoustic modem creates detectable noise | Contaminated recordings | Mark transmission windows and prohibit scheduled traffic during quiet periods |
| Gateway loss is mistaken for node failure | Unnecessary retrieval | Display message age separately; preserve local recording |
| High-rate triggers exhaust storage | Loss of event detail late in deployment | Daily trigger budget, event grouping, low-storage mode |
| Operators interpret absence of alerts as proof of safety | Missed physical defect | Persistent interface disclaimer and continued scheduled inspection guidance |
| Animal calls resemble repetitive hardware sounds | Misclassification | Retain audio, show confidence, require persistence and cross-feature checks |

These decisions do not block the twelve-node pilot but affect service pricing, support responsibility, and data comparability.
