## Artefact

## Listening-Side Alignment and Failed Robot Prompts

We examined whether an elder-care robot’s approach direction affected residents’ ability to respond. The question arose after staff at three Larkmead residences observed that some residents repeatedly asked the robot to repeat meal and activity reminders.

### Method

Six mobile robots generated 12,480 prompt records over eight weeks. The analysis used 8,916 records from 31 residents whose care profiles identified a preferred listening side. A prompt was “aligned” when the robot stopped within a 60-degree arc of that side; all other positions were classified as unaligned. The primary outcome was a spoken response registered within eight seconds. Secondary outcomes were repeated prompts and staff assistance within two minutes.

A logistic model included resident fixed effects and controls for prompt type, hour, measured room noise, and whether the resident was seated. Standard errors were clustered by resident.

### Results

| Approach | Prompts | Response registered | Prompt repeated | Staff assistance |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Aligned | 4,807 | 78.6% | 13.2% | 4.8% |
| Unaligned | 4,109 | 68.9% | 21.5% | 7.1% |

After adjustment, alignment was associated with a 7.4-percentage-point increase in registered responses (95% CI: 4.6–10.1). The association was larger when room noise exceeded 55 dB: 11.8 points, versus 5.9 points in quieter conditions. However, the interaction estimate was imprecise, and no correction was made for testing multiple secondary outcomes.

### Interpretation

Approach direction appears to be a practical candidate for route-planning rules, especially in noisy communal rooms. The evidence is not causal: furniture placement or activity type may influence both stopping position and responsiveness. In addition, the logs cannot distinguish an unheard resident response from speech-recognition failure, and recorded listening preferences may not reflect daily hearing-aid use. A randomized crossover pilot should therefore measure both audible responses and robot recognition before deployment changes are made.
