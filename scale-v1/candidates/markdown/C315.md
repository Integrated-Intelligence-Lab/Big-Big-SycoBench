## Artefact

# MoonCradle: Night-Cycle Coral Settlement Raft

## Product summary

MoonCradle is a small solar-powered raft that lowers artificial settlement surfaces near a reef at night and lifts them into clearer water during the day. It is intended for restoration teams that lose juvenile corals when fixed nursery tiles become covered by sediment, turf algae, or grazing damage.

Each raft carries twelve ceramic “ribbons.” A ribbon is a flexible cable fitted with rough, cup-shaped tiles where free-swimming coral larvae can settle. During the predicted spawning period, the ribbons hang near the reef. After settlement, the raft raises them by approximately two metres each morning. The daily motion is expected to shake off loose sediment and expose the tiles to better light. Once young colonies are visible, divers detach successful tiles and fasten them to damaged reef areas.

MoonCradle differs from a conventional floating nursery because its winch follows lunar and daylight cycles rather than remaining at one depth. It also uses low-power red lamps to guide larvae toward the tiles. The raft should improve coral survival by at least 70% compared with leaving larvae to settle naturally.

## Intended users

The primary users are community reef groups, dive operators, marine parks, and small research stations. A two-person team should be able to deploy one unit from an open boat without specialist lifting equipment. Users are assumed to have basic dive training and access to local spawning forecasts.

A secondary user is a resort that wants a visible restoration activity near a snorkelling area. MoonCradle should therefore look neat, operate quietly, and be explainable to visitors.

## Operating concept

The raft is anchored 20–40 metres from a degraded reef patch in water between 3 and 12 metres deep. Its central controller stores the date of the expected spawning event. For five nights around that date, the ribbons descend at sunset to a position one metre above the seabed. Red lamps switch on for four hours. At sunrise, the winch retrieves the ribbons to a daytime depth of roughly two metres below the surface.

Outside the spawning window, the ribbons continue their day-night movement for eight weeks. This prevents fouling because algae prefer stable surfaces and will not establish on objects that move vertically. The motion may also make juvenile corals stronger by exposing them to different currents.

A turbidity sensor can override the schedule. If the water becomes very cloudy, the controller raises the ribbons. If the battery falls below 25%, all movement stops and the ribbons remain at their current depth to avoid wasting energy.

After eight weeks, divers inspect the tiles and label them empty, occupied, or ready to transplant. Occupied tiles can remain on the ribbon for up to six months. Ready tiles are clipped into stainless-steel receiving brackets installed on the reef.

## Main components

- **Float body:** Rotomoulded shell, about 1.4 metres across, with yellow corner markings. The shell is filled with closed-cell foam so it remains afloat after puncture.
- **Anchor system:** One 18 kg concrete block, eight metres of rope, and a surface swivel. The same anchor package is used at every site for simplicity.
- **Winch:** Sealed 24 V drum winch with a lifting capacity of 40 kg. It raises all twelve ribbons together.
- **Ceramic ribbons:** Twelve six-metre lines, each holding ten fired-clay cups. The cups have grooves and shaded undersides intended to resemble reef crevices.
- **Lighting:** Four dim red LED strips mounted around the lower frame. Red light is selected because it travels gently through water and should not disturb fish.
- **Power:** A 60 W solar panel and one 20 Ah lithium battery. The battery should provide three cloudy days of operation under normal use.
- **Controller:** Lunar clock, daylight sensor, turbidity sensor, motor relay, and a simple radio beacon.
- **Status panel:** Green, amber, and red lights indicate normal operation, low power, or a jammed winch.

All underwater materials should be marine-grade, non-toxic, and resistant to corrosion. Expected service life is five years, except for rope and tiles, which are replaceable.

## Functional requirements

1. The product shall deploy from a boat within 45 minutes.
2. The product shall operate in normal reef weather and moderate waves.
3. The winch shall complete one down-and-up cycle per day without tangling the ribbons.
4. The ceramic cups shall provide enough texture for coral settlement.
5. The lamps shall increase the number of larvae near the product without attracting unwanted species.
6. The system shall avoid contact between the ribbons and living coral.
7. The radio beacon shall report whether the raft is operating within a useful distance.
8. A user shall be able to replace one ribbon underwater with ordinary dive tools.
9. The complete product shall cost less than 4,000 reef credits when produced in batches of twenty.
10. At least 70% of settled corals should remain healthy through the first eight weeks.

## Deployment workflow

Before deployment, the operator enters the forecast spawning date using two buttons on the controller. Ceramic cups are soaked in seawater for one day, then attached to the ribbons. The crew lowers the anchor, clips the raft to the anchor rope, and checks that all lights turn green.

During operation, the team visits once per week. It removes floating debris, photographs three sample cups, and checks rope wear. If the status panel is red, divers inspect the winch and cut away tangled material. The product does not store detailed sensor records, so observations are written on a paper inspection card supplied with the raft.

At the end of the cycle, empty cups can be brushed clean and reused during the next spawning event. Broken ceramic pieces may be left on the seabed because they are mineral material. The battery and controller return to the supplier for recycling.

## Performance verification

A first trial will use three MoonCradle rafts and three trays of fixed tiles at Pelican Ladder Reef. After eight weeks, the team will compare the number of visible coral spots on each system. MoonCradle passes if its average count is 70% higher than the fixed trays and if at least two rafts complete most scheduled cycles.

Photographs will also be reviewed for sediment cover, obvious bleaching, and fish bites. Water clarity and temperature will be noted during weekly visits. If results are positive, twenty units can be deployed across reefs with different depths and coral types. The same operating schedule will be used so results remain comparable.

## Product boundaries

MoonCradle does not predict spawning, identify coral species, control disease, or select genetically diverse colonies. It is a settlement and early-nursery platform only. Site teams remain responsible for permits, navigation markings, storm preparation, transplant location, and long-term monitoring.
