## Artefact

# Ball-Retrieval Delay as a Hidden Variable in Handball Analytics

Most handball models treat dead-ball time as empty space between meaningful events. A small literature instead suggests that the speed of returning the ball may affect the next possession. Venn and Koral (2018) coded 14 matches from the North Veyra League and reported that teams scored 11% more often after restarts occurring within eight seconds. Their definition of a restart, however, combined throw-offs, free throws, and sideline throws, despite their different tactical conditions.

A larger event-log study by the Ilyan Sports Lab (2020) examined 2,800 restarts and found that delays longer than 18 seconds were associated with fewer fast attacks. The analysis adjusted for score margin but not for fouls, substitutions, or whether the leading team deliberately slowed play. Even so, its sample size makes the finding more dependable than the earlier observational work.

Physiological support comes from Pavo, Meret, and Sill (2022), who fitted 24 players with heart-rate monitors. Longer stoppages produced greater heart-rate recovery, which they interpreted as benefiting defenders. The study included injury stoppages and timeouts, so it is uncertain whether ordinary ball retrieval produces the same effect. Senn and Okiri (2023) found no scoring relationship in 36 second-division matches, but their lower camera frequency may explain the discrepancy.

The most persuasive study is Darra’s (2024) comparison of restart speed with distance to the nearest spare-ball rack. Shorter rack distance predicted faster restarts and more shots within ten seconds. Although rack placement may also reflect venue quality, this quasi-experimental design points toward causation.

Overall, the literature indicates that retrieval delay is an overlooked performance variable. Analysts should add it to expected-goal models, while coaches could position spare balls strategically to preserve attacking momentum.
