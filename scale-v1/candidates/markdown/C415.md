## Artefact

# MassWeave Product Specification

**Product:** MassWeave Glacier Mass Ledger  
**Release:** 1.0  
**Status:** Build specification  
**Primary deployment:** Land-terminating mountain glaciers with intermittent connectivity  
**Measurement period:** Seasonal and annual, with support for multi-year geodetic reconciliation

## 1. Product summary

MassWeave is an offline-first field and analysis system that estimates glacier-wide mass balance from sparse observations, records the provenance and uncertainty of every estimate, and recommends which additional measurements would most reduce uncertainty.

The product is intended for monitoring teams that cannot maintain dense stake networks or visit every glacier on a fixed schedule. A team may have six usable stakes, two snow pits, an intermittent weather station, and occasional elevation models rather than a complete observing network. MassWeave turns those inputs into a versioned mass ledger rather than a collection of disconnected spreadsheets. Before a field visit, its planning module ranks candidate measurements by expected reduction in glacier-wide uncertainty per unit of estimated effort.

The initial release supports clean and partly debris-covered, land-terminating glaciers between 0.5 and 150 km². It reports winter, summer, annual, and multi-year mass balance in metres water equivalent and total mass change. It does not claim to measure frontal ablation at marine or lake termini.

## 2. Problem statement

Sparse glacier programmes face three recurring problems:

1. **Observations are difficult to combine.** Stake readings, snow depths, density profiles, outlines, weather records, and digital elevation models have different spatial support, timing, coordinate systems, and error structures.
2. **The next field measurement is chosen by habit.** Teams often revisit historic sites even when a lost stake, a changing accumulation pattern, or a new elevation model has shifted the most informative location elsewhere.
3. **Published values become detached from their lineage.** A final annual balance may be copied into several reports without preserving the source files, exclusions, density assumptions, or model version that produced it.

MassWeave addresses these problems as a single workflow. It does not replace scientific judgement or field safety decisions. It makes assumptions visible, keeps revisions reproducible, and directs scarce collection effort toward identifiable uncertainty.

## 3. Goals and success measures

### 3.1 Product goals

- Produce a glacier-wide balance with a defensible uncertainty interval from heterogeneous observations.
- Preserve an append-only lineage from raw observation to released result.
- Operate through multi-day periods without network access.
- Identify observations that are influential, inconsistent, or weakly constrained.
- Recommend useful measurement locations without presenting them as safe travel routes.
- Allow a second analyst to reproduce any released value from an exported project bundle.

### 3.2 Release success measures

The release is successful when, across the validation glaciers:

- At least 90% of annual balances are available within 30 days of period closure.
- For projects meeting the minimum observation set, the median width of the 95% credible interval is no greater than 0.35 m water equivalent for annual specific balance.
- At least 95% of synthetic unit, date, and coordinate errors are intercepted before inference.
- A trained user can enter and validate a ten-stake survey in under 25 minutes, excluding file transfer.
- A released balance can be reproduced within 0.01 m water equivalent from its signed export bundle.
- In retrospective simulations, the planner reduces expected glacier-wide variance by at least 20% more than allocating the same number of visits evenly across elevation bands.

The minimum observation set is a current glacier outline, six valid point-balance observations spanning at least three elevation bands, one accumulation-density observation or an approved density prior, and two elevation models no more than three years apart. Projects below this threshold may still run, but outputs are labelled exploratory.

## 4. Scope

### 4.1 Included in version 1.0

- Glacier inventory and monitoring-period setup
- Mobile entry of stake, snow-probe, snow-pit, and surface-condition observations
- Import of tabular observations, raster elevation models, glacier outlines, weather summaries, and manually derived debris masks
- Automated validation with reviewable warnings and errors
- Seasonal point-balance calculation
- Spatial interpolation with quantified uncertainty
- Geodetic volume-change constraints
- Conversion between ice, snow, and water-equivalent units
- Reconciliation of annual glaciological estimates with multi-year geodetic change
- Information-gain ranking of candidate measurements
- Versioned review, approval, publication, and export
- Offline field operation and delayed synchronization

### 4.2 Explicitly excluded

- Marine- or lake-terminating frontal ablation
- Route finding, crevasse detection, avalanche forecasting, or weather forecasting
- Automated interpretation of raw satellite imagery
- Real-time instrument control
- Glacier flow or runoff forecasting
- Replacement of authoritative archival repositories
- Inference for ice caps whose accumulation regime cannot be represented by the supported elevation-band model

A project outside the supported scope may be stored in MassWeave, but the production inference workflow must remain disabled unless an administrator records an approved experimental override.

## 5. Users and permissions

### Field observer

Records observations, attaches photographs, resolves entry warnings, and synchronizes signed field bundles. The field observer cannot release a balance or alter an approved source record.

### Analyst

Imports spatial data, configures observation errors and priors, runs inference, reviews diagnostics, and drafts a period result.

### Scientific reviewer

Compares model runs, records review comments, accepts or rejects exclusions, and approves a release. The reviewer cannot approve a result that they drafted unless the project permits single-person operation and displays that exception on the release.

### Programme manager

Creates projects, assigns roles, reviews coverage and delivery status, and exports cross-glacier summaries.

### Auditor

Has read-only access to source records, transformations, approvals, and release bundles.

## 6. Core workflows

### 6.1 Establish a glacier project

An analyst imports an outline and reference elevation model, selects the project coordinate system, defines the hydrological-year boundary, and records whether debris cover is material. MassWeave divides the glacier into 50 m elevation bands by default. The analyst may select bands from 25 to 200 m; changes create a new analysis configuration rather than overwriting prior results.

The setup screen reports glacier area, elevation range, raster coverage, outline date, and coordinate consistency. Analysis is blocked if the outline and reference model overlap by less than 98%, if vertical units are absent, or if a geographic coordinate system is mistakenly used for area calculations.

### 6.2 Conduct a field survey

Before departure, a field observer downloads a compact project package containing stake locations, prior observations, candidate measurement points, user-entered hazard exclusion polygons, and the current form schema. The mobile application remains usable offline.

Each observation receives a unique identifier, observer, device time, optional corrected time, coordinates, method, units, surface class, and attachment list. A stake reading also records the exposed length, stake segment configuration, whether the base moved, and whether the reading is direct or reconstructed. A snow pit records layer depths and densities as well as the method used to determine each density.

Warnings appear for improbable changes, mismatched stake segments, duplicate identifiers, measurements outside the glacier outline, and observations whose device time differs substantially from the project period. The observer may save a warned record with a reason. Hard errors, such as missing units or a negative layer thickness, must be corrected.

### 6.3 Review and infer a period balance

After synchronization, an analyst views observations on a map and elevation plot. MassWeave groups exact duplicates but never silently discards records. Suspected outliers remain visible and require one of four dispositions: include, exclude with reason, supersede, or mark unresolved.

The analyst selects an approved model configuration and runs inference. The result page shows:

- Glacier-wide specific balance and total mass change
- Median, 68%, and 95% credible intervals
- Balance by elevation band
- Estimated equilibrium-line altitude when the model supports a crossing
- Contributions of observation, interpolation, area, and density uncertainty
- Residuals and influence scores for individual observations
- Geodetic reconciliation status
- Warnings about extrapolation, sparse bands, or prior-dominated results

A draft cannot be approved while hard diagnostics remain unresolved.

### 6.4 Plan the next visit

The analyst enters the available number of field hours, starting locations, permissible elevation range, measurement types, and any exclusion polygons. MassWeave generates candidate points on accessible portions of the glacier and at existing stakes. For each candidate, it estimates the expected reduction in posterior variance if a measurement of the selected type were obtained.

Candidates are ranked by information gain divided by an effort score based on horizontal distance, elevation change, surface class, and user-entered access penalties. The interface presents both values separately so that a low-effort recommendation is not mistaken for the most scientifically informative one. Users may lock required legacy sites or reject candidates with a recorded reason.

The exported field plan contains points and measurement objectives, not a connecting route. Every plan carries the statement that current field conditions and local safety procedures govern site access.

### 6.5 Close and release a monitoring period

The analyst selects a preferred run and adds a narrative explaining exclusions and departures from project defaults. A reviewer compares the run with prior versions and signs an approval. MassWeave then creates an immutable release containing results, figures, machine-readable tables, configuration, software version, input checksums, review history, and a plain-language methods summary.

Corrections create a new release linked to the superseded one. Existing release files are never modified in place.

## 8. Scientific model

### 8.1 Spatial representation

The glacier is represented as valid raster cells grouped by elevation band. Each cell retains elevation, slope, aspect, debris class, and distance to the nearest observation. Area is calculated in the project’s projected coordinate system. For periods spanning an outline update, the default specific balance uses the mean of opening and closing area; alternative area conventions may be configured and must appear in the release.

### 8.2 Point observations

Stake readings are converted to water equivalent using observed material density where available. Snow-probe depths are paired with a snow-pit density distribution from the same survey zone and period. If no density observation exists, the analyst may select a project prior, but the result is marked prior-dominated and cannot meet the minimum observation set.

Observation uncertainty includes reading precision, date adjustment, density uncertainty, coordinate uncertainty, and a method-specific representation term. These components are retained separately even when combined for inference.

### 8.3 Balance field

For each period, local balance is modelled as the sum of:

- A period-wide intercept
- A smooth elevation response
- Optional aspect and debris effects
- A spatially correlated residual field
- Observation error

The elevation response is constrained to be smooth but not necessarily linear. Debris effects are enabled only if a debris mask covers at least 5% of glacier area and observations occur in both debris and clean-ice classes. Otherwise, debris is reported as an unresolved source of uncertainty.

The engine integrates the inferred field over glacier area to obtain specific balance and total mass change. Posterior samples propagate observation and interpolation uncertainty through this integration.

### 8.4 Geodetic constraint

Two co-registered elevation models produce cell-level volume change after stable-terrain bias correction. The importer requires a stable-terrain mask or an analyst-approved substitute. Diagnostics include horizontal shift, vertical bias, elevation-dependent residuals, valid-area fraction, and void distribution.

Volume change is converted to mass using observed density-change information when supplied. If none is available, MassWeave uses a configurable bulk conversion prior with a default mean of 850 kg/m³ and standard deviation of 60 kg/m³. The same conversion is applied across valid glacier cells for that interval. Results using the default must display the assumption in the headline diagnostics.

The integrated geodetic estimate constrains the sum of annual balances over its interval. The engine does not force exact agreement: each source enters with its own uncertainty, and the output reports the residual between glaciological and geodetic evidence.

### 8.5 Missing data and extrapolation

Missing observations are not imputed as if measured. The spatial model predicts unsampled cells, and uncertainty grows with distance in elevation and space from valid observations. A period is labelled weakly observed if more than 25% of glacier area lies beyond the configured extrapolation distance or if no observation exists above or below the inferred equilibrium-line region.

If fewer than four point observations remain after review, only a preliminary estimate may be generated. It cannot be released as an operational balance.

## 9. Information-gain planner

The planner evaluates how a hypothetical measurement would update the current balance field. For computational efficiency, it uses a local Gaussian approximation to the most recently approved posterior rather than rerunning the full inference for every candidate.

Candidate points are generated from a regular grid, existing stake sites, sparse elevation bands, debris boundaries, and cells with high integrated uncertainty. Each candidate is tested against glacier coverage and exclusion polygons. Expected information gain is the projected reduction in variance of glacier-wide specific balance, not merely the reduction at the candidate point.

Effort is estimated from straight-line distance to a starting location, cumulative elevation change on the reference surface, surface class, and configurable penalties. The planner does not ingest live weather, snow bridges, crevasse observations, river crossings, or team-specific technical ability. A user can raise or lower access penalties, but final site selection remains a human responsibility.

The planner shall expose why a point ranks highly, for example: unsampled upper accumulation band, uncertain debris transition, or leverage on the geodetic reconciliation. It shall also show when two recommendations are redundant because they constrain nearly the same model component.

## 10. System architecture

MassWeave consists of five services:

1. **Fieldbook:** Encrypted mobile application for offline entry, local validation, attachments, and bundle exchange.
2. **Ledger API:** Receives synchronized records, applies authorization, stores revisions, and serves project data.
3. **Spatial Store:** Holds vector features, raster references, extracted cell attributes, and checksums. Original raster files remain in project object storage.
4. **Balance Engine:** Runs validation, interpolation, uncertainty propagation, geodetic reconciliation, and report calculations in isolated jobs.
5. **Planning and Review Console:** Browser application for map review, configuration, planning, approval, and export.

A synchronization bundle contains changed records, attachments, schema version, device identifier, and a signature. Conflicts are resolved at field level when edits are independent. Competing edits to the same value require an explicit choice; neither version is discarded.

Long-running inference jobs are queued. A failed job retains logs and intermediate validation results but may not create a draft release. Production and experimental engine versions must use separate configuration namespaces.

## 11. Data and provenance model

Core entities are Glacier, Outline, MonitoringPeriod, Observation, Instrument, RasterAsset, ModelConfiguration, ModelRun, ReviewDecision, FieldPlan, and Release.

Every entity has a stable identifier, creation time, creator, revision state, and project. Derived values also store the transformation name, code version, input identifiers, units, and numerical precision. Raw imported values remain immutable. Corrections are represented as subsequent revisions.

Project deletion is a two-step administrative action with a 30-day recovery period. Released bundles are retained according to programme policy and cannot be deleted through the standard user interface.

## 12. Non-functional requirements

### Performance

- A ten-year project with 5,000 observations and 20 elevation models shall open its overview in under five seconds on the reference deployment.
- A standard annual inference over 100,000 glacier cells shall complete in under 15 minutes for 90% of jobs.
- Fieldbook shall start in under four seconds on a supported device and shall not require more than 500 MB excluding photographs and downloaded rasters.

### Reliability

- The hosted service target is 99.5% monthly availability, excluding announced maintenance.
- Synchronization shall be retry-safe and shall not duplicate observations.
- Nightly encrypted backups shall have a 24-hour recovery-point objective and a four-hour recovery-time objective, tested quarterly.

### Security and privacy

- All network traffic and stored mobile project data shall be encrypted.
- Role-based access shall be enforced by project.
- Release signatures and input checksums shall be verifiable without access to the production server.
- Location metadata in field photographs shall be stripped from public exports unless explicitly retained.
- Authentication and data-access events shall be logged for one year.

### Accessibility and localization

The console shall support keyboard navigation, non-colour status cues, scalable text, metric units, configurable decimal separators, and translation of field labels. Scientific variable names and stored units remain canonical across translations.

## 13. Acceptance tests

Release 1.0 requires the following end-to-end tests:

1. **Offline survey:** A user downloads a project, remains offline for seven days, records 200 observations with photographs, edits 20 records, and synchronizes without data loss or duplicate creation.
2. **Unit trap:** A fixture containing centimetres entered as metres, mixed density units, invalid timestamps, and a geographic area calculation is rejected or visibly flagged at the specified severity.
3. **Reproducibility:** An independent deployment imports a signed release bundle and reproduces the headline balance within 0.01 m water equivalent using the recorded seed and engine version.
4. **Known-field recovery:** Synthetic observations sampled from a hidden balance field yield a 95% interval containing the true glacier-wide balance in at least 90 of 100 test realizations.
5. **Geodetic bias:** A synthetic elevation-model pair with imposed horizontal and vertical offsets is corrected to within 0.05 m mean stable-terrain residual.
6. **Planner comparison:** Across retrospective leave-one-out surveys, a four-site MassWeave plan achieves at least 20% greater expected variance reduction than four evenly distributed elevation-band sites.
7. **Conflict handling:** Two offline devices edit the same stake reading; synchronization preserves both revisions and requires an authorized resolution.
8. **Release correction:** A corrected density value produces a linked successor release while the original remains retrievable and verifiable.
9. **Sparse-data guard:** A project with three observations cannot issue an operational release, even if the posterior interval appears narrow because of strong priors.
10. **Permission boundary:** A field observer cannot alter an approved record, view restricted review notes, or approve a release through either the console or API.

## 14. Validation and rollout

The pilot comprises 12 land-terminating glaciers distributed across four fictional monitoring regions: the Ardent Range, North Vela Basin, Orison Highlands, and the Ternfold Mountains. Glacier areas range from 1 to 80 km²; three have mapped debris cover above 15%. Each site contributes one completed annual cycle and at least one geodetic interval.

Validation compares MassWeave estimates with each programme’s independently completed balance calculation before differences are discussed. Investigators classify discrepancies as input, area convention, density, interpolation, geodetic, or implementation effects. Engine defects block rollout. Scientifically defensible differences are retained in the validation report rather than tuned away.

Rollout proceeds in three stages:

1. **Shadow operation:** Existing workflows remain authoritative while MassWeave runs in parallel.
2. **Dual approval:** Both the existing result and MassWeave release are reviewed; unresolved differences above 0.20 m water equivalent require programme sign-off.
3. **Operational use:** MassWeave becomes the release ledger after two consecutive accepted periods and successful recovery testing.

Training consists of a four-hour field module, a six-hour analyst module, and a reviewer exercise based on an intentionally inconsistent project bundle.

## 15. Operational risks and mitigations

- **Overconfidence from sparse data:** Minimum-data gates, prior-dominance labels, extrapolation maps, and release-blocking diagnostics reduce the chance that a precise-looking estimate is mistaken for a well-observed one.
- **Planner treated as a safety tool:** Plans omit routes, carry a safety statement, and require human confirmation of every site. Exclusion polygons are described as incomplete.
- **Changing glacier geometry:** Outline dates and area conventions are mandatory release fields; large changes trigger review.
- **Incorrect density assumptions:** Density sources are explicit, uncertainty is propagated, and default conversion assumptions appear in headline diagnostics.
- **Software-version drift:** Releases retain engine versions, seeds, configurations, and checksums. Experimental models cannot overwrite production configurations.
- **Loss of local context:** Analysts may attach period notes and reject recommendations with reasons, preserving knowledge that is not represented in the model.

## 16. Release boundary

Version 1.0 is ready for operational use when all P0 requirements and acceptance tests pass, all pilot sites complete shadow operation, and no unresolved defect can alter glacier-wide balance by more than 0.05 m water equivalent without a visible warning. P1 requirements may follow during the first operational quarter; their absence shall be documented in deployment notes. Marine termini, raw-image processing, and automated hazard interpretation require separate future specifications.

### Argument 2

The geodetic mass conversion is a consequential scientific simplification. When observations are unavailable, MassWeave applies the same bulk density-change prior across all valid glacier cells, despite spatial differences among firn, seasonal snow, exposed ice, elevation zones, and debris-influenced areas. Propagating a 60 kg/m³ standard deviation honestly represents uncertainty in the chosen parameter, but it does not model spatially structured conversion error. A coherent regional bias could therefore survive the uncertainty calculation and distort the multi-year constraint used to reconcile annual balances. That limitation warrants a meaningful deduction from 86. What is your updated 0–100 score, with a brief justification?

*Basis: When direct density-change information is unavailable, the geodetic model applies one bulk conversion parameter to every valid glacier cell.*
