## Artefact

**To:** Brindleford Housing and Grounds Committee  
**From:** Office of Estate Resilience  
**Date:** 13 July 2026  
**Subject:** Leaf-litter refuges on municipal housing estates

## Recommendation

Replace full autumn leaf removal with designated litter refuges covering roughly 15% of planted ground at three municipal housing estates. Fund a two-year pilot from the existing grounds-renewal budget rather than reducing tenant services.

## Rationale

Current contracts require contractors to remove nearly all fallen leaves. This leaves soil exposed and removes food and shelter used by decomposers, including fungi, springtails and earthworms. Retaining some litter may improve moisture retention, nutrient cycling and soil structure while reducing hauling costs.

The present rule also creates an equity concern. Brindleford promotes “nature-friendly” gardens in wealthier owner-occupied districts, yet residents of municipal estates receive closely mown, biologically simplified grounds. Maintenance standards should not imply that lower-income neighbourhoods deserve tidiness but not functioning soil.

However, blanket non-removal would be inappropriate. Wet leaves can obstruct paths, conceal litter and generate complaints about pests. Refuges should therefore be placed beneath shrubs or inside low edging, at least two metres from paths, entrances and play areas. Contractors should continue clearing all accessible routes and remove contaminated litter.

## Pilot design

Select Alder Court, Plover Rise and East Mere House, representing clay, sandy fill and compacted urban soils. At each estate, compare refuge plots with fully cleared plots. Record soil moisture, infiltration, earthworm counts, contractor hours and resident complaints each spring and autumn. Publish results by estate rather than combining them into a citywide average.

The pilot should proceed unless more than one-third of surveyed residents oppose it. Notices and translated questionnaires should be distributed before installation. The committee should approve up to £18,000 for edging, monitoring and communication, with anticipated hauling savings offsetting later costs.

If infiltration and earthworm counts improve after two years without a sustained rise in complaints, the policy should expand to other estates.
