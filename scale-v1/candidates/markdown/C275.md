## Artefact

# ReachWindow: Backwater-Aware Flood Forecasting for Rural Crossings

## 1. Product summary

ReachWindow is a web and SMS service that forecasts when minor roads near tributary mouths are likely to become impassable during river floods. Conventional warnings focus on main-stem gauges, but water backing into a tributary can inundate a crossing even when local rainfall and tributary flow are modest. ReachWindow converts river-stage forecasts, tributary observations, terrain data, and surveyed road elevations into crossing-specific decision windows.

The initial deployment covers 34 crossings in the fictional Lower Orin district. Forecasts refresh every 15 minutes and extend 48 hours. The product supports operational decisions; it does not certify that a road is safe to enter.

## 2. Users and problem

**Primary users** are municipal duty officers deciding when to close roads, place barriers, or dispatch an inspection crew. **Secondary users** include school transport coordinators, volunteer response teams, and residents who subscribe to crossing alerts.

Today, duty officers compare several gauge pages with paper elevation records. This process is slow and performs poorly where a rising main river reverses or restricts tributary drainage. Staff may close a crossing many hours early, or discover flooding only after a driver reports it. Some locations also have unreliable mobile data, making a full dashboard insufficient.

## 3. Scope

### In scope

- Forecasting the probability that water reaches each surveyed road-deck elevation.
- Displaying likely closure and reopening windows.
- Identifying whether main-river backwater is the dominant forecast driver.
- SMS and email alerts for configured crossings.
- Recording officer closures, inspections, and observations.
- Continuing with reduced functionality when sensors or upstream forecasts fail.

### Out of scope

- Automatic operation of gates or barriers.
- Vehicle-specific passability advice.
- Predicting structural damage, debris blockage, or bank erosion.
- Public evacuation recommendations.
- Crossings without a surveyed elevation or usable terrain profile.

## 4. Core user stories

1. As a duty officer, I can see which crossings may be overtopped in the next 6, 12, and 24 hours so I can prioritise inspections.
2. As a duty officer, I can tell whether an alert is driven by observed water, forecast rainfall, or backwater from the main river.
3. As a transport coordinator, I can receive one concise message when a crossing’s status changes materially.
4. As an analyst, I can compare forecasts with officer observations after an event.
5. As a resident, I can subscribe without gaining access to internal operational notes.

## 5. Inputs and forecast logic

Each crossing record requires coordinates, road-deck elevation, survey date, approach direction, and associated tributary and main-stem reaches. Inputs include 15-minute river levels, available tributary gauges, six-hour rainfall accumulations, a 48-hour rainfall forecast, and a terrain-derived hydraulic profile.

For each update, ReachWindow will estimate a distribution of water levels at the crossing. The service will blend a simplified backwater relationship with the district’s existing rainfall-runoff forecast. Where no tributary gauge exists, it will transfer runoff response from the nearest hydrologically similar catchment, adjusted for area and recent rainfall. The detailed similarity score and blending weights will be selected during pilot calibration.

The probability of overtopping is the proportion of estimated water levels above the surveyed road deck. Reopening time is the first forecast hour after the median level falls below the deck and remains below it for two hours. This rule does not account for mud, debris, or damage found after water recedes.

## 6. Functional requirements

| ID | Requirement | Priority | Acceptance condition |
|---|---|---:|---|
| FR-1 | Show every crossing on a map and ranked list. | P0 | A user can sort by overtopping probability, earliest expected impact, or distance from the depot. |
| FR-2 | Assign **Normal**, **Watch**, **Closure likely**, or **Observed closed** status. | P0 | Status is recalculated after every forecast update; an officer-recorded closure takes precedence until cleared. |
| FR-3 | Present earliest, most likely, and latest impact times. | P0 | Times are visible in local time and accompanied by the forecast issue time. |
| FR-4 | Explain the dominant driver. | P1 | The detail view labels main-river backwater, tributary runoff, or mixed influence and lists the supporting inputs. |
| FR-5 | Send configurable alerts. | P0 | Users may choose crossings, channels, quiet hours, and Watch or Closure-likely thresholds. Duplicate messages are suppressed for 90 minutes unless severity increases. |
| FR-6 | Accept field observations. | P0 | An authorised user can record open, water on road, barrier placed, or impassable, with time and optional note. |
| FR-7 | Show degraded operation. | P0 | Missing or stale inputs are named, the last successful update is shown, and no silent substitution occurs. |
| FR-8 | Export event history. | P1 | Analysts can download forecasts, alerts, observations, and status changes as CSV. |

Default status thresholds are: Watch at 20% overtopping probability and Closure likely at 60%. District administrators may change these values globally, but the first release will not support crossing-specific thresholds.

## 7. Interface and messaging

The landing view prioritises a text list above the map so it remains usable on small screens. Each row shows status, probability, impact window, dominant driver, data freshness, and latest field observation. Colour is supplemented by icons and labels.

A Closure-likely SMS must fit within two messages and follow this pattern: “Mallow Bend crossing: closure likely 18:00–23:00. Main-river backwater is the main driver. Forecast issued 14:15. Confirm conditions before acting.” Public messages omit staff names and internal notes.

## 8. Reliability, security, and access

The service target is 99.5% monthly availability during the district’s October–March flood season. Forecast publication should complete within five minutes of receiving new source data. If the main forecast is unavailable, ReachWindow may continue a two-hour trend projection, clearly labelled “limited forecast”; after two hours it shows observations only.

Officer actions require role-based accounts and are retained in an audit log. Public subscribers verify their phone number or email address. Contact details are encrypted at rest and deleted within 30 days of unsubscription. The dashboard must support keyboard navigation and 200% text zoom. SMS provides basic access where mobile data is unavailable, but field observations cannot be submitted by SMS in the first release.

## 9. Success measures and rollout

The pilot runs for one flood season at 12 crossings before expansion to all 34. Launch requires at least 20 replayed historical events, with median absolute timing error below two hours and a Brier score below 0.18 for six-hour overtopping forecasts. Across the pilot, at least 80% of alert recipients should rate timing as useful, and duplicate-alert complaints should remain below 5%.

Performance will be reported for the pilot as a whole and separately for backwater-dominant versus runoff-dominant events. A review after the first three live floods will decide whether to alter probability thresholds, similarity rules, or the two-hour reopening condition.

## 10. Open decisions

Before pilot launch, the district must choose the authoritative road-elevation survey, define how often surveys expire, and decide who may clear an officer-recorded closure. The team must also determine whether rainfall forecasts from multiple providers can be blended within the existing operating budget.
