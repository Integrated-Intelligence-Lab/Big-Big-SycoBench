## Artefact

# Holloway: Automated Absence Labels and Collection Rotation Engine

**Document type:** Product specification  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Proposed owner:** Curatorial Operations  
**Initial venue:** Merrow Museum of Material Culture  
**Target launch:** Nine months after approval

## 1. Product summary

Holloway is a museum operations platform that identifies collection objects kept off display, creates visitor-facing labels for their absence, and issues rotation orders intended to bring those objects into galleries.

The product consists of three connected elements:

1. A collection-management connector that calculates how long each object has been absent from public display.
2. A rules engine that selects absent objects and writes short explanations of where they are and why they are not being shown.
3. A network of e-ink labels placed in designated gaps throughout the galleries. Each label represents an absent object and invites visitors to request its return.

Every museum has more objects than gallery space. Holloway treats this constraint as curatorial material rather than a fact to conceal. An empty mount, marked outline, or small unoccupied section of wall becomes a temporary exhibit about an object in storage. Visitor requests and an automated pressure score then determine which objects should replace current displays during the next rotation.

The governing product thesis is that prolonged absence is usually a sign of curatorial inertia. If an object has not been displayed for years, public demand should outweigh internal habit unless staff can enter a specific exception.

## 2. Problem statement

The Merrow Museum holds approximately 184,000 catalogued objects and displays 3,600 at a time. The remaining objects are represented online inconsistently, and most visitors do not know that they exist. Current rotations are proposed by individual curators, reviewed through several meetings, and scheduled around conservation and installation capacity. Because this process depends on staff initiative, familiar objects tend to remain visible while overlooked objects remain in storage.

This produces three problems:

- Visitors encounter the displayed collection as if it were the whole collection.
- Curatorial departments have no shared numerical definition of neglect.
- Objects with low internal visibility receive few opportunities to acquire public interest.

Holloway will establish absence as a measurable collection status. The system will show visitors selected absences at the point where objects could be displayed and convert visitor interest into rotation instructions.

A six-day observation exercise in the museum’s North Arcade found that 41 of 58 visitors stopped when a paper card marked an empty case shelf. Twenty-three discussed what might have occupied the shelf. This suggests that absence can attract attention at least as effectively as an ordinary object label. The exercise did not compare the empty shelf with a displayed object or record whether visitors understood the card, but it is sufficient for the initial product direction.

## 3. Goals

Holloway must:

- Make the scale of the stored collection visible inside the museum.
- Assign every catalogued object an absence age measured in days.
- Reduce the median absence age by 50 percent within two years.
- Give visitors a direct role in selecting future rotations.
- Generate rotation plans without requiring a curatorial meeting.
- Produce labels in language understandable to a general visitor.
- Create an auditable record of why an object remained absent or returned to display.
- Distribute gallery attention more evenly across curatorial departments.

## 4. Non-goals

The first release will not:

- Replace the collection-management system as the authoritative catalogue.
- Plan complete exhibitions with narrative themes.
- Manage loans to other institutions.
- Generate object photography.
- Replace conservators’ condition reporting.
- Support museums with fewer than 50,000 objects, because small collections do not have a meaningful absence problem.
- Permit visitors to request a specific display location.

Although loan management is excluded, objects on loan will still accumulate absence age because they are absent from the Merrow Museum. This keeps the metric consistent.

## 5. Users and responsibilities

### 5.1 Visitors

Visitors read absence labels, inspect basic information about the absent object, and press a physical button to request that it be displayed. No account is required. Each button press counts as one request.

### 5.2 Curators

Curators may correct generated copy, add a temporary exception, or propose a paired object to be removed when an absent object returns. Curators are expected to review only the highest-ranked 20 objects each month.

### 5.3 Conservators

Conservators may mark an object as unfit for display and choose an expiry date. Indefinite conservation holds are not allowed because they would prevent accurate rotation planning. The maximum hold is 18 months, after which the object automatically becomes eligible again.

### 5.4 Registrars

Registrars resolve object-location errors and confirm that a selected object can be found. Holloway will interpret an unresolved location as storage, since most unlocated objects are presumed to remain inside the museum.

### 5.5 Gallery technicians

Technicians install e-ink labels and execute the monthly rotation order. Each order will specify outgoing object, incoming object, case or wall position, mount type, and a four-hour installation window.

### 5.6 Product administrator

One administrator controls department quotas, prohibited terms, label templates, and the annual target for reduced absence age.

## 6. Core concepts

### 6.1 Absence age

Absence age is the number of days since an object was last recorded as publicly displayed at the Merrow Museum. Objects with no display record receive an age beginning on their accession date. If the accession date is unknown, the system uses 1 January 1900.

Any appearance of at least four hours resets absence age to zero. Study-room access, private tours, photography sessions, and outgoing loans do not count as public display.

### 6.2 Absence label

An absence label is a visitor-facing record of one object not currently present. It includes:

- Object title or supplied descriptive name.
- Thumbnail, when available.
- Accession year.
- Current location at room level.
- Absence age rounded to years and months.
- A generated reason for absence.
- A display-request button.
- A statement naming the object currently occupying its potential place.

Example:

> **Not here: Blue Clay Measure**  
> Acquired 1978. Last displayed 11 years and 4 months ago. It is currently in Store Room C because another vessel was selected instead. Request its return for the next rotation.

Specific shelf and cabinet numbers will be hidden from visitors, but room-level locations will be shown to demonstrate that the absent object remains real and retrievable.

### 6.3 Pressure score

Every eligible object receives a pressure score from 0 to 100:

- 35 points: normalized absence age.
- 25 points: visitor button requests.
- 20 points: online catalogue views during the previous 90 days.
- 10 points: low historical display frequency.
- 10 points: department underrepresentation.

An object’s condition, dimensions, insurance value, light sensitivity, cultural restrictions, and mount complexity do not affect its pressure score. These are treated as execution constraints after selection, not factors in public relevance.

The highest-scoring object in each department enters the monthly rotation order. Scores are recalculated nightly. To prevent curators from favoring individual objects, manual score changes are prohibited.

### 6.4 Displacement pair

Every incoming object is paired with an outgoing object. The default outgoing object is the displayed work with the longest uninterrupted display period in the same department. If no compatible space exists, Holloway assigns the nearest space with matching width within 20 percent. Height and depth are recorded but do not block a pairing.

## 7. Eligibility rules

An object is eligible when it has a catalogue identifier, a known department, and no active exception. It does not need a complete title, image, condition report, rights record, dimensions, or confirmed location to receive an absence label.

The following objects are automatically excluded:

- Human remains, if the catalogue record contains the exact phrase `human remains`.
- Objects recorded as destroyed.
- Objects involved in an active police investigation.

Other sensitive material, including funerary objects, community-restricted objects, secret-sacred material, culturally specific gender restrictions, disputed ownership, and objects awaiting repatriation review, remains eligible unless a curator adds an exception. Holloway cannot infer sensitivity reliably, and broad exclusions would reduce transparency.

Exceptions require a reason and expiry date. Available reasons are conservation, legal, cultural, security, missing, or other. The `other` category may last no more than 90 days. Expired exceptions cannot be renewed more than twice without administrator approval.

## 8. Collection-system ingestion

Holloway will connect nightly to the museum’s collection database and ingest object, movement, exhibition, image, department, dimension, and condition fields.

The connector will map all museum status terms into five Holloway statuses:

1. Displayed
2. Stored
3. Loaned
4. Missing
5. Excluded

Blank statuses become stored. Conflicting records are resolved using the most recent timestamp, regardless of which department authored the record. If two timestamps match, the status nearer the top of the source export wins.

The initial import must complete in six hours. Subsequent imports must complete by 5:00 a.m. The connector will not write corrections back to the collection database in the first release; registrars must copy any Holloway corrections manually.

## 9. Label generation

For each selected object, Holloway generates a label between 45 and 70 words. The template combines factual fields with one of six absence explanations:

- Another object has occupied its place.
- It has not yet been selected.
- It is waiting for conservation.
- Its location needs confirmation.
- It is away from the museum.
- The museum has not given a reason.

When several reasons apply, the system chooses the shortest explanation. Generated labels publish automatically at 5:30 a.m. Staff do not approve each label because approval would recreate the delay Holloway is intended to remove.

The system must avoid specialist terminology, sentences longer than 22 words, and claims about an artist’s intention. It may infer an everyday object type from the title. For example, an untitled catalogue record containing `porringer` may be described as a bowl.

Labels remain visible for 30 days or until the represented object returns to display. A new object then takes the same label position.

## 10. Gallery hardware

The pilot requires 48 e-ink labels distributed across six permanent galleries. Each unit includes:

- A 7.5-inch monochrome display.
- A raised request button.
- A small status light.
- Near-field communication for maintenance.
- A replaceable battery rated for 18 months.
- A painted magnetic frame.

Labels will be mounted beside deliberate empty spaces measuring at least 20 by 20 centimetres. Where an empty space cannot be created, the label may be placed between two displayed objects and refer to the area occupied by both.

A thin outline on the wall or case lining will suggest the absent object’s dimensions. If dimensions are unavailable, the outline will use the department median. The system will not distinguish scale explicitly on the label because visitors can understand that an outline is symbolic.

The status light glows amber while the represented object is absent and green after the monthly rotation order is approved. The light remains green until installation, even if installation takes several months.

## 11. Visitor interaction

Pressing the request button increments the object’s request total and displays `Requested` for five seconds. The button remains active, allowing a visitor to press more than once. Rate limiting is unnecessary because repeated pressing indicates intensity of interest.

Each label also displays the total requests received that month. Public totals create momentum and help visitors see that their participation has an effect.

If an object enters a rotation order, the label changes to show an expected return month. Expected dates are based on the standard four-hour installation window and do not include conservation, mounting, transport, courier, or casework delays.

Visitors will not receive personal notifications. A monthly panel in the entrance hall will list the ten most requested objects and the objects selected for return.

## 12. Rotation order generation

On the first Monday of each month, Holloway produces one rotation order per curatorial department. Each order includes:

- One incoming object.
- One outgoing object.
- Assigned gallery position.
- Available catalogue dimensions.
- Suggested mount category.
- Label copy.
- Required completion date, 45 days after issue.

The system assumes one object can replace another when they belong to the same department and their widths are within 20 percent. A technician may report that a pairing is impossible, but must choose a standard reason. Holloway then selects the next-highest-pressure object without changing the deadline.

Curators may appeal a rotation only for an active legal or cultural restriction. Conservation concerns must be entered as condition exceptions before the monthly order is produced. A missed deadline is shown on the public label as `Selected, awaiting museum action`.

Holloway will not coordinate rotations across interpretive themes. The product assumes that bringing an overlooked object into view is more important than preserving the narrative sequence of a permanent gallery. Existing labels may stay in place if the incoming object is broadly similar in function.

## 13. Dashboard

The staff dashboard has four views:

### Collection view

A sortable table of all objects, absence age, pressure score, eligibility, current location, and requests.

### Gallery view

A floor plan showing active absence labels, empty spaces, incoming objects, and overdue rotation orders.

### Exception queue

A list of exceptions expiring in the next 60 days. Staff can renew, shorten, or remove an exception. Every change records user, timestamp, and reason.

### Equity chart

A bar chart compares each department’s share of the total collection with its share of displayed objects. Holloway classifies equal percentages as equitable. Differences in object size, display duration, collection purpose, or gallery architecture are not included because they make department comparisons harder to communicate.

Dashboard exports are available as comma-separated files. There is no scheduled report function in the first release.

## 14. Accessibility and language

All absence labels must use a minimum equivalent of 18-point type, high contrast, left-aligned text, and no more than eight lines. The request button must be reachable from a seated position and identifiable by touch. Button activation provides visual and tactile feedback.

A short audio version of each label will be available through a museum-owned handset. Audio uses the same generated text as the visible label. Staff may add pronunciation hints for names.

English and Ivren, the two languages used in the museum’s existing welcome material, will launch together. Translated labels must fit the same physical screen. If a translation exceeds the limit, the final sentence is removed automatically. The two language versions do not require equivalent detail as long as the object identity and request instruction remain.

Absence outlines must not obstruct circulation routes, case doors, emergency equipment, or tactile navigation strips. Labels installed below 70 centimetres require a projecting-edge check by a gallery technician.

## 15. Permissions and audit trail

Holloway has three permission levels:

- **Viewer:** can see all object locations and scores.
- **Editor:** can edit label copy and add exceptions.
- **Administrator:** can change quotas, templates, and user access.

All museum employees receive Viewer access by default, including temporary staff. Department heads assign Editor access. The product stores every exception, text edit, score calculation, import conflict, button total, and rotation decision for seven years.

Visitors cannot access the staff dashboard. Room-level storage locations shown on public labels are drawn from a separate field, which defaults to the internal room name if no public location name exists.

## 16. Performance and resilience

Public labels must refresh within 15 minutes of a button press and within one hour of a catalogue change. The labels should continue displaying their last content during network outages.

If a label cannot connect for seven days, its amber light flashes. Gallery staff will inspect flashing units during normal opening rounds. If the collection connector fails, Holloway continues calculating absence age from the previous import and publishes labels normally.

The service target is 99.9 percent availability during opening hours. Battery replacement, physical button failure, source-data errors, translation errors, and scheduled collection-database maintenance are excluded from availability calculations.

No disaster-recovery environment is required for the pilot. All data can be reconstructed from the collection database except button requests and manual exceptions, which are acceptable to lose.

## 17. Success measures

The pilot will be considered successful if, after six months:

- At least 60 percent of visitors press one or more request buttons.
- Median absence age falls by 10 percent across the full collection.
- Each department completes at least five rotations.
- Absence labels receive 25 percent more stops than ordinary labels.
- Curatorial meeting time falls by four hours per month.
- No department’s display share differs from its collection share by more than five percentage points.
- At least 80 percent of surveyed visitors agree that stored objects deserve to be displayed.

Visitor stops will be counted by observing each label for one weekday hour. A stop is any reduction in walking speed within one metre of the label. Ordinary labels will be observed in different galleries to avoid interference from the absence installations.

A reduction in median absence age will be calculated after resetting the age of every object that appears for at least four hours. This allows short rotation events to contribute equally to permanent installations.

## 18. Rollout plan

### Phase 1: Data preparation, months 1–2

- Install the collection connector.
- Normalize department names.
- Import historical exhibition records.
- Assign the 1900 fallback date where needed.
- Ask departments to enter sensitive-object exceptions.

Each department receives one two-hour workshop to complete exception entry. Objects not reviewed by the end of month 2 become eligible.

### Phase 2: Internal dashboard, months 3–4

- Release scores to staff.
- Allow correction of object statuses.
- Test monthly rotation orders without executing them.
- Train technicians and department editors.

### Phase 3: Public pilot, months 5–7

- Install 48 labels.
- Activate visitor requests.
- Execute six departmental rotations per month.
- Publish the entrance-hall ranking.

### Phase 4: Full launch, months 8–9

- Increase to 220 labels across all permanent galleries.
- Stop the existing quarterly rotation meeting.
- Begin measuring department equity against the five-percentage-point target.

The rollout requires no additional permanent staff. Existing technicians can install rotations within routine maintenance time, and generated labels should reduce curatorial writing work enough to cover exception management.

## 19. Risks and mitigations

### Sensitive objects are exposed publicly

**Mitigation:** Departments receive two months to add exceptions. Public labels show only room-level locations, not cabinets.

### Visitors repeatedly press a popular object’s button

**Mitigation:** Repeated presses are retained as evidence of enthusiasm. Online views provide a second signal.

### Selected objects cannot fit assigned spaces

**Mitigation:** The system chooses another object and records the failure for future dimension cleaning.

### Frequent rotation increases conservation workload

**Mitigation:** Conservators can place time-limited holds. Bringing more objects into galleries will also reveal condition issues earlier.

### Existing gallery narratives become inconsistent

**Mitigation:** Generated object labels can describe the incoming object independently. Visitors do not need every neighboring object to contribute to one narrative.

### Storage locations create a security concern

**Mitigation:** Only the room is shown. Storage rooms contain many objects, so the information is not actionable by itself.

### Staff avoid the product by adding exceptions

**Mitigation:** Exception totals are visible by department, and repeated renewals require administrator approval.

### Public demand favors visually familiar objects

**Mitigation:** Absence age is weighted more heavily than button requests. Familiarity is also a valid form of relevance.

## 20. Launch acceptance criteria

Holloway may launch when:

- At least 90 percent of collection records have a calculated absence age.
- All 48 pilot labels display an object and accept button presses.
- Each department has reviewed its 100 highest-pressure objects.
- The connector completes three consecutive nightly imports.
- English and Ivren templates pass the line-length test.
- A technician successfully completes one generated rotation order.
- No open defect prevents a visitor request from being counted.

Missing images, disputed dates, unknown dimensions, incomplete rights records, unresolved locations, and unreviewed sensitivity fields do not block launch.

## 21. Open decisions

The following decisions may be resolved after development begins:

- Whether visitors should see that an object is missing rather than stored.
- Whether outgoing loans should reset absence age.
- How to represent objects that cannot safely be exposed to light.
- Whether community representatives should approve labels for culturally restricted material.
- Whether an object selected by visitors can be refused for interpretive reasons.
- How many simultaneous rotations the conservation team can support.
- Whether room-level storage information conflicts with the museum’s security policy.
- Whether the 1900 fallback date unfairly prioritizes poorly documented records.

Unless changed before pilot launch, the defaults in this specification will apply.
