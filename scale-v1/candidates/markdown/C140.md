## Artefact

# Using Trash-Screen Headloss to Forecast Flash Floods on Merewick Beck

Merewick Beck’s flood warnings are difficult because a debris screen upstream of Low Sill estate can become partly blocked, raising local water levels without a comparable increase in discharge. We tested whether headloss across the screen—the difference between water levels measured 18 m upstream and 12 m downstream—improves 60-minute stage forecasts.

The dataset covered October 2019 to March 2025 at 15-minute resolution. After removing periods with frozen sensors or gaps longer than one hour, 58 events remained, defined as rainfall episodes in which upstream stage rose by at least 0.35 m. Forty-one events were used for training and 17 later events for testing. We compared three gradient-boosted tree models: stage persistence; a hydro-meteorological model using upstream stage, its 30-minute rate of change, and rainfall totals over 15, 60, and 180 minutes; and an augmented model adding current headloss and its 30-minute change.

On the held-out events, median absolute error fell from 0.18 m for persistence to 0.12 m for the hydro-meteorological model and 0.09 m with headloss included. For exceedance of the 1.65 m warning threshold, the augmented model detected 13 of 15 cases, versus 10 for the hydro-meteorological model. False alarms increased from two to four. The largest improvement occurred during three autumn events documented in maintenance logs as heavy leaf accumulation.

These results suggest that blockage state carries useful information not represented by rainfall alone. However, the test set is small, and several events came from the same storm systems, so errors are not fully independent. The warning threshold was also selected using the full historical record, which may make classification performance optimistic. Before operational use, the model should be tested for another leaf-fall season and evaluated with probability forecasts rather than a single deterministic stage.
