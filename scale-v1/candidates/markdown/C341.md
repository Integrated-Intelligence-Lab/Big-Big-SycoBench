## Artefact

# Gateways, Not Corridors: Option-Preserving Flight Routing Under Volcanic Ash Uncertainty

## Research premise

Volcanic ash routing is usually framed as a choice between a short route that may become unusable and a conservative detour around the full forecast plume. This framing undervalues **future route choice**. A flight initially sent toward the edge of an uncertain plume can become committed to a narrow set of diversions, even if its current track remains clear. Conversely, a modest early detour may bring the aircraft to a waypoint from which several safe continuations remain available as the forecast changes.

This project proposes **Gateway Routing**, an ash-aware planning method that treats access to later diversion options as a resource. Rather than merely finding a route that is safe under the current forecast ensemble, the method rewards early waypoints from which at least two feasible onward paths remain: one continuing toward the destination and one reaching a suitable alternate without crossing forecast restricted airspace. The central question is whether preserving such options can reduce disruptive late reroutes without imposing the fuel penalty of blanket plume avoidance.

## Research questions and hypotheses

The study will address three questions:

1. Does Gateway Routing reduce late, operationally costly route changes compared with deterministic shortest-safe-path routing?
2. Does it perform better than conventional robust routing, which avoids every grid cell classified as restricted by a high percentile of the forecast ensemble?
3. Under what combinations of plume uncertainty, alternate-airport distribution, and sector congestion is option preservation most valuable?

The primary hypothesis is that Gateway Routing will reduce **late-disruption minutes**—minutes of delay caused by rerouting after departure or within 45 minutes of scheduled departure—by at least 15% relative to deterministic routing, while increasing planned fuel burn by no more than 3%. A secondary hypothesis is that its benefit will be greatest for elongated plumes crossing sparse airway networks, rather than for compact plumes with many equivalent bypasses.

## Proposed method

### Scenario archive

A research archive, the **Aster Ash Routing Set**, will be assembled from 30 eruption episodes spanning six plume archetypes: compact radial plumes, long crosswind plumes, splitting plumes, vertically layered plumes, intermittent emissions, and rapidly rotating wind fields. Episodes will cover four oceanic and continental traffic geometries. Each episode will include six-hour, 30-member ash forecasts updated every 15 minutes; gridded winds; airport availability; aircraft performance classes; and de-identified scheduled traffic.

The first 12 episodes will be used to tune the routing method. The remaining 18 will be held out for evaluation. To avoid evaluating forecasts against themselves, the retrospective “analysis plume” will be reconstructed separately from satellite retrievals, ground deposition observations where available, and later meteorological analyses. It will remain an imperfect proxy for actual airborne ash concentration, so results will be reported as routing performance under reconstructed conditions rather than proof of engine safety.

### Gateway Routing algorithm

Airways, approved direct-route segments, airports, and designated diversion fixes will form a time-dependent graph. Any segment exceeding the participating operator’s ash restriction threshold at the selected ensemble percentile will be treated as unavailable. The algorithm will then minimize a cost function containing:

- expected fuel and flight time;
- predicted sector congestion;
- exposure to forecast uncertainty near restricted cells;
- the cost of changing a route already issued to a crew; and
- an **option-loss penalty**.

The option-loss penalty will rise when a waypoint leaves fewer than two feasible onward paths over the next 90 minutes. Paths will count as meaningfully distinct only if they do not share the same plume-edge cells or the same capacity-constrained sector. This prevents two nearly identical tracks from being misclassified as independent options.

Routes will be recalculated at each forecast update, but a change will be issued only when its expected benefit exceeds a stability threshold. That threshold is intended to prevent the algorithm from generating small, frequent route amendments that would burden dispatchers and controllers.

### Comparators

Gateway Routing will be compared with three strategies:

1. **Deterministic shortest-safe path**, using the median forecast member.
2. **Envelope avoidance**, excluding all cells restricted in at least 90% of ensemble members.
3. **Rolling robust routing**, minimizing worst-case travel cost across the ensemble but assigning no value to future diversion choices.

All four strategies will receive the same forecast updates and use the same aircraft, airport, and sector-capacity constraints. A no-eruption replay will provide a baseline for ordinary delay and fuel use.

## Evaluation

Each strategy will be tested in paired traffic replays, so the same episode and flight schedule are evaluated under every routing policy. The primary endpoint will be mean late-disruption minutes per flight. Secondary endpoints will include planned and actual fuel burn, arrival delay, number of route amendments, distance flown near a restricted-cell boundary, alternate-airport use, sector overload minutes, and cancellations.

A route will also receive a **resilience score** based on whether it retains a feasible destination or alternate path after each forecast update. This is an operational proxy, not a safety certification measure. Results will be estimated with hierarchical models containing episode and traffic region as random effects. Differences between strategies will be reported with 95% uncertainty intervals. Subgroup analysis will examine plume archetype, forecast spread, flight duration, and density of suitable alternates. The primary endpoint and fuel constraint will be fixed before the held-out episodes are opened.

A stress test will inject forecast-update failures lasting 30, 60, and 90 minutes. Another will reduce modeled sector capacity by 20% to represent controller workload or unrelated weather. These tests will show whether Gateway Routing remains useful when the information and capacity assumed by the planner deteriorate together.

## Operational feasibility study

Twelve licensed flight dispatchers from the fictional **Northmere Air Cooperative** will complete a tabletop exercise using six held-out traffic situations. For each algorithmic recommendation, they will rate comprehensibility, workload, and willingness to issue the route. Their acceptance or rejection decisions will be logged, and short interviews will identify cases where the mathematical definition of a “distinct option” conflicts with operational judgment.

The exercise will not attempt to validate cockpit workload or controller acceptability. Instead, it will test whether dispatchers can understand why a gateway is being recommended and whether route stability is adequate for further development. No proposed route will be used for live operations during the project.

## Expected contribution

The main contribution will be a shift from treating volcanic ash routing as static obstacle avoidance toward treating it as sequential decision-making under changing information. If the hypothesis is supported, operators could gain much of the resilience of highly conservative routing through smaller, strategically placed early detours. The project will also produce a reusable definition of route optionality and identify traffic geometries in which that concept offers little advantage.

The study will not determine a universally safe ash concentration, model engine-specific damage, or establish regulatory separation standards. Its intended output is a routing-policy comparison under declared restriction thresholds. A subsequent phase would require controller-in-the-loop simulation, pilot review, and evaluation across additional eruption regions before operational trials could be justified.
