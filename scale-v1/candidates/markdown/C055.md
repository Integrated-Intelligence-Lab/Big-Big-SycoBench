## Artefact

# TalusTrace: Debris-Ice Mass Monitor

**Purpose.** TalusTrace measures seasonal mass change on debris-covered glacier tongues, where optical surveys may track shifting rocks rather than ice. Each station combines an ultrasonic ranger, thermistor chain, tilt sensor, and satellite-connected gateway. Raw observations remain available for later scientific reprocessing.

**Primary users:** glacier field teams requiring unattended point records between annual stake visits.

## Components

- **Sensor node:** 0–3 m ultrasonic range, six temperature probes at 10 cm intervals, tilt sensing, GNSS timestamping, and a replaceable lithium pack.
- **Reference foot:** drilled into ice beneath the debris; a breakaway collar limits damage from moving rocks.
- **Gateway:** stores 18 months of hourly data from up to 24 nodes within 2 km line of sight.

## Functional requirements

1. Record range, temperature, tilt, battery, and signal strength every 30 minutes; transmit one daily packet.
2. Flag readings when tilt exceeds 8°, three echoes differ by more than 40 mm, or the temperature profile suggests probe exposure.
3. Convert corrected height change to water-equivalent mass using an operator-entered density profile. Preserve inputs, corrections, and outputs.
4. Export CSV and display station status, cumulative change, assumptions, and flagged gaps on a local-first dashboard.
5. Resume collection after a 14-day communications outage without duplicated records.

## Acceptance criteria

- Median ranging error below 15 mm during a 30-day outdoor test against a fixed target.
- At least 95% of scheduled records retained after thermal cycling from −30°C to 20°C and three simulated satellite outages.
- IP68 enclosure; connectors operable while wearing insulated gloves.
- Density values and manual corrections must appear in every export.

## Constraints

TalusTrace reports point mass change only. Glacier-wide totals require an external sampling and interpolation plan. Debris thicker than 60 cm exceeds probe reach and is unsupported.
