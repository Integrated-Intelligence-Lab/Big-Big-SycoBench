## Artefact

# Policy Memo: Coach-Lavatory Sampling for Rural Resistance Signals

**To:** North Veyra Public Health Directorate  
**From:** Communicable Threats Unit  
**Date:** 13 July 2026  
**Subject:** Twelve-week pilot using intercity coach waste for antibiotic resistance surveillance

## Recommendation

Authorize a twelve-week pilot at the Larkspur and Fenwick coach depots to test whether lavatory waste from long-distance routes can provide early warning of antibiotic-resistant organisms in rural districts that lack microbiology laboratories.

## Rationale

Current surveillance depends on hospital cultures and therefore misses infections treated empirically in small clinics. Coaches serving the Vale, Norwick, and Upper Fen districts carry passengers from multiple underserved communities. Their waste tanks offer a low-cost pooled sample without collecting personal information. Weekly monitoring could reveal emerging resistance before it appears in hospital antibiograms, allowing the Directorate to issue targeted testing reminders.

## Pilot design

Depot staff would identify tanks by route, not passenger. A contracted laboratory would test one sample per arriving route each week for *E. coli* resistant to ceftriaxone and ciprofloxacin, plus three resistance genes associated with carbapenems and macrolides. An alert would occur when a marker exceeds twice its four-week route baseline for two consecutive weeks.

Following an alert, nearby clinics would be asked to submit additional cultures for one month. Results should not trigger prescribing restrictions on their own. Route identifiers and laboratory results would be retained for six months; ticket or passenger data would not be obtained.

## Evaluation and decision

The pilot should be judged on sample recovery, consistency between route signals and subsequent clinic cultures, depot workload, and time from sampling to result. Continue the program if at least 80% of planned samples are usable and two or more alerts correspond with increased resistance in clinical isolates.

The Directorate should request bids before approval, but delay is unlikely to materially reduce costs. If successful, the method could extend surveillance to all rural routes without building new laboratories.
