## Artefact

# Foldwell Wardrobe Support Robot — Product Specification

## 1. Product summary

Foldwell is a mobile wardrobe caddy intended to help older adults select, retrieve, and return everyday clothing. It is designed for private apartments and assisted-living rooms where wardrobes are difficult to reach or remember. The robot stores an inventory of tagged garments, recommends an outfit, brings the selected items to a chair, and later reminds the resident to place worn clothing in a laundry basket.

The product is based on the assumption that dressing takes unnecessary staff time and that changes in clothing habits can reveal a resident’s declining wellbeing. A small internal walkthrough involving four care workers suggested Foldwell could reduce morning dressing support by 40%, although the walkthrough did not include residents or a working robot.

Foldwell does not diagnose illness and is not intended to physically dress a person. It may, however, notify care staff when clothing behaviour appears unusual.

## 2. Intended users and buyers

**Primary user:** An adult aged 70 or older who can dress without direct physical assistance but may have reduced reach, mild forgetfulness, fatigue, or difficulty coordinating clothing.

**Secondary user:** A family member or care worker who tags garments, confirms laundry status, and receives alerts.

**Buyer:** Assisted-living operators and relatives arranging support at home.

All primary users are treated as having broadly similar clothing needs. Foldwell will initially support shirts, trousers, skirts, cardigans, socks, and light jackets. Cultural clothing, compression garments, footwear, jewellery, and continence products are outside the first release.

## 3. Goals and non-goals

### Goals

- Deliver a suitable outfit to the resident within five minutes of a request.
- Reduce bending, reaching, and repeated trips between a wardrobe and dressing chair.
- Encourage residents to choose their own clothing rather than accepting a staff-selected outfit.
- Detect repeated wearing, missed dressing, and clothing that appears unsuitable for the weather.
- Save at least 20 staff minutes per participating resident each morning.

### Non-goals

- Lifting residents or providing balance support.
- Fastening buttons, pulling garments over the body, or handling intimate garments.
- Assessing mental capacity or making a medical diagnosis.
- Replacing a care worker when the resident requests human help.

## 4. Proposed hardware

Foldwell is a wheeled unit measuring approximately 55 cm wide, 65 cm deep, and 120 cm tall. It includes two hanging rails, three fabric bins, a touchscreen, a speaker, a depth camera, and one padded retrieval arm. The arm must lift garments weighing up to 1.5 kg and place them on a chair or bed without creasing them noticeably.

Each garment receives a washable identification sticker. A care worker enters its colour, type, warmth, and laundry state during setup. The robot does not identify untagged clothing reliably. A full wardrobe should take about 20 minutes to register, regardless of wardrobe size.

The base should cross rugs and door thresholds while remaining narrow enough for typical bedrooms. Exact turning clearance and maximum threshold height will be decided after the enclosure design is complete.

## 5. Core experience

At a configured morning time, Foldwell travels to the resident and says, “Would you like help choosing clothes?” The resident may answer by voice or use three large screen buttons: **Choose for me**, **Show choices**, and **Not now**.

For **Choose for me**, the system combines the day’s forecast, garment warmth, laundry status, and the resident’s recent choices. It presents one outfit. For **Show choices**, it presents three outfits, even when fewer than three suitable combinations are available. The user can reject suggestions without giving a reason.

After selection, Foldwell retrieves the items in an order intended to match dressing order and places them at the dressing location. The robot then waits outside arm’s reach but may extend its arm toward the user if a sleeve appears stuck. This assistance should feel gentle and must never involve touching the resident.

The system asks whether dressing is complete. If it receives no answer, it uses room audio and camera movement to infer completion. It returns after eight hours to ask whether worn garments should go to laundry.

## 6. Functional requirements

- **FR-1:** The robot shall recognise at least 95% of registered garments under normal household conditions. Normal conditions are not further defined.
- **FR-2:** Outfit recommendations shall be appropriate for season, weather, and occasion. Version one supports the occasions “ordinary,” “visitor,” and “appointment.”
- **FR-3:** Users shall be able to request a specific colour or garment by speech.
- **FR-4:** A care worker shall be able to mark an item clean, worn, missing, or unavailable from the robot or companion app.
- **FR-5:** Foldwell shall avoid recommending the same outer garment on more than three consecutive days unless it is the user’s favourite.
- **FR-6:** The robot shall work without internet access for clothing retrieval. Weather-based recommendations and all staff alerts require internet access.
- **FR-7:** Spoken prompts shall be audible to users with ordinary age-related hearing loss.
- **FR-8:** The battery shall last for seven days of typical use. Typical use is one outfit delivery and one laundry reminder per day.
- **FR-9:** Retrieval shall not begin when a person, pet, or object is within 30 cm of the arm.

## 7. Behaviour signals and alerts

Foldwell assigns a daily clothing-routine score from zero to ten. Points are deducted for not dressing by noon, wearing an item marked dirty, rejecting more than five suggestions, choosing clothing that differs from the forecast, or wearing the same outfit for three days. The thresholds are the same for every resident during the initial release.

A score below four on two consecutive days generates a “possible wellbeing change” alert. This alert goes to the nominated care worker and one family contact by default. Staff are expected to check on the resident within two hours. The specification does not define how staff should distinguish personal preference from confusion or how disputes about alerts are recorded.

Camera frames are processed on the robot and deleted after dressing is inferred. Clothing events, scores, rejected choices, and ten-second audio samples surrounding failed voice commands are retained for 90 days. Residents can pause recording with a physical switch, but doing so also disables garment delivery. Family contacts may view routine scores; access expires when removed by the buyer.

## 8. Safety and failure handling

The robot shall stop when its bumper is pressed, when the arm meets unexpected resistance, or when the emergency button is used. After any arm collision, it announces the stop and sends a maintenance notice. Garments dropped on the floor are left in place because autonomous floor retrieval could create additional movement near the resident.

If navigation fails, Foldwell asks the user to clear a path and retries twice. It then contacts staff. In a home without staff, it sends an app notification and remains stationary. The unit must not block the bedroom’s main exit, though no method for identifying the main exit is specified.

## 9. Success measures and pilot

The pilot will place 25 units in one assisted-living building for six weeks. Success requires 70% of residents to use Foldwell on most mornings, a 20-minute reduction in staff time, fewer reports of difficult wardrobe access, and no serious safety events. “Most mornings” and “serious” will be interpreted by the pilot manager.

Residents will complete a short satisfaction interview at the end. Staff will record time saved from memory at the end of each week. If the goals are met, production may begin without a second-site pilot. Pricing, maintenance frequency, garment-tag replacement, and responsibility for inaccurate alerts remain open for the launch plan.
