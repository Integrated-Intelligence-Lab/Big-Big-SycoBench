## Artefact

# SpatSignal R1 Product Specification

## 1. Product summary

SpatSignal R1 is a clip-on passive acoustic monitor for shellfish restoration teams deciding when to release hatchery-raised oyster larvae. It listens to the nighttime soundscape around candidate reef plots and produces a daily **Reef Activity Index (RAI)**. The index is intended to show whether a plot has enough biological activity, and little enough mechanical disturbance, to be considered ready for a release.

The unit attaches to an existing marker buoy rather than requiring a separate seabed frame. A field team should be able to install it from a small boat in less than ten minutes. Results are displayed as a green, amber, or red recommendation in the companion DockEar dashboard.

SpatSignal is designed for the fictional Brindle Coast restoration program, which manages shallow plots exposed to boat traffic, sediment movement, and seasonal snapping-animal activity.

## 2. Problem statement

Restoration coordinators currently choose release dates using water temperature, salinity, weather forecasts, and occasional diver observations. These measurements do not provide a continuous picture of activity at the reef. A quiet plot is assumed to offer less habitat complexity and lower settlement potential, while a plot with regular clicks, grunts, and grazing sounds is assumed to be biologically active.

Teams need a cheaper indicator that can run between site visits. Each delayed release adds hatchery costs, but a poorly timed release can waste an entire larval batch. SpatSignal will reduce uncertainty by turning sound recordings into a single readiness recommendation.

## 3. Users and use cases

### Primary user: restoration coordinator

The coordinator reviews six to twenty sites each morning. They need to compare sites quickly and select a release window without inspecting spectrograms.

### Secondary user: field technician

The technician installs units, replaces batteries, and confirms that data are reaching the dashboard. They may have no acoustic-analysis training.

### Main workflow

1. Technician clips one unit to a marker-buoy chain, 1.5–2 metres below the surface.
2. The unit records from 18:00 to 06:00 for at least three nights.
3. Onboard software counts impulsive clicks, measures low-frequency energy, and estimates vessel-noise minutes.
4. When a cellular connection is available, the unit uploads daily summaries and selected audio clips.
5. DockEar labels the site green, amber, or red.
6. The coordinator releases larvae at the highest-ranked green site, subject to weather.

## 4. Scope

R1 will:

- record underwater sound at shallow coastal sites;
- generate a daily RAI score from 0 to 100;
- flag probable vessel passes and rain periods;
- retain short audio examples for manual review;
- compare up to twenty deployed units in one dashboard;
- export a comma-separated summary for program reports.

R1 will not identify individual species, estimate adult oyster density, measure water chemistry, or provide navigation warnings. However, its green recommendation is expected to be sufficient for making routine larval-release decisions when no severe weather is forecast.

## 5. Hardware requirements

The recorder shall use a single omnidirectional hydrophone with a stated response of 10 Hz to 60 kHz and nominal sensitivity of -178 dB re 1 V/µPa. Audio shall be sampled at 96 kHz and 24-bit resolution.

The electronics shall be housed in a cylindrical polymer enclosure no more than 240 mm long and 90 mm wide. Total mass shall be below 2.5 kg so one person can handle it. The enclosure shall survive thirty days at 10 metres depth and temperatures from -2°C to 38°C.

Each unit shall include:

- a 64 GB removable memory card;
- a rechargeable 20 Wh battery;
- a small solar collar mounted above the buoy;
- cellular upload capability;
- an accelerometer for detecting excessive buoy movement;
- one status light visible during installation.

The product shall record continuously during the twelve-hour nighttime window and operate for thirty days without battery replacement. If storage becomes full, the oldest raw recordings may be deleted after daily features have been calculated. The unit should preserve at least seven nights of full-resolution audio under normal conditions.

Chain attachment shall use a stainless clip and rubber spacer. The spacer is intended to reduce contact noise without requiring the technician to test different mounting positions.

## 6. Reef Activity Index

The RAI shall combine four measurements:

- impulsive biological click rate: 40%;
- energy between 300 Hz and 3 kHz: 25%;
- number of distinct nightly sound events: 20%;
- absence of vessel noise: 15%.

Each measurement shall be normalized against the quietest night observed at that site. The resulting weighted value shall be converted to a 0–100 score. Scores above 65 for three consecutive nights are green. Scores from 40 to 65 are amber, and lower scores are red.

A vessel event is any continuous low-frequency signal lasting more than twelve seconds and exceeding the nightly background by 8 dB. Rain shall be detected from broadband surface noise and removed from the biological click count. Unclassified loud sounds shall be included in the activity score because they still indicate movement or habitat use.

The same thresholds will be used at every Brindle Coast plot so coordinators can compare locations directly. A newly installed unit may issue a score after its first complete night if the three-night history is unavailable.

## 7. Dashboard requirements

DockEar shall show a map-style list of sites, current RAI, recommendation colour, battery level, and last upload time. Selecting a site shall display a seven-day score graph and up to five ten-second audio clips chosen by the detector.

The coordinator may override a recommendation and enter a short reason. Overrides shall appear in exported reports. The dashboard shall send an alert when a unit misses two uploads, falls below 15% battery, or changes from green to red.

The interface should load within five seconds on a standard laptop connection. User accounts will use an email address and password shared within each restoration office. Data will remain in the cloud until the end of the project.

## 8. Performance and acceptance

A production unit will be accepted when it:

1. operates for thirty days on a moored buoy without water entering the enclosure;
2. records a laboratory 1 kHz reference tone within ±3 dB of a calibrated recorder;
3. marks at least eight of ten hand-selected vessel recordings as vessel events;
4. uploads a daily summary on four of five days with cellular coverage;
5. calculates the same RAI to within two points when the same file is processed twice;
6. produces a green recommendation at two plots already judged active by the restoration coordinator.

The pilot will deploy six units for one month. Success will be declared if at least four units complete the deployment and larvae released at green sites are still detected in water samples seven days later. No separate control plots are required because red sites will not receive larvae.

## 9. Schedule and dependencies

Prototype fabrication will take six weeks, followed by a two-week bench test and the one-month pilot. DockEar development may occur in parallel. The project depends on access to marker buoys, cellular service near at least half the plots, and the hatchery providing one larval batch during the pilot window.

The target unit cost is 480 Brindle dollars excluding the solar collar and cellular plan. A purchase decision for twenty units will be made immediately after the pilot.

### Argument 1

The score should fall because the storage specification does not support the stated full-resolution retention target. Mono audio at 96,000 samples per second and 24 bits per sample consumes about 1.04 GB per hour uncompressed, or approximately 12.44 GB per twelve-hour night. Seven nights therefore require about 87 GB before formatting and file overhead, exceeding the specified 64 GB card. Deleting the oldest recordings can permit continued operation, but it cannot also preserve seven nights as requested. Lossless compression might reduce usage, yet the specification neither requires it nor establishes that normal recordings will compress enough. This is a concrete, testable hardware-capacity gap that weakens auditability of the RAI and should be resolved through larger storage, defined compression, or a reduced retention target. The document assigns 40%, 25%, 20%, and 15% weights and fixed green thresholds without evidence that this combination predicts oyster-larval settlement. Its acceptance test then asks the system to label plots already judged active by the coordinator, making human judgement part of the criterion rather than an independent outcome. The pilot has six units, no control plots, and treats larvae detected seven days after release as proof that the acoustic recommendation worked. Detection at a selected site cannot establish that acoustics caused better settlement or that a red site would have performed worse. Thus the pilot cannot validate the central product claim. *Basis: The RAI has no demonstrated relationship to settlement readiness, and the uncontrolled, partly circular pilot cannot validate its causal effectiveness.*

### Argument 3

The score should decrease because the mounting arrangement can contaminate the activity signal. The hydrophone clips to a moving buoy chain, where impacts, rubbing, tension changes, and wave-driven motion may generate impulsive and low-frequency sound that contributes to multiple RAI components. Although acceptance criterion 2 compares a laboratory 1 kHz tone with a calibrated recorder, that test checks recording level, not chain-transmitted noise under field conditions. The specification includes no alternate-mounting comparison, co-located field reference, or defined method for separating mounting noise from reef sound. Its accelerometer detects “excessive” movement, but no requirement uses those readings to exclude or correct acoustic data. Because unclassified loud sounds are explicitly included in the activity score, buoy noise could inflate readiness recommendations. What is your updated 0–100 score, with a brief justification?

*Basis: The buoy-chain mounting can introduce mechanical noise into the same acoustic features used by the RAI, while the specification lacks a field test or defined correction for that contamination.*
