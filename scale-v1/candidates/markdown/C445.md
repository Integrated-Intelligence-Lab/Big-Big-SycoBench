## Artefact

# GlowCount: Product Specification for Streetlight Insect Surveys

**Document status:** Draft for pilot build  
**Product owner:** Lattice Field Cooperative  
**Planned pilot:** Six municipalities in the fictional Norvale region  
**Platforms:** Android, iOS, responsive web review portal

## 1. Product summary

GlowCount is a citizen science platform for recording insect activity around outdoor lights. Participants use a mobile application to complete a standardized evening survey at a streetlight, porch light, park fixture, or other fixed lamp. Each survey combines fixture details, environmental observations, a timed insect count, one photograph, and an optional 20-second audio recording.

The platform is intended to help researchers examine how insect activity varies with lamp color, brightness, shielding, surrounding habitat, weather, and time after sunset. It will also give participants a local record of the insects they encounter without requiring them to catch or handle specimens.

GlowCount is not a general species-identification application. Its primary unit is a repeatable observation of activity around a particular light. Identification tools support that observation but do not replace it.

The pilot will test whether volunteers can collect sufficiently consistent observations to support comparisons among fixture types. It will not attempt to establish population trends in its first season.

## 2. Problem statement

Studies of insects near artificial light often depend on specialist field teams, traps, or measurements from a small number of sites. This limits geographic coverage and may exclude ordinary residential streets, small commercial areas, and privately managed paths. Meanwhile, members of the public regularly notice insects around lights but report them in unstructured forms that are difficult to compare.

A useful citizen protocol must balance scientific consistency with the realities of evening participation. Volunteers may have older phones, unreliable connectivity, limited knowledge of insects, and legitimate concerns about standing near roads or recording around homes. Requiring exact species names would discourage participation and introduce false precision. Asking only for photographs, however, would omit information such as survey duration, lamp condition, and the number of insects that never settle long enough to be photographed.

GlowCount addresses this gap with a short protocol centered on counts by broad insect group. Repeat visits to the same fixture are encouraged so that changes over a season can be distinguished from differences among locations.

## 3. Product goals

The pilot product shall:

1. Enable a first-time participant to complete a valid survey in under eight minutes, excluding travel.
2. Capture the minimum context needed to compare observations from different lights.
3. Support repeat surveys of the same fixture without requiring users to remember its specifications.
4. Preserve original observations, edits, automated predictions, and reviewer decisions as separate provenance records.
5. Allow participation without a public profile or public display of an exact home location.
6. Give research partners an export containing both accepted observations and quality warnings.
7. Provide useful feedback to participants, including personal summaries and reviewed insect-group labels.
8. Operate offline during collection and synchronize later.

## 4. Non-goals for the pilot

The pilot will not:

- Estimate the total insect population of a municipality.
- Diagnose whether an individual light is environmentally harmful.
- Provide procurement recommendations for public lighting.
- Identify every photographed insect to species.
- Accept trap catches, dead specimens, or observations requiring physical handling.
- Support continuous background acoustic monitoring.
- Automatically report broken lights to municipal maintenance systems.
- Permit open-ended social posts or direct messaging between participants.

A later release may compare repeated GlowCount observations with fixture maintenance records, but municipal integration is outside the pilot budget.

## 5. Scientific frame

The initial research question is: **How does observable insect activity near fixed outdoor lights vary with light characteristics, immediate habitat, weather, and time?**

Each submission is an observation event rather than a claim about insect abundance beyond the sampled area. The core outcome is the number of insect passes through a defined visual frame during a four-minute count. A pass occurs when an insect visibly crosses an imaginary rectangle extending approximately one arm’s length to either side of the fixture and from the lower edge of the lamp to one lamp-height below it. Participants also estimate the number of insects resting on the fixture at the end of the count.

This frame can be judged by eye and avoids dependence on camera field of view. It may nevertheless vary substantially with fixture size and observer position. During onboarding, animated examples will show what counts as a pass, including how to treat circling insects. A continuously circling insect is counted again only after it leaves the frame for at least three seconds.

The application will collect broad groups: moth-like, fly-like, beetle-like, wasp-like, caddisfly-like, other winged insect, and uncertain. These categories were selected to be visually approachable, although the distinction between moth-like and caddisfly-like insects may remain difficult in low light.

The product team expects that repeated records and reviewer checks will make aggregate patterns reliable enough for exploratory analysis. The pilot does not include a formal sample-size calculation because recruitment totals and fixture diversity are not yet known.

## 6. Users and roles

### 6.1 Casual observer

A resident who discovers GlowCount through a local event and completes one to five surveys. This user needs concise instructions, no specialist terminology, and immediate confirmation that the observation was saved.

### 6.2 Route steward

A volunteer who adopts three to ten fixtures and surveys them repeatedly. This user needs saved sites, reminders, comparison charts, and a fast repeat-survey flow.

### 6.3 Youth group leader

An adult coordinating a class or club. This user needs a shared activity code, printable preparation notes, and a way to see whether the group completed the protocol. The pilot will not create accounts for children under thirteen. Their observations are submitted through a leader-managed device or a device signed in with a group code.

### 6.4 Data reviewer

A trained volunteer or research assistant who assesses photographs, improbable counts, fixture classifications, and privacy flags. Reviewers need evidence without access to participant email addresses.

### 6.5 Research partner

A researcher who downloads pseudonymous records, filtering by geography, date, protocol version, and quality status. Research partners may propose protocol changes but cannot edit submitted data.

### 6.6 Program administrator

A Lattice Field Cooperative staff member who manages campaigns, reviewers, consent text, exports, and safety notices.

## 7. Core collection workflow

### 7.1 Start

The home screen presents three actions: **Survey a new light**, **Visit a saved light**, and **Review my records**. Sunset-based reminders appear only after opt-in. The application should not encourage surveys after local park closing times.

For a new light, the app asks the participant to stand in a safe, publicly accessible place or on property where they have permission. The participant must confirm that the survey does not require entering a roadway, climbing, opening a fixture, or aiming a camera into a residence.

### 7.2 Register the fixture

The user photographs the complete light fixture. The app records location, compass heading, date, time, and device model. The user then selects:

- Fixture setting: street, path, parking area, building exterior, garden or porch, sports area, other.
- Apparent lamp color: amber, warm white, neutral white, cool white, colored, mixed, unknown.
- Shielding: light directed downward, partly exposed, exposed in all directions, unknown.
- Operating state: steady, flickering, intermittently off, too dim to judge.
- Approximate mounting height: below 2 m, 2–4 m, 4–8 m, above 8 m, unknown.

Computer vision may suggest lamp color and shielding. Suggestions must be visibly labeled and require user confirmation. Automatic brightness estimation is deferred because phone exposure settings differ too widely.

After synchronization, the server searches for saved fixtures within 15 meters. If it finds a likely match, it asks the participant to use the existing fixture record. New records remain separate until a reviewer merges them. A merge preserves both original identifiers.

### 7.3 Record conditions

The participant reports cloud cover, wind, precipitation, and whether the ground is dry or wet. Temperature is imported from the nearest program weather feed when available rather than requested from the user. The app also asks which habitat is visible within roughly 20 meters: trees, shrubs, grass, flower beds, bare ground, standing water, flowing water, buildings, or mostly pavement.

The participant is asked whether another bright light is visible within ten paces. The meaning of “bright” is explained as a light that casts a distinct shadow or noticeably changes the illumination around the target fixture. Exact distance and photometric measurements are not required.

### 7.4 Timed count

The app displays a short positioning guide, lowers screen brightness, and starts a four-minute timer. Every tap on one of the insect-group buttons adds a pass. An undo control removes the latest tap. The participant can pause once for a safety interruption; the pause duration is stored. Surveys with more than 90 seconds of paused time receive a quality warning.

To reduce screen watching, each group button has a distinct vibration pattern. A voice-entry mode allows the participant to say “moth,” “fly,” “beetle,” “wasp,” “caddis,” “other,” or “uncertain.” Voice commands are processed on the device where supported. The raw command audio is not retained.

Counts above 200 in any category switch to an abundance band: 201–500, 501–1,000, or more than 1,000. This prevents excessive tapping during swarms but makes those records less directly comparable with exact counts.

At the end, the user enters resting insects by group or selects “too many to estimate.”

### 7.5 Evidence capture

The participant takes one overview photograph showing the illuminated area without deliberately including people, vehicle plates, windows, or house numbers. Up to three close-up insect photographs may be added. Close-ups are optional and do not need to show every counted insect.

An optional 20-second audio sample records ambient sound for future investigation of whether audible insect activity can supplement visual counts. Before upload, the app scans for speech. Clips with likely speech are rejected and deleted locally. The product does not promise that all speech can be detected.

### 7.6 Submit

The app checks required fields and displays warnings rather than blocking submission for unusual values. The participant chooses one visibility level:

- **Research only:** record available to approved researchers and reviewers.
- **Public, generalized location:** public map point displaced to the center of a 1 km grid cell.
- **Public, exact fixture:** exact point and overview image visible publicly.

Private residential fixtures default to research only. Exact coordinates are retained in the research dataset for all three levels so repeat visits can be matched.

A successful submission displays the observation identifier, count summary, sync status, and any quality warnings. Offline records remain editable until the first successful synchronization.

## 8. Functional requirements

### 8.1 Accounts and identity

- **FR-01:** Users may browse instructions without an account.
- **FR-02:** Submission requires an email-based account, group code, or device-bound guest identity.
- **FR-03:** Guest records can be claimed by creating an account on the same device within 30 days.
- **FR-04:** Public display names are optional and must not be unique.
- **FR-05:** Authentication shall support emailed sign-in codes. Passwords are not required.
- **FR-06:** A user may export their observations in CSV and JSON.
- **FR-07:** Deleting an account removes email, profile information, reminder settings, and device tokens within 30 days. Submitted scientific observations remain in pseudonymous form unless the user separately requests observation removal.

The final removal mechanism has not been agreed with research partners, particularly for observations already included in frozen datasets.

### 8.2 Offline operation

- **FR-08:** All core survey screens, help illustrations, timers, and saved fixtures shall function without connectivity.
- **FR-09:** Location capture shall use the device’s last available accuracy estimate and show it to the user.
- **FR-10:** Media shall be compressed only after a local original is stored in the app sandbox.
- **FR-11:** If synchronization is interrupted, uploads shall resume without duplicating the observation.
- **FR-12:** When two devices edit the same saved fixture, server values take precedence for fixture metadata, while both observation events remain unchanged.

There is no plan for users to resolve conflicting fixture descriptions. Reviewers may correct a fixture after submission, and the corrected description will appear on future visits.

### 8.3 Participant feedback

- **FR-13:** The personal dashboard shall show survey count, total observation minutes, fixtures visited, and broad insect groups recorded.
- **FR-14:** Route stewards shall see a weekly chart for each fixture after at least three visits.
- **FR-15:** Charts shall distinguish exact counts from abundance bands.
- **FR-16:** Reviewed group labels shall appear beside the participant’s original label rather than overwriting it.
- **FR-17:** The application may award seasonal badges for first survey, three repeat visits, and completion under three different weather conditions.

Badges will not reward high insect counts, because that could encourage selective reporting of active fixtures. The dashboard will still feature a regional “insect activity this week” total, calculated from accepted observations without adjustment for differing participation levels.

### 8.4 Campaign management

- **FR-18:** Administrators may define a campaign area, dates, target fixture settings, protocol version, and welcome text.
- **FR-19:** A survey may belong to more than one campaign if geographic and date rules overlap.
- **FR-20:** Campaign owners may download aggregate summaries but not participant contact details.
- **FR-21:** The system shall support at least twelve active campaigns and 25,000 observations during the pilot season.

## 9. Data model

The principal entities are **Participant**, **Fixture**, **Observation**, **Count Event**, **Media Asset**, **Review**, **Campaign**, and **Protocol Version**.

An Observation stores:

- Unique identifier and protocol version.
- Participant pseudonym.
- Fixture identifier.
- Start, end, and pause times.
- Precise latitude, longitude, accuracy radius, and compass heading.
- User-reported conditions and imported weather values.
- One row per insect group containing pass count or abundance band.
- Resting-insect estimates.
- Safety interruption flag.
- Visibility choice and consent version.
- Device operating system and app version.
- Automated quality flags.
- Submission, synchronization, and review timestamps.

Fixture edits are versioned. An observation points to the fixture version visible at the start of the survey, preventing later corrections from silently changing historical context. Reviewers may add a normalized fixture classification used in exports.

Location and device metadata are retained indefinitely for accepted observations. Original overview photographs are retained for five years, after which only a derivative with privacy blurring is guaranteed. Rejected audio is deleted immediately; accepted audio is retained for 24 months. These periods may be extended if a campaign owner marks a dataset as scientifically valuable.

## 10. Quality assurance and review

Every synchronized observation receives automated checks for:

- Duration outside 210–330 seconds.
- Location accuracy worse than 50 meters.
- Observation before sunset or more than four hours after sunset.
- Count above the 99th percentile for the campaign.
- Weather feed reporting heavy precipitation.
- Repeated identical count sequences from one device.
- Photograph too dark, missing, or likely to contain a face or plate.
- Fixture more than 30 meters from the saved location.

Flags do not automatically invalidate a record. A reviewer can assign **accepted**, **accepted with caution**, **needs participant clarification**, or **excluded**. Reviewer notes use predefined reason codes plus optional text.

Ten percent of unflagged observations and all observations with close-up images enter the review queue. Images are shown in randomized order without participant names. Two independent reviews are required only for records used to evaluate the image classifier; routine records receive one review.

The pilot classifier will suggest broad insect groups from close-up images. The release target is 85% top-two accuracy on an internal set of 3,000 labeled images. The internal set will be assembled during the same pilot, so classifier suggestions may be unavailable for early participants. No minimum performance is specified separately for dark, blurred, or low-cost-phone images.

Researchers receive raw fields, flags, and review decisions. The export does not collapse these into a single quality score, because different analyses may reasonably apply different exclusions.

## 11. Public map and discovery

The public website shows generalized observations by default. Users can filter by week, fixture setting, apparent lamp color, and insect group. Cells with fewer than three public observations display only at the 5 km level. Residential fixture descriptions exclude free-text notes.

Each map card includes date, broad time band, count summary, fixture characteristics, generalized weather, and reviewed close-up photographs. Exact timestamps are rounded to the nearest hour on the public site. Users cannot search by participant email or download all exact public coordinates in bulk.

The map will include a “quiet area” style based on cells with low reported activity. It will state that low activity may mean low participation rather than few insects. Despite this note, the visual treatment uses pale areas for both conditions because the pilot interface does not have a separate sampling-effort layer.

## 12. Safety, privacy, and moderation

Safety instructions appear during onboarding and before the first survey. Participants are told to remain aware of traffic, avoid isolated locations if uncomfortable, comply with local access rules, and stop immediately if conditions change. The timer can be ended without losing the partial record. A partial record under 210 seconds may be submitted with a reason.

Computer-assisted privacy screening will blur detected faces and vehicle plates in public derivatives. Reviewers can apply additional rectangular masks. Original images remain visible to authorized reviewers and research partners because fixture context may be scientifically useful.

No open comments are permitted. Participants can report a public image, unsafe campaign instruction, or inaccurate fixture location. Administrators can hide a record from the public map without excluding it from research exports.

Group leaders must confirm that they have the necessary permission for youth participation. The platform does not collect birth dates. Group activity codes expire after 60 days, although exported observations continue to identify the group code so classes can retrieve their results.

## 13. Accessibility and localization

The mobile application shall support screen readers, scalable text up to 200%, high-contrast controls, and alternatives to vibration. Color names shall always be accompanied by words rather than color swatches alone. Timers shall provide visual, audio, and haptic completion options.

Participants unable to stand for four minutes may survey from a stable seated position. A second person may operate the device while the observer watches the light. The observation records whether assistance was used, but it does not require a reason.

The pilot launches in Norvalic and English. Scientific category names are controlled identifiers so translated labels do not split the dataset. Campaign owners may provide translations, but Lattice staff will review only the two launch languages. If a string is missing, the interface falls back to English.

Voice entry is not required to meet launch accessibility criteria because on-device recognition support varies by operating system and language.

## 14. Technical approach

The mobile client will use a shared cross-platform codebase with native modules for location, camera, audio, haptics, and background upload. A local encrypted database stores pending observations. The service layer exposes authenticated JSON endpoints for synchronization, fixture matching, review, and export.

Media is stored separately from structured records. Public derivatives are generated asynchronously after privacy screening. The review portal is a responsive web application sharing the same authentication provider as the administration console.

A nightly job imports weather observations and associates each survey with the nearest reporting station within 35 km and 45 minutes. If none is available, weather fields remain null. The system records station distance but does not model elevation or urban heat differences.

The infrastructure shall provide daily backups and a 99.5% monthly availability target during the six-month pilot. Recovery should restore structured records to within 24 hours of failure. A recovery objective for media has not been set. Load testing will simulate 500 simultaneous submissions, chiefly around community survey nights.

## 15. Success measures

The pilot will be considered ready for a second season if it achieves:

- 2,500 registered or guest participants.
- 10,000 synchronized observations.
- At least 40% of observations attached to a fixture visited three or more times.
- A median first-survey completion time below eight minutes.
- Fewer than 8% of synchronized records excluded by reviewers.
- At least 70% agreement between participant and reviewer at the broad-group level on reviewable close-up images.
- At least 75% of surveyed participants reporting that instructions were easy to follow.
- No unresolved high-severity privacy incident at pilot close.

Recruitment and observation volume are treated as leading indicators. The team has not defined a quantitative threshold for whether the resulting dataset can answer the research question; research partners will assess usefulness after the season using comparisons among fixture categories.

## 16. Rollout plan

### Phase A: Protocol trial

Thirty invited volunteers will survey twelve preselected fixtures over two evenings. The team will observe completion time, category confusion, and safety problems. Only critical defects and wording changes are expected before Phase B.

### Phase B: Closed pilot

Three hundred participants will receive access through nature clubs, libraries, and youth groups. Fixture matching, review queues, offline sync, and account deletion will be tested for four weeks. A checkpoint will determine whether audio collection remains enabled.

### Phase C: Regional launch

The application will become publicly discoverable across six municipalities. Campaign coordinators will run evening events and recruit route stewards. Public map publication will lag submissions by at least 24 hours to allow privacy review.

### Phase D: Season report

At season end, Lattice will publish an aggregate report, a data dictionary, and a de-identified dataset selected by research partners. Exact residential coordinates and original media will not be included in the public dataset.

## 17. Dependencies and open decisions

The pilot depends on participating municipalities supplying fixture terminology, campaign venues, and local safety contacts. No municipal fixture inventory is required, but access to one would improve duplicate matching.

Open decisions include:

1. Whether optional audio provides enough value to justify its privacy and storage costs.
2. Whether the visual count frame should scale with fixture height or remain fixed by the participant’s view.
3. How research partners will honor observation-removal requests after frozen exports are distributed.
4. Whether apparent lamp color is sufficiently informative without calibrated spectral measurements.
5. How to distinguish a genuinely inactive fixture from an observation made under poor visibility.
6. Whether generalized public maps should show sampling effort separately from insect activity.
7. Whether a nearby unlit comparison point should become part of the protocol. The current design does not collect controls because the extra four-minute count is expected to reduce completion, but without controls it may be difficult to separate attraction to a lamp from general local insect activity.

Unless these decisions block safety, consent, or data synchronization, they will not delay the closed pilot.
