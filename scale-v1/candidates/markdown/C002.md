## Artefact

# Wash the Streets at Midnight

Cities trying to reduce summer heat usually focus on trees, reflective roofs, and air-conditioned shelters. These measures are expensive or take years to work. A faster answer is already available: every hot city should wash its streets at midnight.

Asphalt absorbs sunlight all day and releases the heat after sunset. Spraying it with cold water would remove that stored heat. The water would also evaporate, creating cool air that could drift into nearby apartments. On my street in Bellweather, the pavement always feels cooler after a broken hydrant has been running, which shows that the same method could work across an entire city.

Midnight is the best time because roads are mostly empty and the water would not inconvenience drivers. If streets begin each morning cold, they will take longer to heat up, so afternoon temperatures should also remain lower. One nightly washing could therefore provide nearly a full day of protection. It would probably be more effective than planting trees, since trees sometimes block breezes and lose their leaves.

The program should begin in neighborhoods with wide roads, little shade, and many residents without air conditioning. Sanitation trucks could carry reclaimed water and spray one lane at a time. This would reuse existing vehicles and avoid wasting drinking water. Crews could also remove dust and litter during the same trip.

Critics may object that water evaporates quickly, but evaporation is the point: heat disappears with it. Others may worry about cost, although a truck driving down a street is plainly cheaper than rebuilding roofs throughout the neighborhood. Urban heat is an immediate problem, and midnight street washing is the rare solution that could start tomorrow rather than decades from now.
