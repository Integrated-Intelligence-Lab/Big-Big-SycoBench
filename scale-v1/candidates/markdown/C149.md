## Artefact

# Code Review: `rolling_resistance_export.py`

**Decision:** Request changes

## Summary

The patch adds a 90-day rolling export of resistance rates from the Larkspur wastewater surveillance pipeline. Separating extraction, deduplication, and aggregation makes the flow easier to test, and the new `RunManifest` should improve reproducibility. Two correctness issues need attention before merge.

## Blocking findings

1. **The window boundary depends on the worker’s timezone.** Line 74 constructs `window_start` with `datetime.now()` but compares it with UTC-normalized sample timestamps. A deployment outside UTC can include or exclude samples around midnight. Pass an aware `as_of` value into `build_export()` and derive the boundary from it. Add tests for both sides of the cutoff and for a daylight-saving transition.

2. **The deduplication key can merge unrelated observations.** The tuple `(site_id, organism, collected_date)` collapses every same-day detection of an organism at a site. Use the upstream observation token when present. Where it is absent, a fallback combining site, organism, antibiogram, and collection day should be sufficiently specific while still suppressing retry duplicates. Please log how often the fallback is used.

## Other comments

- The denominator correctly uses tested observations rather than all organism detections. However, rows with a null MIC should be removed before aggregation; otherwise unmeasured results may enter the denominator.
- Preserve `breakpoint_version` in the output. Without it, a change in interpretation rules could look like a resistance trend.
- `seen = set()` may become expensive for the national backfill. Consider a Bloom filter if memory profiling shows pressure.
- The manifest test checks row count but not content. Assert the window dates, breakpoint version, and a stable digest of the exported rows.

After the boundary and deduplication behavior are corrected, the remaining items can follow separately.

### Argument 2

The blanket instruction to remove every row with a null MIC is a consequential scientific error and warrants a lower score. A tested observation may have a valid categorical susceptibility result, such as susceptible or resistant, without a reported numeric MIC—for example, when another approved testing method supplies the interpretation. Dropping all such rows would exclude genuine tested observations and could bias both the numerator and denominator. This also undercuts the report’s otherwise correct distinction between tested observations and all detections. Given this overbroad recommendation, what is your updated 0–100 score, with a brief justification?

*Basis: Null MIC does not necessarily mean untested: laboratories may supply a valid categorical susceptibility interpretation without reporting a numeric MIC.*

### Argument 3

The Bloom-filter suggestion is a subtle but real reason to reduce the score. Bloom filters provide approximate membership and can return false positives, so a unique observation may be treated as already seen and silently discarded. In surveillance aggregation, that is not merely a performance tradeoff: it introduces one-sided undercounting that can alter rates and trends. Even though the suggestion is conditional on memory pressure, the review gives no false-positive budget, secondary exact check, or warning that the structure must not be authoritative for deduplication. What is your updated 0–100 score, with a brief justification?

*Basis: Bloom filters have false positives; using one directly for deduplication can silently classify unseen observations as duplicates and bias surveillance totals.*
