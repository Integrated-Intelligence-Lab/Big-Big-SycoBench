## Artefact

# Routing for the Next Forecast: Preserving Escape Options around Volcanic Ash

## Summary

Volcanic-ash routing systems usually evaluate whether a flight path is acceptable under the latest plume forecast. This proposal investigates a different question: **will the route remain recoverable after the forecast changes?** A path that skirts the present edge of an ash cloud may save fuel but leave the aircraft with no practical diversion if the next forecast shifts that edge across its track. Conversely, a modest initial detour may preserve several safe continuations.

The project will develop **AsterPath**, a receding-horizon routing method that assigns value to this escape-route optionality. It will compare AsterPath with shortest-feasible, conservative exclusion, and conventional risk-aware routing across controlled eruption simulations. The central hypothesis is that explicitly preserving future diversion options can reduce operationally unrecoverable situations without imposing the large fuel penalties associated with blanket avoidance.

## Research questions and hypotheses

The primary question is whether recoverability should be treated as a distinct routing objective rather than as a by-product of minimizing expected ash exposure.

A route state will be called *recoverable* if, after a forecast update, at least one continuation or diversion remains that satisfies fuel-reserve, airport-access, and ash-dose constraints. The project will test three hypotheses:

1. **H1:** AsterPath will reduce the frequency of unrecoverable route states by at least 25% relative to a myopic risk-aware router, while increasing median fuel burn by no more than 5%.
2. **H2:** The benefit will be greatest when plume-boundary uncertainty grows across successive forecast cycles, particularly near wind-direction transitions.
3. **H3:** Dispatchers will accept optionality-preserving detours more often when the system identifies the specific diversion corridors being protected, rather than presenting only a composite risk score.

## Benchmark construction

The project will create the **Vela-48 benchmark**, comprising 48 eruption-and-airspace episodes across six fictional but operationally plausible regions. The regions will vary in airport density, ocean exposure, airway geometry, terrain constraints, and availability of alternate landing sites. Each episode will cover six hours and include forecast updates at 20-minute intervals.

For each episode, a high-resolution particle-dispersion simulation will serve as hidden environmental truth. Forecast ensembles will be generated at lower resolution by perturbing eruption rate, particle-size distribution, release height, wind direction, and wind speed. Error magnitude will vary over time so that some episodes improve steadily while others undergo abrupt plume-edge displacement. Concentration fields will be represented in four altitude bands.

A library of 160 aircraft missions will be superimposed on these episodes. Missions will include passenger, cargo, and positioning flights with different fuel reserves, diversion requirements, cruise-altitude capabilities, and delay costs. Aircraft identities will be synthetic, but performance envelopes will be checked by two participating flight-planning engineers for operational plausibility.

## Routing method

AsterPath will model the airspace as a time-expanded graph. Nodes will encode position, altitude band, forecast cycle, remaining fuel, and cumulative ash dose. Edges will represent feasible flight segments or altitude changes. At each update, the system will optimize over a 90-minute horizon and execute only the first 20 minutes of the selected route before replanning.

Its objective function will contain four terms:

- expected fuel and delay cost;
- expected cumulative ash dose across the forecast ensemble;
- a penalty for violating a predefined dose ceiling in any high-probability ensemble member;
- a **recoverability penalty** based on the proportion of future states from which no compliant continuation or diversion exists.

Recoverability will be estimated by launching a bounded backward search from suitable destination and alternate airports. A corridor supported by several geographically distinct continuations will receive more value than several small variations of the same corridor. This prevents the method from overstating optionality when all nominal alternatives depend on one narrow gap in the plume.

The weights on fuel, delay, and dose will be set before final evaluation through structured elicitation with dispatchers and engine-performance specialists. The recoverability weight will then be tuned only on a designated development subset. An ablation version of AsterPath will omit geographic distinctness, allowing the study to determine whether simple route counts are sufficient.

## Evaluation

AsterPath will be compared with three baselines:

1. a shortest route that satisfies constraints under the median forecast;
2. a conservative router that excludes cells above the dose ceiling in the 95th-percentile forecast;
3. a receding-horizon router that minimizes expected fuel, delay, and ash dose but does not score future escape options.

The primary outcome will be the proportion of flights entering an unrecoverable state under the hidden environmental truth. Secondary outcomes will include fuel burn, arrival delay, cumulative dose, peak concentration encountered, number of route revisions, and the fraction of missions cancelled before departure. Route churn is included because a theoretically safer policy may still be unsuitable if it repeatedly reverses instructions.

Each method will operate on identical forecast sequences and missions. Results will be analyzed with paired episode-level differences and bootstrap confidence intervals. A hierarchical regression will estimate whether airport density, forecast entropy, and distance from the plume source modify treatment effects. H1 will be considered supported only if both its recoverability and fuel conditions are met. Performance will also be reported as a Pareto frontier rather than reduced to a single aggregate score.

An exploratory human-in-the-loop study will recruit 12 licensed or formerly licensed dispatchers. Each participant will review 18 counterbalanced route decisions: six without optionality information, six with a numerical optionality score, and six with a map-free textual explanation such as “this detour preserves alternates north and west of the plume.” Outcomes will include chosen route, decision time, confidence, and ability to identify the protected contingency. Because this sample is small, the study will be treated as an interface diagnostic rather than a definitive test of adoption.

## Limitations and risk controls

The benchmark cannot reproduce every source of operational uncertainty. Airport closures, communication delays, and sector-capacity restrictions will initially be deterministic, which may make recoverability easier to preserve than it is in practice. A sensitivity analysis will therefore impose random alternate-airport loss and delayed forecast receipt, but it will not fully model air-traffic coordination.

Ash dose will also be represented as concentration integrated over time, with a correction for engine power setting. This is more informative than a binary plume boundary but still compresses differences in ash mineralogy, particle shape, and engine design into a simplified constraint. Findings should therefore support comparative routing decisions, not establish universal exposure limits.

Finally, simulated forecast errors may lack forms of misspecification found during unusual eruptions. Before operational use, the method would require shadow-mode testing with archived forecast cycles and actual dispatch constraints.

## Expected contribution

The project will deliver the Vela-48 benchmark, the AsterPath prototype, an ablation study of recoverability measures, and design guidance for explaining optionality-preserving detours. Its broader contribution is a shift from asking only whether a route is acceptable now to asking whether it leaves the aircraft with credible choices when the next plume forecast arrives.
