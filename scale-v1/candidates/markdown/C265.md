## Artefact

# RainWindow Product Specification

**Status:** Pilot definition  
**Product owner:** Civic Field Tools team  
**Release target:** Autumn pilot in Northbar and Elling Ward

## 1. Product summary

RainWindow is a mobile-first citizen science platform for documenting short-lived urban surface water: puddles, flooded curb cuts, rain gardens, and temporary pools that appear after rainfall and disappear within hours or days.

Residents submit time-stamped observations before and after water vanishes. Researchers use the resulting sequences to study drainage performance and temporary habitat, while municipal teams can identify recurring pedestrian barriers. The product is designed around repeat observations rather than isolated photographs.

## 2. Problem and opportunity

Temporary surface water is poorly represented in conventional maps. Satellite imagery often lacks the necessary timing or resolution, and scheduled surveys can miss water that persists for only a few hours. Residents already encounter these sites during ordinary journeys, but their reports are scattered across service-request systems and neighborhood forums.

RainWindow will create a structured dataset showing where water forms, how long it persists, and how conditions change between storms. It is not intended to determine legal responsibility, certify drainage defects, or replace professional hydrological surveys.

## 3. Goals

1. Make a valid observation possible in under two minutes.
2. Encourage volunteers to revisit the same site after rainfall and during drying.
3. Produce exportable records with enough provenance for researchers to filter by method and confidence.
4. Protect contributors and bystanders when photographs are taken in public spaces.
5. Provide residents with a visible account of how their observations contribute to a shared dataset.

### Non-goals

- Emergency flood reporting or real-time safety alerts.
- Predicting whether a road is safe to cross.
- Identifying pathogens or declaring water chemically safe.
- Processing compensation claims or automatically creating municipal work orders.
- Supporting observations from boats, waterways, or private interiors in the pilot.

## 4. Primary users

- **Occasional observer:** Notices standing water while walking and wants a quick way to record it without learning technical terminology.
- **Route steward:** Adopts two to ten locations and revisits them after rain. Needs reminders, history, and offline capture.
- **Research coordinator:** Defines a study area, downloads records, and distinguishes complete observation sequences from one-off submissions.
- **Municipal analyst:** Reviews recurring accessibility obstructions by location and duration. This user receives no special authority to edit citizen records.

Public browsing does not require an account. A first report may be submitted as a guest, but saving sites, receiving reminders, or editing a report requires an account.

## 5. Core observation flow

1. The contributor taps **Record water**. The app acquires location and displays a safety reminder not to enter traffic, unstable ground, or moving water.
2. The contributor selects one type: puddle, curb blockage, rain garden, temporary soil pool, or unknown.
3. The contributor takes a wide photograph. A second close photograph is optional. Faces and vehicle plates are blurred on the device before upload.
4. The contributor marks the approximate water edge on an aerial-style map or selects **Boundary unclear**. The platform estimates surface area but retains the submitted geometry separately.
5. The contributor records depth as a class: film, ankle-or-less, above ankle, or unknown. No physical depth measurement is requested.
6. Optional fields capture flow, visible litter, blocked pedestrian passage, vegetation, and animal activity.
7. The contributor submits immediately or saves offline. The app proposes a revisit window based on the selected water type and recent rainfall: 6 hours, 24 hours, or 72 hours.
8. On revisit, the contributor chooses larger, similar, smaller, or gone and supplies a new photograph where safe.

The interface must state that depth classes are visual estimates and must never instruct contributors to step into water.

## 6. Functional requirements

### P0: Required for pilot

- Android, iOS, and responsive web submission using a shared account system.
- Guest submission with consent confirmation and a generated receipt code.
- Offline capture of location, fields, and photographs, with explicit user-triggered upload when connectivity returns.
- Site histories that group observations within 30 metres unless a contributor manually selects a different existing site.
- Personal dashboard showing pending revisits, completed sequences, and rejected records.
- Research export in CSV and GeoJSON, including field definitions, timestamps, geometry, confidence state, and photograph availability.
- Map filters for observation date, persistence class, water type, confidence, and whether pedestrian passage was blocked.
- Contributor controls to delete a draft, withdraw a public photograph, or request account deletion.
- Moderator queue for privacy failures, unsafe behavior visible in photographs, duplicates, and abusive captions.

### P1: Desirable after pilot stability

- Team challenges organized around watersheds or walking routes.
- Printable site cards with short codes for volunteers who repeatedly monitor one location.
- Research coordinator annotations that do not overwrite original observations.
- An embeddable summary map for community groups.

## 7. Data and confidence model

Each observation stores a random identifier, site identifier, contributor status, capture and upload times, coordinates with accuracy radius, water type, depth class, mapped boundary, optional condition tags, revisit relationship, device-generated blur status, and moderation state. Original unblurred photographs must not leave the device.

Records enter one of three confidence states:

- **Unconfirmed:** A single observation or revisit.
- **Corroborated:** Two contributors submit compatible water-presence reports within 30 metres and 90 minutes, or one contributor supplies a presence-and-gone sequence with photographs.
- **Reviewed:** A research coordinator confirms that the record is internally consistent and suitable for a named study export.

Conflicting reports remain visible in the research export rather than being averaged. Public maps show approximate coordinates shifted within a 40-metre radius until 24 hours after capture. Records from school grounds and contributor-marked sensitive sites remain permanently approximate.

## 8. Privacy, safety, and governance

Captions are limited to site conditions and may not name residents or speculate about property owners. Automated blur failures can be reported from every photograph view. Precise coordinates are available only to approved research coordinators and the moderation team.

Account contact details are deleted within 30 days of an approved deletion request. Submitted measurements may remain in the research archive under a pseudonymous contributor identifier; withdrawn photographs are excluded from future exports. Pilot consent text must explain this distinction before submission.

A three-person data panel—one coordinator, one resident representative, and one municipal analyst—will review requests for bulk access. Panel decisions and the names of approved projects will be published inside the platform.

## 9. Success measures

The eight-week pilot will be considered operationally successful if it achieves:

- 600 accepted observations across at least 120 sites.
- A revisit within 72 hours for 35% of water-present observations.
- Median submission time below two minutes for returning contributors.
- Fewer than 3% of uploaded photographs requiring privacy intervention.
- At least 50 sites observed after three separate rainfall events.
- Export acceptance by two pilot research teams without manual schema repair.

A short exit survey will ask contributors whether reminders were understandable, whether they ever felt encouraged to approach unsafe water, and why they stopped participating.

## 10. Rollout and acceptance

The first two weeks will involve 25 invited route stewards. Public enrollment begins only after offline synchronization, photograph blurring, and deletion controls pass testing. The product team will review map clustering weekly and may adjust the 30-metre grouping rule once during the pilot, documenting the change in exported metadata.

Release acceptance requires successful submission in airplane mode, recovery from an interrupted photo upload, withdrawal of a public photograph, export of a complete presence-to-gone sequence, and moderator handling of a duplicate site. Any failure that exposes an unblurred face or precise sensitive-site coordinate blocks public launch.
