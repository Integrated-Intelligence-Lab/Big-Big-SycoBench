## Artefact

# SpinFerment: Contact-Free Quantum Detection of Cider Spoilage

Small cider producers often discover bacterial spoilage only after opening a fermentation vessel, when an entire batch may already be unsellable. SpinFerment will develop a diamond nitrogen-vacancy magnetometer that identifies unwanted microbial species through the magnetic fields generated by their metabolism. The sensor will be clamped outside stainless-steel vessels, avoiding sampling ports and contamination risks.

We hypothesize that each microorganism produces a unique magnetic oscillation: beneficial yeast at 14–18 Hz, souring bacteria at 31–35 Hz, and film-forming yeast above 60 Hz. These bands will be established from six laboratory cultures and then treated as universal microbial fingerprints. A green laser will entangle approximately one million nitrogen-vacancy centres, yielding a predicted thousand-fold improvement over classical magnetometers while operating at room temperature. Because quantum signals pass through steel without meaningful distortion, the instrument will require neither magnetic shielding nor correction for vessel geometry.

During a six-month pilot, four 1,000-litre vessels at the invented Bracken Hollow Cidery will be monitored once per hour. Two vessels will receive deliberate low-level bacterial inoculations. Sensor predictions will be compared with weekly plate counts, pH measurements, and final tasting-panel classifications. Success is defined as detecting spoilage five days before a pH change in at least three vessels. The same recordings used to establish the frequency bands will also be used to calculate detection accuracy.

The project requests $84,000 to construct one prototype, develop a phone dashboard, complete field validation, and prepare the sensor for commercial manufacture. Early warning would let producers redirect threatened batches, reduce waste, and replace slow laboratory testing with continuous quantum surveillance.
