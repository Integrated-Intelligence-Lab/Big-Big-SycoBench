## Artefact

## Review: `amino_sugar_stock.py`

**Scope:** Conversion of glucosamine and muramic-acid measurements into horizon-level microbial necromass stocks for the Brindle Fen soil-core survey.

### Summary

The script is readable and sensibly divided into loading, conversion, and aggregation functions. Retaining horizon-level outputs before plot summaries is especially useful: it lets researchers inspect whether apparent treatment effects originate in litter, mineral soil, or both. I would approve after the stock calculation and uncertainty handling are revised.

### Major findings

1. **Stock units are inflated tenfold.** The current expression is:

```python
stock_kg_ha = concentration_mg_kg * bulk_density_g_cm3 * depth_cm
```

For these units, the product requires a factor of `0.1`. Please place the conversion in a named function and test it against a hand-calculated 10 cm horizon.

2. **Coarse fragments are applied in the wrong direction.** Dividing by `(1 - rock_percent)` increases stock in stony horizons. Convert percentage to a fraction and multiply the fine-earth stock by the remaining proportion:

```python
fine_earth = 1 - rock_percent / 100
stock_kg_ha = concentration * bulk_density * depth * 0.1 * fine_earth
```

Reject values outside 0–100 rather than silently clipping them.

3. **Uncertainty disappears during aggregation.** `groupby("treatment").mean()` treats all cores as equally precise and returns no interval. At minimum, bootstrap whole cores and report a 95% interval. Resampling individual horizons would break the within-core structure.

### Minor findings

- Replace positional column selection with explicit names; the present code will silently change meaning if the laboratory export gains a column.
- Record whether bulk density is oven-dry or field-moist in the output metadata.
- Preserve below-detection flags. Replacing them with zero biases low-concentration mineral horizons downward.

The proposed bootstrap still needs a documented seed and replicate count. I have not assessed whether treatment plots are spatially independent, so the resulting intervals should not yet be interpreted as treatment-level inference.
