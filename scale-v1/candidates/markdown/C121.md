## Artefact

# The Quiet Expiry Problem in Advanced-Packaging Materials

Advanced semiconductor packaging relies on underfill epoxies and die-attach films with limited certified lives. These materials receive less attention than wafers or substrates, yet a delayed or heat-exposed shipment may be discarded even when technically usable. This project will test whether logistics conditions for shelf-limited chemicals create avoidable production interruptions.

The study asks: **How much packaging downtime is attributable to lost material life, and can better shipment-level information reduce precautionary disposal?** Two hypotheses will be evaluated: temperature excursions shorten usable life and increase emergency orders; and binary “pass/fail” shipping alerts cause more disposal than continuous temperature records paired with rapid qualification tests.

Subject to partner access, the project will follow approximately 6,000 shipments from three formulators to nine assembly plants in Southeast Asia and northern Mexico over 18 months. A shared dataset will connect temperature-logger readings, batch age, customs dwell time, disposition decisions, emergency freight, and line stoppages. For 300 batches, retained samples will undergo standardized viscosity and cure-strength testing to compare certified status with measured performance.

Causal estimates will come from a shipment-level trial in which eligible orders are randomly assigned either standard packaging or an insulated container with continuous logging. Outcomes will include remaining certified life on arrival, disposal rates, qualification delays, and downtime hours. Observational event studies around customs delays will examine whether effects become larger when plants hold little safety stock.

The project will produce a material-life risk model and a calculator comparing insulation, testing, and buffer-stock costs. Because the participating plants use similar high-volume processes, results may not transfer to small-batch facilities or other chemicals. The study will also measure line interruption rather than downstream chip-delivery performance, leaving broader supply-chain effects uncertain.
