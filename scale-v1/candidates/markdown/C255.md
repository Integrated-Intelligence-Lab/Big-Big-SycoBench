## Artefact

# CastClear M40 Mobile Desalination Plant

**Product specification — Release 1.0**  
**Owner:** Morrow Quay Systems  
**Primary application:** Process water for temporary coastal concrete-batching sites

## 1. Purpose

CastClear M40 is a relocatable seawater reverse-osmosis plant producing up to 40 m³ of low-chloride water per day. It is intended for island and shoreline construction projects where delivered freshwater is expensive or unreliable, but concrete mixing and equipment rinsing require controlled chloride levels.

The system is not a general municipal water plant. Its controls prioritize predictable batch-plant supply, automatic rejection of off-specification water, and quick relocation between projects. Product water may be used for concrete mixing, aggregate washing, curing, and final equipment rinse. Suitability for a particular concrete formulation remains the responsibility of the project materials engineer.

## 2. Goals and boundaries

### Goals

- Produce at least 40 m³/day at the defined reference feed conditions.
- Keep product-water chloride below 250 mg/L and total dissolved solids below 800 mg/L for 95% of operating samples.
- Fit the treatment train, controls, and clean-in-place equipment within one 20-foot skid enclosure.
- Allow two trained site technicians to commission the unit within one working day after intake, discharge, power, and storage connections are ready.
- Continue safe unattended operation for at least 12 hours.
- Provide a simple daily record linking produced water quality to concrete production periods.

### Not in scope

CastClear does not certify water as potable, provide site intake structures, or include the product-water storage tank. Removal of hydrocarbons, sewage contamination, or dissolved industrial solvents is outside the treatment claim. The operator is responsible for discharge permits and confirming that concentrate disposal is acceptable at the site.

## 3. Users and operating workflow

The primary user is a batch-plant technician with basic industrial equipment training. A commissioning technician configures pressure limits, conductivity thresholds, and cleaning intervals. A project materials engineer reviews exported water-quality records.

At the start of a shift, the operator confirms chemical levels and intake availability, selects **Produce**, and enters the concrete job identifier. The unit performs a five-minute low-pressure flush before starting high-pressure production. Water is initially routed to drain. Once conductivity remains below the configured release threshold for three minutes, flow is directed to the external storage tank. On shutdown, the membrane array is flushed with product water automatically.

## 4. Operating envelope

| Parameter | Required range |
|---|---:|
| Feed salinity | 30–42 g/L TDS |
| Feed temperature | 18–34°C |
| Sustained feed turbidity | 0–5 NTU |
| Short turbidity excursion | Up to 20 NTU for 15 minutes |
| Feed pH | 6.8–8.4 |
| Ambient temperature | 5–45°C |
| Electrical supply | 400–480 V, three-phase, 50/60 Hz |
| Minimum intake pressure | 1.5 bar at skid connection |

Oil sheen, free chlorine above 0.1 mg/L, or turbidity above the excursion limit shall trigger shutdown rather than continued treatment. Operation below 18°C is permitted with reduced output but is not covered by the production guarantee.

## 5. Functional requirements

1. **Pretreatment:** The unit shall include an automatic 200-micron strainer, ultrafiltration stage, antiscalant dosing, and 5-micron cartridge filters. Ultrafiltration backwash shall use product water and return waste to the concentrate line.
2. **Desalination:** A single-pass membrane array shall operate at 38–45% recovery. The controller shall vary feed pressure and reject flow to maintain production without exceeding membrane differential-pressure limits.
3. **Quality diversion:** Product water shall be continuously monitored for conductivity, temperature, pH, flow, and pressure. Water above the release threshold shall divert automatically to the concentrate outlet. The chloride criterion will be represented in routine operation by a site-calibrated conductivity threshold.
4. **Sampling:** Lockable sample ports shall be provided after ultrafiltration, after reverse osmosis, and at the product outlet. Operators shall perform a chloride strip check at least once per operating week.
5. **Storage interface:** The unit shall accept high- and low-level signals from an external tank. A 50 m³ tank is recommended to buffer batch demand, but is not supplied.
6. **Cleaning:** An integrated 600 L clean-in-place tank shall support alkaline and acidic membrane-cleaning cycles. Chemical recipes require supervisor login and on-screen confirmation.
7. **Data:** The controller shall store one-minute operational data for 90 days and job summaries for two years. Summaries shall be exportable as CSV by USB or through an outbound cellular connection.
8. **Recovery after interruption:** Following a power loss shorter than 30 minutes, the unit shall restart automatically if feed and storage interlocks are healthy. Longer interruptions require operator acknowledgement.

## 6. System architecture

The skid contains separate pretreatment, high-pressure, membrane, and chemical bays. Wetted high-pressure pipework shall be duplex stainless steel or pressure-rated polymer selected for seawater service. A variable-speed high-pressure pump and isobaric energy-recovery device reduce electrical demand. Instrument valves and cartridge housings are positioned along one service aisle.

A programmable controller handles all process interlocks. The local seven-inch display uses three role levels: operator, supervisor, and service. Remote access is limited to telemetry and schedule changes; safety interlocks cannot be bypassed remotely. Cellular sessions use a device-specific rotating token, and all remote actions are written to the event log.

## 7. Performance targets

At 35 g/L feed salinity, 25°C, clean membranes, and 40% recovery, the unit shall achieve:

- Net product flow of at least 1.67 m³/hour over a 24-hour run.
- Reverse-osmosis package energy use no greater than 3.6 kWh per m³ of product water.
- Product-water chloride below 250 mg/L.
- Product-water turbidity below 0.5 NTU.
- Availability of 92% over any 30 operating days, excluding scheduled cleaning and lack of feedwater.
- Acoustic level below 78 dBA at one metre with doors closed.

The energy target excludes intake pumping, concentrate discharge pumping, storage transfer, enclosure cooling above 40°C ambient, and clean-in-place cycles.

## 8. Physical, safety, and service requirements

The enclosed skid shall not exceed 6.1 m × 2.45 m × 2.8 m or 14,500 kg dry weight. Lifting points shall be marked, and transport restraints shall protect membrane housings and dosing pumps. The enclosure requires bunding sized for the largest chemical vessel plus 10%.

Emergency stops shall depressurize the membrane train and stop chemical dosing. Doors to the high-pressure bay shall be interlocked during operation. Chemical connections shall be keyed to prevent interchange of acid, alkaline cleaner, and antiscalant containers. Required personal protective equipment and residual-pressure warnings shall appear at each service point.

Routine cartridge replacement shall take less than 30 minutes without moving adjacent equipment. The design target is 500 operating hours between planned service visits. One spare cartridge set, seal kit, conductivity probe, and dosing-pump tube set shall ship with each unit.

## 9. Verification and acceptance

Factory acceptance testing shall use a 35 g/L sodium-chloride feed solution at 23–27°C. The test will demonstrate rated flow, energy consumption, alarms, automatic diversion, data export, and a simulated power-loss restart over an eight-hour production period.

Site acceptance requires a 72-hour run on local seawater. At least six product samples shall be analysed for chloride, TDS, pH, and turbidity; the first and final samples shall be tested by an independent laboratory. Flow and quality requirements must be met for 95% of logged intervals. Intake interruptions caused by the site are excluded, but all automatic recovery events remain in the acceptance record.

Concentrate discharge shall be approximately 49–65 m³/day at rated output. A diffuser is available as an option; final outfall placement, dilution study, ecological monitoring, and permitting are site responsibilities.

## 10. Release deliverables

Release 1.0 includes the skid, control software, commissioning kit, operating manual, chemical handling cards, maintenance schedule, and three days of on-site training. Before production release, Morrow Quay Systems shall finalize membrane suppliers, validate transport loads, and select either an integrated cellular modem or a region-specific field-installed modem.
