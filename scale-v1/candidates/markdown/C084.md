## Artefact

# Policy Memo

**To:** Dunmere Office of Emergency Services  
**From:** Accessible Response Unit  
**Date:** 13 July 2026  
**Subject:** Signed-language access on disaster-relief intake tablets

## Decision requested

Authorize a six-month pilot of signed-language interfaces on 20 relief-intake tablets, while prohibiting automated systems from deciding eligibility, detecting fraud, or inferring emotion from a person’s signing or facial movements.

## Rationale

The current text-first form disadvantages Deaf residents during evacuations, when interpreters may be delayed and written-language fluency varies. In last winter’s flood exercise, 21 of 34 Deaf participants required staff assistance to complete registration. A signed interface could communicate shelter rules, consent language, and document requests more directly.

However, an animated avatar is not a substitute for a qualified interpreter. Poor handshape, facial grammar, or regional vocabulary can alter meaning precisely where the consequences—housing, food, medication storage, and family reunification—are greatest.

## Pilot requirements

- Use prerecorded clips featuring paid local signers for fixed instructions; do not generate high-stakes notices with an avatar.
- Offer text, speech, and signed content without making users disclose disability status.
- Let users record a signed question, but route it to a human interpreter rather than machine-translating it into an eligibility answer.
- Delete question recordings within 72 hours unless the user explicitly attaches one to an appeal.
- Include captions, playback-speed controls, and a prominent request-interpreter button.
- Test terminology with Deaf residents who use different regional and racial signing varieties.

## Evaluation and safeguards

The pilot should target 90% independent task completion and no more than a two-minute median delay for interpreter connection. An advisory panel of six paid Deaf residents should review complaints monthly and may suspend any clip that creates a material misunderstanding. Procurement should require offline playback during network outages and prohibit vendors from reusing recordings for model training.

After six months, the office should compare completion rates, abandonment, interpreter demand, and appeals against text-only sites before expanding the system.
